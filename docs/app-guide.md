# Watchtower — User Guide

Watchtower is a macOS desktop app that syncs your Slack workspace to a local database and uses AI to generate insights: daily briefings, an inbox action strip (proposals and reminders awaiting your decision), calendar integration with meeting prep, digests, tracks, and people analytics.

## First launch: setup

Setup is three steps and asks for nothing you must have: **Goals → Connect → About you**, then you are in the app. There is no AI interview and no "all done" screen.

**Goals** — tick what you want Watchtower for: **Work communication** (Catch-Up after time away, what is on fire every morning), **Tasks & Jira** (Jira issues and chat follow-ups in one list with a next step for each), **Meetings** (context before, transcript and notes after) and **Development in Workbench** (Claude Code works your folder's board; no connections needed). All but Meetings start ticked; unticking everything is fine and means Development only. Each goal turns on its features; **Customize features →** shows them as switches (Knowledge search and Attention detection are always on — you can still turn either off in Settings → Features; Memory comes with Work communication) and, once you flip one, your pick wins over the goals until **Reset**. The **Assistant language** row under the goals shows the language Watchtower writes Catch-Up, briefings, tracks and digests in — your Mac's first language to start with ("from macOS") — and clicking it opens the picker to choose another. Watchtower also checks that Claude Code or Codex CLI is installed and signed in; until it is, a box explains how to install it (with **Check again**, **Set the path manually** and **No npm?**) and Continue stays off.

**Connect** — only the sources your goals need: Slack, one Google card (mail, and calendar if you picked Meetings) and Jira. **Connect** opens the same sheet as in Settings; a connected source shows a check with its name. Everything is optional ("You can continue without any") and everything stays on your Mac. After you connect Slack, Watchtower loads its people list in the background ("Loading people… N") for the next step. Picking Development alone skips this step.

**About you** — shown only with Slack connected: your role and team, your manager, your reports and your peers, picked from your Slack workspace's people (search by name or @handle; not you, not bots). It decides whose messages and tasks come first. **Done** saves it, **Later** skips it; you can change it any time in Settings → Profile.

**Right after setup** — Watchtower starts its background sync once and opens Catch-Up (if you picked Work communication and its tab shows — see the sidebar rules below), else Workbench (Development), else AI Chat. While that first sync runs, Catch-Up says "Syncing Slack for the last N days — usually 3–5 min" with the sync's progress.

**Run setup again** — Settings → Profile → **Run Setup Again** goes through the same three steps, starting from your current goals (worked out from the features that are on; "Features customized" if you changed them by hand), your current language and your current team. **Cancel** (top right) goes back without changing anything more. If nothing changed, the background sync is left as it is and you return to the tab you were on.

## Tabs & Features

### Menu

The app menu on the left lists the tabs: Targets, Tracks, Workbench and Calendar on top, then the FOCUS (Catch Up, Briefings, Day Plan, Inbox, Ideas), EXECUTION (Project Map, Releases, Blockers, Workload) and INSIGHTS (Digests, People, Memory, Statistics) sections (each collapsible, remembered across launches), AI Chat, and the TOOLS (Search, Boards, Usage, MCP Server) at the bottom, with the next meeting, the Jira indicator and Update Available below them. Right-click a tab in a section to hide it into that section's Hidden list.

**⌘B** (or the sidebar button at the top of the menu) folds the menu into a narrow icon rail and back. Watchtower remembers whether the menu is full or folded, and the same for these side panels: the chat history, the chat's Artifacts/Sources column width, an artifact's comment list, the Workbench side panel and FILES section, and the Files pane's Usages/Questions inspector with its tab and width — after a restart or an update they come back as you left them (other split panes, such as Catch Up's, Ideas' or Memory's, reopen at their default width). The rail keeps the same order: the tabs as icons, and each section as one group icon — click it to open or fold that section's tabs right in the rail. Sections open and fold independently, and the fold is the same one the menu has: fold a section in the rail and it is folded in the menu too (and the other way round), and Watchtower remembers it across restarts. A section opens by itself only when you go to one of its tabs while it is folded (from a notification, a link or a shortcut — also while the window is closed: the section is open when the window comes back) — for that run only: after a restart it is folded again, as you left it; a restart or reopening the window on the same tab keeps your folds, and a folded section holding the tab you are on is tinted in the accent colour. Badges become small coloured dots (a closed group shows a dot when any of its tabs has one); hover an icon for its name and count. Hidden and turned-off tabs stay out of the rail. A tab also stays out of the menu until it has something to show: its feature must be on (Settings → Features) **and** its source connected — Calendar needs a calendar, Boards, Workload, Blockers, Project Map and Releases need Jira, Statistics needs Slack or mail, and Inbox and Catch Up show with any source (Slack, mail, Jira or a calendar). Connecting an account in Settings shows its tabs right away. Until Slack, mail and Jira are all connected, a quiet **+ Connect …** line at the bottom of the menu names what is missing and opens Settings → Connections; its × hides it for good. Catch Up also needs Attention detection or one of the digest features (Slack Digests, Stream Digests) on; Inbox has no feature switch. At the bottom, the next meeting is a calendar icon with its start time — click it for the full card with Join; Jira, Update Available and running background tasks show as small icons.

### Catch Up
A recap of a window you were away for — what happened, what was decided, and what needs you — written from your synced sources.

**Left column:** the window bar — **Auto** (since the last recap you marked caught up; the last 24 hours when there is none, and at most 31 days), **Today**, **Yesterday**, **3 days** or **Week**, or tick **Range** to pick your own from/to (neither may be in the future) — and **Build recap**. Below it, the history of recaps; click one to read it. A failed build shows as a banner over the list and never hides the older recaps.

**Recap (right):** the window and what it covered, a TL;DR, then **What happened** (topic cards, each with its sources, which expand in place, and 👍/👎 with an optional comment), **Decisions**, **Meetings** and **For you** (mentions, DMs, mail, tracks and tasks due that need you). A window with nothing in it says "Quiet — nothing happened in this window."

**Action bar:** type a **Correction…** and press **Regenerate** to rebuild the same window with it; **I'm caught up** marks everything in the window read — digests, stream digests, tracks, attention items and briefings — and the next Auto recap starts from there. CLI: `watchtower catchup run|ack|list|show|feedback`.

### Briefings
Personalized daily briefings generated by AI. Each briefing consolidates data from all other pipelines (digests, tracks, people cards) into a single morning overview tailored to your role.

A briefing contains five sections:

**Needs Attention** — Items requiring your immediate focus: unanswered mentions/DMs/replies/mail addressed to you, updated tracks, overdue/blocked tasks, new red flags from people cards. Each item shows priority, reason, and links to its source (track, digest, task, or person). Items may include `suggest_task` — a prompt to create a task from a track that needs action. Click to navigate.

**Your Day** — Active tasks and tracks for the day. Shows priority, current status, and ownership. Tasks with due dates or high priority appear first. If no tasks or tracks exist yet, a hint suggests creating tasks from tracks or briefing recommendations. Click to navigate to the task or track.

**What Happened** — Notable events from recent channel digests: decisions, key discussions, important topics. Shows channel name, item type, and importance. Click to open the digest.

**Team Pulse** — People signals: activity spikes or drops, new red flags, highlights, conflicts. Click to navigate to the person's card.

**Coaching Corner** — Communication and process recommendations based on your interactions. Categories include communication, delegation, conflict, and process tips.

**Workbenches** — when a workbench had activity since the previous briefing (a blocked target, open asks waiting for your answer, unread agent comments), the briefing can bring it into Needs Attention, labeled *Workbench*. Workbench activity alone is enough for a briefing to be generated.

The list panel shows briefings by date with unread indicator (blue dot), attention count, and task count. Selecting a briefing marks it as read. Briefings are generated once per day after the configured hour (default: 8:00 AM, configurable in Settings via `briefing.hour`).

With no briefings yet, the empty list offers **Generate Briefing**. If the run fails, the reason appears in red under the button instead of the click silently doing nothing. A run keeps going if you switch to another tab: coming back shows it still in progress (the button stays disabled, so a second click cannot start a parallel run) or shows its result or error.

**No connected account:** a briefing is written for *you*, so Watchtower first needs to know who you are — it takes that from your Slack account #1, else your first Google account, else the first enabled Jira site whose owner identity is known. With none of them connected, the empty list instead says "Connect Slack, Google or Jira so Watchtower knows who you are", and its **Open Connections** button opens Settings on the Connections tab. There is no Generate button until an account is connected. A freshly connected Jira site can show this same empty state briefly, until Watchtower reads your identity from Jira in the background.

### Day Plan
Today's schedule: a **Timeline** of time blocks and calendar events, and a **Backlog** of things to fit in if time permits (**Add** creates your own item). The header shows the date, how many items are done, and a **Briefing** button when that day has a briefing. The footer has **Generate today's plan** while there is no plan yet, then **Regenerate with feedback…** and **Reset plan**. A failed generation shows its reason in a red banner at the top (**Dismiss** hides it).

**No connected account:** like briefings, a day plan belongs to you, so it needs a connected Slack workspace, Google account or Jira site to know who you are. Without one, a day with no plan shows "Connect Slack, Google or Jira so Watchtower knows who you are" with an **Open Connections** button (Settings → Connections) instead of the empty timeline, and the footer offers no Generate, Regenerate or Reset. A plan that already exists for the day stays readable.

### Inbox
A flat queue of things waiting on **your decision** — not a feed you scroll and forget. Every card exists because of something you did: reacting to a Slack message with a dictionary emoji, asking the assistant to remind you about something, or leaving a chat proposal unapproved. Nothing is ever added here from passive Slack/Jira/Calendar/Gmail activity — for "what happened while I was away," see Catch Up instead.

**How it works — reaction commands:** react to a Slack message with an emoji from your dictionary (configured in Settings → Slack, see below) and Watchtower gathers the message's context, composes it into a task, and brings back a ready decision here. The built-in dictionary covers 👀 (spin up a Track), 💡 (capture an Idea), ⏰ (remind me later), 📌 (a read-only context brief), and ✅/🎫 (a Target / a Jira issue, from the original release). This whole surface is the **Reaction Commands** feature, on by default (it can be turned off from Settings → Features). Watchtower checks your reactions on every sync cycle — use the tray's **Sync Now** to have a fresh reaction picked up right away. Note that the first check after connecting records reactions you had already placed as history rather than acting on them, and removing and re-adding the same emoji on the same message does not re-trigger it — react on a different message instead.

**The list:** two sections, newest/soonest first.
- **Reminders** — a reminder only surfaces once it comes due (its time has passed); a still-pending one shows nothing. Each row offers **Done** and **Snooze**.
- **Proposals** — every action awaiting a decision, regardless of where it came from (a reaction, the main AI Chat, a Target's chat, or a workbench's Claude Code terminal — e.g. a Slack message it wants to send): **Approve**, **Reject**, or **Retry** (if it failed) — the same cards and the same underlying approve/reject path used inside those chats. A tool marked low-risk (idea capture, a reminder, a context brief) applies itself immediately and appears here only as a confirmation card; anything that touches a Target, a Track, or an external system like Jira waits for your Approve.

**Links on the cards:** once an action is done, its card links to what it produced — **Open →** takes you to the new Idea (selected and visible in the Ideas tab even if a filter or search would have hidden it), Target or Track, a created Jira issue's link (**Open ABC-7 →**) opens it in your browser, and a connected Jira board opens the Boards tab. A context brief shows its summary right on the card; a reminder has nothing to open (it comes back to this strip when due). Every card that came from a reaction — and every due reminder — also has a **Slack message ↗** link back to the message you reacted to.

**Empty state — the reaction cheat sheet:** when both sections are empty, the tab shows a cheat sheet instead of a blank page: one line per enabled emoji in your dictionary — the emoji, what it does in plain words ("Brief me", "Save as idea"…), whether it asks for your Approve or runs immediately (a Jira issue always asks), and where the result lands (Targets, Jira, Tracks, Ideas, or this Inbox). It is read live from the same dictionary as Settings → Slack, so an edit there shows up here; disabled mappings are left out. If Reaction Commands is off, an **Enable Reaction Commands** button turns it on right here (reactions you already placed are not replayed — only new ones count, and removing and re-adding the same emoji on the same message will not trigger it; react on a different message instead); if it is on, a status line says "Watching your Slack reactions — last check N min ago" (the time of the last successful poll, omitted until there has been one). **Edit reaction commands in Settings…** opens Settings → Connections → Slack. When the strip has cards, the same cheat sheet sits behind the **?** button above the list.

**Actions / Learned / Profile:** the segmented control at the top of the tab switches between three views.
- **Actions** — the strip described above.
- **Learned** — the rules manager. These are the preferences the assistant applies when it writes your digests, tracks, briefings and Catch-Up recaps: each rule shows its weight and where it came from ("learned from your feedback" / "I added this manually"), and you can add, edit or remove any of them. New rules come from the 👍/👎 you leave on a Catch-Up topic when you also type a comment.
- **Profile** — your assistant brief: a free-text description of your role and what matters to you, used by Catch-Up, the idea chat and meeting follow-ups.

The sidebar badge counts what the strip shows: proposals awaiting your decision plus due reminders.

**Note on the previous Inbox:** the AI-clustered situations Dashboard ("what happened" across Slack/Jira/Calendar/Gmail) was removed in September 2026 — that recap job belongs to **Catch Up**, and "what did this channel discuss" belongs to **Digests** and **Tracks**. What survives is silent: Watchtower still detects the mentions, DMs, thread replies, Jira comment mentions, calendar invites and mail addressed to you, and still closes each one automatically when you answer in the source — but there is no per-item screen for it. That detection is what fills Catch Up's "needs you" list and the daily Briefing's attention section. Situations already created are kept and still reachable from any Target or Track that was made from one; no new ones are generated.

### Ideas
A registry for ideas and proposals — things not yet decided — that surface in Slack threads, meetings, Jira comments, and email and would otherwise get lost. The pipeline mines candidates from all four sources a few times a day; you only triage what it finds. Decisions are mined by the same pipeline, but they never land here — a mined decision is born settled and goes straight to the Digests → Decisions ledger (see the Digests section below) instead of asking for review.

**How it works:** Every stream first gets a lightweight pre-digest — Slack and meeting recaps already extract `ideas`/`decisions`, and Gmail/Jira get their own new digest passes over recent threads and issues (comments included). A few times a day, a stronger consolidation pass reads only the new material plus your recent verdicts, and proposes new ideas or attaches a repeat mention to something already in the registry.

**List (left):** an **Ideas | Notes** segmented control on top splits the two into separate lists — each segment has its own "For review" section (freshly proposed entries plus anything resurfaced: a repeat mention of something you'd already said "not now" to or rejected), then the full registry below, filterable by status, with a text search over titles, essence, and mention quotes. A flagged note is reviewed under Notes, so it never mixes into your ideas — each segment's label carries its own waiting-for-review count ("Notes (2)"), so a queue on the other side is visible without switching to it.

**Detail (right):** the essence, a 👍/👎 rating with an optional teaching comment, and an action bar that depends on the item's status — **Approve**/**Reject** for a freshly proposed item, **Not now** (with an optional wake date) / **Activate** to toggle it back, **Convert to Target** to graduate an idea into a trackable target, **Merge** (pre-filled when the miner flagged a similar existing item) to fold duplicates together, and **Drop**. A trash button on the right deletes the entry outright — available whatever its kind or status, and asking to confirm first. Below that: the mentions chronology (quote, author, and a deep link back to the source — Slack, the meeting transcript, the Gmail thread, or the Jira issue), then a collapsed Discuss chat scoped to that item. It looks and behaves like the AI Chat (streamed replies, Stop, Copy, an error card with Retry), and collapsing it — or leaving the item — never stops a reply that is still being written: open it again and the answer is there.

**Converting or merging never deletes anything** — the original row stays, linked to the target it became or the item it merged into, so its history stays reachable. **Delete** is the one exception, and it is permanent: the entry, its mentions, and its Discuss chat all go, and anything previously merged into it goes too (a merged duplicate keeps no content of its own — its mentions moved to the entry you're deleting — so it would only be left behind as an empty shell). Since a mined entry's mentions are also what stops the miner re-proposing it, deleting one you mined can bring it back on a later pass; **Drop** or **Reject** is the way to say "no" and have it stay said.

**Create (+):** add an idea or note by hand — title and free text, no triage needed since it starts out active. (A manual decision is created from the Digests → Decisions ledger instead.)

**Find Ideas:** the sparkle-search button next to Create opens a single-calendar range picker — presets for the last two weeks, month, or quarter, or pick your own from/to by clicking two days in the month grid (either order; arrows page through months) — and mines that historical window on demand. The daemon's regular pass only looks at recent activity, so this is how you backfill ideas and decisions from before you turned the feature on, or catch up after time away — mined decisions from a backfill land straight in the Digests → Decisions ledger, same as a regular cycle. Start closes the sheet and the run continues in the background: a "Mining…" pill with a running timer sits in the Ideas header (you can switch tabs and come back, the run keeps going), turning into the result — the proposed-count summary, or "Mining failed" — when it finishes; clicking the pill reopens the sheet for details or Cancel. Re-running the same range, or an overlapping one, is always safe — it never creates duplicate items or mentions.

**Sidebar badge** — count of ideas/notes awaiting review (freshly proposed + resurfaced), across both segments whichever one is open; decisions never contribute to this count.

**Settings:** the registry is turned on or off with **Ideas & Decisions** in the Settings → Features list; while it is on, the **Ideas** card below the list adjusts how often it mines in the background (1–48 hours, default 6). The Gmail/Jira stream-digest mining that feeds it (stage 1) is its own feature, **Stream Digests**, in the same list.

### Voice Dictation
Dictate instead of typing, wherever you'd otherwise write free-form text: the idea create sheet, a recording's Notes tab, and every Discuss chat input. A mic button (🎙) sits next to each of those fields.

**Speaking and stopping:** click the mic (or focus the field and it's right there) to start — the button pulses red while listening. With engines that support live transcription (the WhisperKit and Qwen3 choices in Settings), your words appear in the field live as you speak, window by window; with a batch-only engine (Parakeet, Apple) the field stays put until you stop, then the text arrives all at once. Click again (or press Esc) to stop. **In a chat input** — every Discuss chat, the setup assistants — what you said lands in the field exactly as transcribed: no AI pass, no waiting, nothing rephrased. **In the idea create sheet and a recording's Notes tab**, the button shows a brief spinner while a light AI pass cleans up the raw transcript — dropping filler words and false starts, and shaping it for where it's going (a title + body for an idea, a coherent note) — then replaces what you said with the cleaned version. There, a "Cleaned" chip with a **Raw** button appears for a few seconds afterward if you'd rather keep your original wording; clicking it swaps the cleaned text back for the raw transcript. Dictating into an empty field starts fresh; dictating into a field that already has text appends after it.

If nothing was actually said (silence), the field is simply left as it was — no error. If the cleanup step fails (idea and notes only — a chat dictation has no cleanup step to fail), a small error note appears; with a live engine your raw transcript stays in the field, while with a batch-only engine the field is left as it was before you started — either way, nothing you dictated is silently lost.

**When dictation is unavailable:** while a meeting recording is capturing or a recording is still being processed (transcribing/diarizing/summarizing), the mic buttons are disabled with a tooltip explaining why — meeting capture always takes priority, and if you were mid-dictation when a meeting recording starts, whatever you'd said so far is cleaned and delivered to its field automatically before the meeting takes over. Only one field can dictate at a time; starting a meeting recording or another field's dictation disables the rest until it's free again.

**Quick Capture — dictate without opening the app:** press **⌃⌥D** (Control+Option+D) anywhere in macOS, or choose **New Voice Idea** from the Watchtower menu-bar icon, to open a small floating window that starts listening immediately — no need to bring the main window forward first. Speak, click Stop, review the cleaned result, and click **Save** to drop it straight into the Ideas registry (as an active, ready-to-use idea — no review step). Closing the window before saving cancels the capture. If cleanup fails, whatever was transcribed before the failure can still be saved as-is. A confirmation closes the window automatically after a couple of seconds, or click "Open Ideas" to jump straight to it in the main window.

### Calendar
Google Calendar integration showing upcoming events and AI-powered meeting preparation.

**Connecting:** Go to Settings › Connections › Google and click "Connect". This opens the Google OAuth flow via the CLI (`watchtower calendar login`). Once connected, calendar events sync automatically after each Slack sync.

**Unified Meetings screen** — The Calendar screen is a single master-detail list, not a split between events and recordings: every calendar event and every recording (event-linked or ad-hoc) lives in one chronology. Shows past days (last `calendar.history_days` days, default 14) plus the next 7 days, scrolled to "Today" on open. **List merge rule:** an event with one or more recordings folded into it renders as ONE row (`CalendarEventRow`, with an "N rec" badge); an ad-hoc recording, or a recording whose linked event fell outside the window (pruned by sync retention or otherwise not loaded), renders as its own standalone row at its recording time. The list is fully chronological — past days on top, ascending into "Today" and the upcoming days — so scrolling up from "Today" goes back in time. Each event row displays: time range (or "All day"), duration, title, location, attendee count, and the recording badge when present. Events happening now have a green highlight; upcoming events (within 1 hour) have a blue highlight plus a live "in N min" countdown next to the time range, and show a "Join" button when they have a conference link and haven't ended yet. Past day sections are dimmed but fully browsable — past meetings keep their prep, recordings, and recaps reachable. Selecting any row (event or standalone recording) opens the right-hand detail pane; clicking the selected row again — or the pane's ✕ close button — closes it. Ad-hoc recordings are meetings without a calendar event, and are recorded via the "Record" button in the list header, which stays pinned above the list (always visible, no scrolling back to the top).

**Now line** — The Today section shows a red current-time line among its timed rows (started meetings above, upcoming below), ticking once a minute. When that line is scrolled off-screen, a floating "Now" button appears at the bottom of the list (arrow pointing toward the line) and jumps back to it.

**Detail pane composition** — Selecting an event row opens a header (title, time range/duration, attendees, description — collapsed to a 3-line preview with a "Show more" toggle revealing the full agenda when it is longer, text selectable — Prepare / Join / Record / "Open in Google Calendar") followed by its recordings: nothing when the event has no recordings, a compact selector (start time, duration, language badges) when it has more than one, and the selected recording's full detail (the same Recap/Notes/Transcript/Chat tabs described under "Recording detail" below) embedded beneath — a single-recording event skips the selector and just shows that recording's detail directly. Selecting a standalone recording row opens its detail directly, full pane. Clicking "Prepare" swaps the pane to the AI meeting-prep view (see "Meeting Prep" below); its own close button returns to the meeting detail. The pane's header carries a ✕ button that closes the detail pane entirely (clicking the selected row again does the same). **Record button shows only from 10 minutes before the meeting's start until its end** — hidden for events further out (recording starts capturing immediately and links the audio to that event, so a button on a far-future event would mislabel whatever is happening now) and once the meeting has ended; while that event's recording is running the button (as the Stop control) stays regardless. Join remains conditional on a conference link as before.

**Meeting reminders** — A local notification fires N minutes before each meeting (Settings › Features › Meeting Reminders; default 5 minutes, 0 disables). When the event has a conference link the notification carries "Join" and "Join + Record" action buttons ("Join + Record" always starts an event-linked recording, regardless of the auto-record setting). While the app is open, a countdown banner also appears at the top of the window for the next meeting starting within the same window, with Join / Record / dismiss buttons — the banner needs no notification permission. If an event-linked recording is still running 2 minutes after the event's scheduled end, a "Meeting ended — still recording" notification with a "Stop recording" action fires and repeats every 10 minutes until stopped (ad-hoc recordings are exempt). This stop-recording alert is always on — it ignores both the "Meeting reminders" toggle and the minutes stepper (0/"Off" silences only pre-meeting reminders) and is suppressed only by quiet hours or denied notification permission. Pre-meeting reminders respect quiet hours and re-fire if a meeting is rescheduled.

**Join button** — Events with a conference link (Google Meet, Zoom, Teams, Webex — from the event's Meet field, conference data, or a link pasted into the location/description, including CalDAV/ICS events) show a "Join" button next to Prepare/Record, a filled blue button while the meeting is imminent or ongoing (its label stays readable in light and dark mode and when the window is in the background). Clicking it opens the meeting link in the default browser and — if "Auto-record on join" is enabled in Settings › Meetings (default on) and no recording is already running — also starts an event-linked recording. A recording already in flight is never interrupted: Join then only opens the link. The sidebar's "next event" indicator shows the same Join button when that event has a link. That indicator shows the meeting's title on up to two lines (the full title on hover) and when it starts: a countdown ("in 45 sec", "in 12 min", "in 2 h 5 min") up to six hours ahead, then the start time ("today 18:30", "tomorrow 09:30", a weekday or a date).

**Meeting Prep** — Click "Prepare" on any event to open the AI-powered meeting prep sheet. The AI analyzes attendees' people cards, shared tracks, open items, and recent context to generate:
- **Talking Points** — prioritized topics to discuss (high/medium/low), linked to source tracks or digests
- **Open Items** — unresolved items relevant to this meeting's attendees, with person attribution
- **People Notes** — communication tips and recent context for each attendee (from people cards)
- **Suggested Prep** — action items to review before the meeting

Meeting prep is generated via the CLI (`watchtower meeting-prep [event-id|next] --json`). Prep is kept per event for as long as the app runs: leaving Day Plan or Calendar mid-run does not lose it, and reopening Prepare on the same event shows the run still in progress or its finished result instead of starting another one (**Refresh** forces a fresh one). That kept result is the one this app session produced; a newer prep generated elsewhere shows only after **Refresh**. If a Refresh fails, the previous prep stays on screen under an orange banner giving the reason.

**Sidebar** — Shows a compact "next event" indicator with time and title when calendar is connected. The Calendar tab is a top-level sidebar item right under Workbench, shown once a Google account with Calendar or a CalDAV/ICS calendar is connected (it is not in the collapsible FOCUS section and has no Hide menu — if you had hidden it there before, it shows again).

**Not connected** — With no calendar connected the Calendar tab is not in the sidebar at all; connect Google Calendar or a CalDAV/ICS calendar under Settings › Connections and it appears.

**Settings** — The Google Calendar section under Settings › Connections › Google shows connection status and a "Sync days ahead" picker (3/5/7/14 days) when connected. Config fields: `calendar.sync_days_ahead` (default: 2), `calendar.history_days` (past days kept synced and shown in the Meetings list; default: 14).

**Briefing integration** — Today's calendar events appear as "Today's Schedule" at the top of the briefing detail view, before "Needs Attention". Events are also included in the briefing AI prompt for context-aware recommendations.

**AI Chat integration** — Nothing is pre-loaded into the chat context. Ask about your schedule and the assistant looks it up: calendar events, meetings, transcripts and recaps are all indexed by knowledge search, so a question like "what's my next meeting with X" is answered on demand rather than from a standing calendar block.

**Daemon** — Calendar sync runs after each Slack sync, before AI pipelines. It fetches events for the configured number of days ahead from selected Google Calendars.

**CLI commands:**
- `watchtower calendar` — show upcoming events (flags: `--days N`, `--json`)
- `watchtower calendar login` — connect Google Calendar (OAuth flow)
- `watchtower calendar logout` — disconnect Google account #1's calendar and remove that account's events (other accounts, CalDAV/ICS and recorded meetings' events are kept)
- `watchtower calendar sync` — manually sync events

**Meeting Recording & Transcripts** — Record a meeting's audio directly from the calendar and get a local transcript plus an AI recap attached to the event. Requires macOS 14.4+.

- **Recording:** select an event and click "Record" in its detail pane (or the "Record" button in the list header — pinned above the list, always visible — for an ad-hoc recording not tied to any event; an ad-hoc recording is a meeting without a calendar event). Record shows only from 10 minutes before the meeting's start until its end (it stays while that event's recording is running). Captures your microphone AND system audio (other participants) natively — no virtual audio devices; headphones keep working normally. First use asks for two one-time permissions: Microphone and System Audio Recording.
- **Global indicator:** while recording, a floating pill (bottom-right, visible from any tab) shows elapsed time and a Stop button. Recording survives navigating between tabs. After Stop the recording joins a post-processing queue and gets a pill of its own — the indicator stacks the recording capsule (when you are recording) plus one pill per queued, in-progress ("Transcribing" with window N of M, "Identifying speakers…", "Summarizing…") or failed recording, each labelled with its meeting title, so nothing in flight is hidden.
- **Switching headphones mid-recording:** when the Mac's output or input device changes during a recording (headphones plugged in or out, AirPods connecting or disconnecting), the recording reconnects its call audio and microphone capture to the current devices by itself about a second later and keeps going in the same file — only a fraction of a second at the switch is missing, for the call and the microphone alike. No permission prompt appears. If the reconnect fails (it is retried twice, then again on the next device change), the recording carries on with whatever it was still capturing and the **No call audio** warning below appears as usual. Turned off by the "Follow audio device changes" toggle in Settings › Meetings.
- **No call audio warning:** if the call has been heard and then nothing comes from it for over two minutes, the pill shows **No call audio** in orange and a notification goes out — if people are still talking, the call's sound is not reaching the recording (typically after switching headphones or output device), and you can fix it while the meeting is still on. The warning (and the notification) clears as soon as call audio returns or the recording stops. Afterwards, the recording's detail shows a note such as "No call audio from 15:29 to the end — the transcript there may hold only your microphone", and a recording that produced no text says when the call audio was missing instead of a bare "No speech recognized". A room-only meeting with no call never triggers it.
- **Recording while a transcription runs:** you can start a NEW recording before the previous one has finished transcribing — capture never waits for processing. Only one transcription runs at a time, so while the previous recording still holds the transcription engine the new one's live panel reads "Waiting for previous transcription… live transcript will catch up."; the audio keeps being captured and the live transcript fills in once the engine is free.
- **Transcription is fully local** (on-device; the model downloads once on first use). The transcription **Engine** is chosen in Settings › Meetings — WhisperKit is the default and its **Large v3 Turbo** model is the default choice, faster and lighter than full Large v3 while staying multilingual; both the engine and its model are switchable there, with a warning if the engine doesn't support your configured languages. Mixed ru/uk/en meetings are handled by per-window language detection with a sticky fallback. Only the transcript text is sent to the AI provider for the recap.
- **Speaker roles:** saved transcripts label who was speaking — `[Я]` for you (detected from the microphone channel) and `[Speaker 1..N]` for other participants (on-device diarization; models download once on first use). Toggle "Speaker roles" in Settings › Meetings; while roles are being computed the floating pill shows "Identifying speakers…". If diarization is unavailable the transcript is saved without labels.
- **Speaker names (voice registry):** Watchtower recognizes recurring voices across recordings and learns them as it goes — see "Voices window" below for the full flow. `[Я]` normally wins over a voice match, with one refinement for meeting rooms where every voice comes through your mic: when your own voice (learned once you've confirmed it under your Google-account email) confidently identifies your cluster, it wins `[Я]` over a merely louder colleague, and a cluster confidently identified as a colleague is not labeled `[Я]` even if it dominates the mic — the transcript may then have no `[Я]` at all rather than a wrong one. Voice data never leaves your machine unless you deliberately export it (see "Sharing voices" below).
- **Results:** every recording (event-linked and ad-hoc) gets its own detail — Recap/Notes/Transcript/Chat tabs — embedded in its meeting's detail pane in the unified Meetings list (see "Recording detail" below); an event with more than one recording shows a compact selector above the embedded detail.
- **Failure handling:** the audio file is never lost — failed transcription or recap offers Retry; if the app quits mid-recording, the next launch offers to transcribe the recovered file (or **Dismiss** it if you don't want a transcript — the pill goes away and the audio is later reclaimed by retention). There can be several recovered recordings at once — the pill then reads "Transcribe N recovered recordings" and works through them oldest first, one at a time; Dismiss also takes the oldest.
- **Retention:** audio files are auto-deleted after `transcripts.audio_retention_days` (default 30); transcripts are kept forever.
- **Chat integration:** the assistant chat can search transcripts via MCP tools `list_transcripts` / `get_transcript` ("what did we decide about X in Tuesday's meeting").
- **Settings › Meetings:** Engine picker, model picker (options depend on the selected engine), language set, "Live transcription" toggle (default **on** — transcribe while recording so text appears during the meeting; off = record only and transcribe after Stop, keeping the machine idle during the call; flipping it mid-recording applies to the NEXT recording, not the one in progress), "Cross-window context (experimental)" toggle (default off — feeds each transcription window the previous window's text for continuity, at roughly 1.4x decode time; WhisperKit engine only), "Mic auto-gain (experimental)" toggle (default **off** — boosts a quiet microphone toward a healthy recording level during the moments when you are the dominant sound in it, so your own voice is not lost in the recording; moments where remote participants dominate are left untouched, so their audio leaking into your mic is never amplified into an echo, and the boost holds for about a second so it does not flicker between syllables), "Follow audio device changes" toggle (default **on** — reconnect the call audio capture when the output or input device changes during a recording; applies from the next recording), "Preload model before meetings" toggle (default **on** — keeps the transcription model in memory between recordings and loads it about five minutes before a meeting on your calendar starts, so pressing Record transcribes immediately instead of waiting for the model to load; off = the model is loaded per recording and released afterwards, keeping memory free at the cost of that wait), "Auto-record on join" toggle (pressing Join on a calendar event also starts an event-linked recording), retention days, the Speakers section's **Diarization threshold** field (speaker-clustering strictness between 0.3 and 0.9, lower = more distinct speakers, worth lowering when different people keep landing in one "Speaker N"), and advanced detection tuning (window size, confidence threshold, margin, force language). A capability caption under the pickers summarizes what the selected engine/model can do — live transcript while recording vs. text only after Stop, and how many languages it supports ("any language" for WhisperKit). The live in-progress transcript panel while recording only appears for engines that support live transcription; batch-only engines (Apple, Parakeet) showing no live text during recording is expected, not a failure.
- **CLI:** `watchtower meeting-prep transcript save|recap|list|show|notes` (the app calls `save` after transcribing; `recap <id>` retries a failed recap; `notes <id>` generates publishable meeting notes).

**Recording detail** — Embedded in the Meetings screen's detail pane (see "Detail pane composition" above), whether reached via an event's recordings selector or directly for a standalone recording row. Four tabs:

- **Recap** — with generated **chapters** (created automatically after a recording is saved, or via "Generate chapters"): an overall meeting summary on top, then each chapter as an expandable group — title, time range, participants, and inside it the chapter's summary, decisions, action items, and open questions. Tapping a chapter's time range jumps to that spot in the Transcript tab. Each action item has a **Target** button that turns it into a Target (the item stays in the chapter, marked as converted with a link to the Target). **Follow-up draft** buttons (one per chapter plus one for the whole meeting) ask the AI to draft a follow-up message in your own voice from the stated decisions/action items — shown in a copyable sheet, never sent automatically. Below the chapters: "Re-generate chapters" (asks for confirmation — the chapters are replaced; Target links carry over to action items whose text is unchanged, and created Targets are never deleted) and "Re-generate recap" (the recap stays reachable even though chapters replace the flat recap view). Without chapters the tab shows the flat structured recap (summary, decisions, action items, open questions) with "Generate recap" / "Re-generate", plus "Generate chapters" when the recording has per-utterance segments. For event-linked recordings the event's recap is shown; ad-hoc recordings use their own.
- **Notes** — publishable meeting notes: click "Generate" and the AI writes a markdown document (title, participants, summary, decisions, action items, next steps) from the transcript, in a neutral publication-ready tone. Edit it right there (autosaves; the editor is locked while generation runs), then "Copy" puts the markdown on the clipboard for pasting into Slack, Confluence, etc.
- **Transcript** — the full transcript. Recordings with speaker roles show it as per-utterance bubbles (speaker + timecode); hovering an utterance reveals a delete button (soft delete with a 5-second Undo toast). Click any non-«Я» speaker label to **Listen to samples** — this opens the Voices window (see below) scoped to that voice, where you label it by listening rather than by typing a name blind. Older recordings without segments show plain text. If a label changes after the recap was generated, the recap header shows "Speaker names were updated — regenerate the recap?" — nothing regenerates on its own.
- **Chat** — discuss this meeting with the assistant ("what exactly did we decide about X?", "draft a follow-up") — the conversation persists per recording, and the assistant can pull the full transcript via the `get_transcript` tool. It looks and behaves like the AI Chat: replies stream in, **Stop** keeps what was written so far, every message has **Copy**, and a failed reply shows what went wrong with **Retry**. Switching to another recording or leaving the screen never stops a reply; come back and it is there. The Track detail's chat at the bottom of the pane works the same way.

The detail header has "Link to event…" for ad-hoc recordings and a **Delete** button: after confirmation it removes the recording with everything it owns — transcript, meeting notes, its chat, and the audio file. An event's recap is NOT deleted with the recording (it belongs to the event). Deleting cannot be undone.

**Voices window** — a small standalone window (opened from the menu bar, never from the sidebar) for teaching Watchtower who's speaking. Three segments:

- **Queue** — one card per unrecognized or uncertain voice: the meeting, date, a reason ("Not recognized" / "Looks like X (0.66)" / "From an imported file: is this X?" / "Two sources disagree"), and 2–3 short clips (a few seconds each, from different moments of the meeting) with the words spoken in each. A clip's button shows its length ("▶ 6 s") and "at 9:40" where it starts in the meeting; click it to play, click it again ("■ Stop") to stop, or click another clip to switch to it (nothing is written to disk for this). A person picker comes preselected with the best guess, in groups: "In this meeting" (you, shown as "Me", then that meeting's attendees), "Similar voices" (registry people whose voice is close to this one) and "Other known voices", then "New person…". Picking a name does not save it — the card says "Not saved until you press Confirm" until you do. **Confirm** labels the voice everywhere it appears in that recording and teaches it for next time; **Don't know** leaves it as "Speaker N" for good (on a voice that already had a name — a "Listen to samples" card — it takes that wrong name off and puts "Speaker N" back); **Several people** marks the voice as mixed (never learned from); **Skip** leaves it in the queue. Confirming a different person on an already-named voice also forgets what it had taught the old name. A name another speaker of the same recording already has is refused — two voices are never merged under one name. Keys — space plays, 1–9 picks, Enter confirms — act on the first (highlighted) card only; cards whose voice gets recognized meanwhile (for example, by a confirmation in another meeting) leave the queue on their own. A post-meeting notification ("N voices to label") opens straight into that meeting's cards when there's anything to label — a clean recording gets no notification. Once a voice is confirmed, older recordings of the same person that were still unnamed pick up the name automatically, and if that recording already had a recap, its header offers "Speaker names were updated — regenerate the recap?" (never automatic).
- **Review** — on demand only, never scheduled: the registry list (each person's sample counts by channel, when they were last recognized, delete), a spot-check of the most recent auto-labeled clips per person (Correct / Wrong — Wrong undoes that label and re-queues the voice), and the import list ("delete everything from this sender").
- **Train** — bulk labeling across many meetings at once, grouping voices that sound alike into one card so one confirmation labels every occurrence; shows a running quality estimate (how much speech is named, estimated precision/recall) so you can see whether training is helping.

**Sharing voices** — from Review, **Export…** shares your registry (you can deselect people, including yourself) as an encrypted `.wtvoices` file protected by a password you set — never audio, transcript text, meeting titles or dates, just the numbers that identify a voice. **Import…** takes a file and its password, previews who and how many samples it would add, and merges by email; an imported voice only ever *suggests* a name ("from an imported file") until you confirm it once — it never renames anything on its own.

Settings › Meetings → Speakers: **"Voice recognition"** (default on — off stops labeling and notifications, but keeps what's already learned) and **"Notify about unknown voices"** (default on, only meaningful while voice recognition is on).

### Targets (tasks)
Personal action items — what you need to do, follow up on, or react to. Unlike Tracks (which are informational narratives about ongoing initiatives), Tasks are concrete, personal to-do items with ownership and deadlines. Targets of a Workbench board are not listed here; they live on their workbench's board.

**Creating targets** — "New target" opens a **composer**, not a form: one multiline text field with a permanent hint underneath ("Enter — brief the assistant · ⌘Enter — just create"). Write the task in your own words — as much or as little context as you have — and:

- **Enter — brief the assistant.** The target is created instantly and locally (its provisional title is the first line of your text; no AI is involved in creating the row, so an unreachable AI can never block it), and your full text is sent as the first message in the target's Discuss chat. The assistant treats it as a directive: it derives a clean title from your own words, decomposes the work into sub-items or child targets, gathers context from your synced Jira/Slack data and meeting transcripts, fills in the "why it matters" intent, and sets priority or a due date when you asked for one. The detail view opens with the chat working; the run keeps going if you navigate away. If the AI fails mid-run, the target still exists — the error shows in the chat and you can just re-ask there.
- **⌘Enter — just create.** Same instant mechanical creation, no chat message, no AI call — for trivial targets. (Shift+Enter inserts a newline.)

The **Extract** control remains for pasting a large blob of text and splitting it into several targets at once. The old form fields (title, context, checklist, level) are gone — precision editing lives in the target detail view.

Targets can be created from six sources:

| Source | How |
|--------|-----|
| **Track** | "Take action" button on a track detail view |
| **Briefing** | "Add task" on coaching recommendations or attention items |
| **Digest** | Button on a specific decision or topic |
| **Idea** | "Convert to Target" on an approved idea |
| **Manual** | "+" button in the Targets toolbar or sidebar |
| **Chat** | AI suggests creating a task during conversation |

Every source opens the same composer, prefilled with the source's text. Each task created from a source retains a link back to it. Click the source link in the detail view to navigate to the originating track, digest, briefing, or idea. (A seventh source, the old Inbox situations Dashboard, was removed in September 2026 — targets already created from a situation keep their back-link and still open it.)

**Directing the assistant from the chat** — the target's Discuss chat understands two kinds of messages. A question or discussion ("what do you think?", "what do we know about X?") gets an answer, and any suggested changes appear as proposal cards you approve or reject — nothing is applied without you. An instruction ("break this into steps", "set the deadline to Friday", "pull the data from Jira") is executed: the assistant applies the changes to this target immediately (including creating new sub-tasks under it) and reports what was done; a change to an existing sub-task or a parent arrives as a card awaiting your **Approve** (see "Which tasks the assistant can touch" below). It only ever touches this target and its subtree, only does what your instruction implies, and anything interesting it finds beyond that is reported as text for you to act on. It never acts between your messages and never re-runs an instruction on its own. The chat itself looks and behaves like the AI Chat — replies stream in, **Stop** keeps what was written so far, every message has **Copy**, and a failed reply shows what went wrong with **Retry** — and a reply keeps going while you look at another tab or another screen. At most three such assistant replies (across tasks, tracks, ideas and meetings) run at once; a fourth waits, marked **Queued**, and **Stop** takes it back into the input.

**Today view vs All view** — The list is split into two sections:
- **Today** — overdue tasks, tasks due today, and high-priority active tasks. This is your daily focus area (inbox zero approach).
- **All Tasks** — everything else not shown in Today.

**Lifecycle:**
- **To Do** — created, not yet started
- **In Progress** — actively working on it
- **Blocked** — waiting on someone or something (specify in the "blocking" field)
- **Done** — completed
- **Dismissed** — no longer relevant
- **Snoozed** — hidden until a specified date, then returns to To Do

**Ownership & delegation:**
- **Mine** — you own this task
- **Delegated** — assigned to someone else; you track completion
- **Watching** — not your responsibility, but you want visibility

**Ball on** — indicates who currently needs to act. Useful for delegated tasks.

**Task detail** shows: text, intent (why it matters), status switcher, priority badge, ownership badge, due date, sub-items checklist, tags, blocking info, source link, and feedback buttons (thumbs up/down).

**Checklist order** — drag a checklist item onto another item to reorder it; the dropped item takes that position and the new order is saved immediately.

An **ask bar** sits at the bottom of the Details tab, below the scrolling content so it stays reachable wherever you are on the page. What you type there goes to the task's active assistant chat and switches you to the Assistant tab for the reply.

**Assistant (task chat)** — in a discussion the assistant proposes changes instead of making them (on a direct instruction, edits to this task apply at once — see above): each proposal shows up as a **Proposed change** card with **Approve** / **Reject** (and, for a "create something" proposal, a picker for whether it becomes a checkpoint or a sub-task). It can propose most task edits you could make yourself: status, progress, notes, priority, due date, who the ball is on, labels (add or remove), creating sub-tasks and links — and full checkpoint upkeep (add, check off, reword, delete, set a per-item due date). A proposal is not written until you approve it. When the assistant proposes a whole batch at once, an **Approve all** button appears in the chat right above that batch of cards: it applies every still-undecided proposal and tells the assistant about the batch in one go, instead of one round-trip per card. A big batch (4+ proposals) arrives as one collapsed block — what it contains ("39 proposed changes — 9× check off · 21× edit…"), Approve all, and a **Review** toggle that expands the individual cards for deciding one by one; after decisions the block shows how many applied or failed, and the confirmation the assistant gets is a count rather than a re-listing of every item (failures are always spelled out). Approving or rejecting never has to wait for the assistant to stop typing — the change is applied straight away, and the assistant is told as soon as its current reply finishes (or with your next message, if you stopped it).

**Which tasks the assistant can touch** — a task's chat can also propose changes to that task's **sub-tasks at any depth and its parents**, not just the task you opened it on; a card that lands on another task says which one. It can never reach a task off that line — a sibling, or anything unrelated — and such a proposal fails with an explanation instead of writing somewhere unexpected. When you give it a direct instruction it may apply changes to **the task you are looking at** without asking; anything that *changes* another task always waits for your **Approve**, however plainly you asked for it. Linking two tasks is the one exception — the link belongs to the task you are on, so it can be made directly, and it then shows on both tasks' screens.

**Next step card** — the task detail suggests one concrete next step with up to three buttons (ask the assistant, open the linked items, mark done, dismiss). It is a suggestion the AI regenerates, not a plan: ↻ regenerates it at any time. Once the task has moved on since the suggestion was written — you edited it, or you had a chat turn with its assistant — the card gains a **Context changed since this step** strip with a **Refresh step** button. The old text stays visible until you refresh; nothing is regenerated behind your back, the AI runs when you click (or on the daemon's own schedule).

**Chat tabs** — one task can hold several assistant chats: the chips above the conversation switch between them, **+** opens another, and right-clicking a chip offers **Rename…** and **Close** (closing deletes that chat and its messages; the last remaining chat can't be closed). A new chat is called "New chat" until your first message names it. Each tab keeps its own thread, so a turn started in one keeps running while you read another — its chip shows a dot while it works — and it also keeps running if you leave the task screen entirely.

**Quick actions** — right-click any task for: Mark Done, Dismiss, change Status, change Priority, Delete.

**Filters** — filter by level, priority (high/medium/low), or a custom label, or toggle to show completed tasks. The Label submenu lists every label in use and appears once at least one task carries a label.

**Labels** — free-form tags on a task, edited in the detail view's About section: type into "Add label..." and press Enter, pick an existing label from the **+** menu, or remove one with the × on its chip. A task can carry any number of labels; the filter menu shows tasks carrying the selected label.

**Sidebar badge** — shows active task count (todo + in_progress + blocked). Turns red when overdue tasks exist.

**Briefing integration** — Active tasks appear in the briefing's "Your Day" section. The "Needs Attention" section may suggest creating tasks from tracks via `suggest_task`.

**Daemon** — The background daemon automatically unsnoozes tasks whose snooze date has passed, moving them back to To Do.

### Workbench
A workbench is a folder on your Mac — for example a code repository — that a coding agent (Claude Code) works in, with a **board** that outlives any one agent session. Watchtower keeps the overview: what is being worked on, what is blocked, which plans and specs are waiting for your review, and the questions the agent left for you. (Until 2 October 2026 this tab was called Projects; the AI Chat's own **Projects**, which group chats, are a different thing — see the Chat section.)

**New workbench** (the + next to **Workbenches** at the top of the panel) — **New Workbench…** asks for a workbench name and where to put it, and creates a folder of that name (an existing empty folder of that name is used as is; a folder that already has files in it is refused — add it with the other item); **Add Existing Folder…** makes a workbench of a folder you already have, such as a repository. The workbench takes the folder's name. Watchtower creates the workbench, sets the folder up for Claude Code (a `watchtower-workbench` skill, a session-start hook, an end-of-turn board check, the ask guard — see **Asks** — and a local MCP connection, all local to your Mac and excluded from git), makes the folder's text files searchable for the workbench's agent (see **Search**), and opens the workbench with its terminal running Claude Code, which reads the folder's README and docs, fills in the workbench description and sources, and proposes a first board. Nothing is created until you agree — the agent asks you in **Waiting for you**. A folder inside Documents, Desktop, Downloads or a cloud-synced folder shows a warning first: macOS may ask you to allow Watchtower access to it (for a new workbench the warning comes before the folder is created, so choosing another place leaves nothing behind).

**Board language** — the agent writes the board's targets and comments in the language you use with it in the session, whatever language the repository is in. There is no setting: write to it in Russian and the board is in Russian. Code names, file paths and plan references stay as they are, and what is already on the board is not translated.

**Sessions** — a workbench holds several Claude Code sessions, each running in the workbench folder the same as in your own terminal app: the same login, permissions and project memory. The workbench page — its header, the terminal, Board and Files — sits on the same backdrop as the AI Chat conversation, set apart from the panel on the left; the session on screen is highlighted as a tab that runs into the page. The terminal blends into that backdrop and keeps its dark colours even when macOS is in light mode, so Claude Code's dark theme stays readable. Click a workbench in the left panel to see its sessions (a green dot = running). A workbench's Claude Code session also says what the agent is doing, in a caption with a small symbol under its name and in its dot (filled while the session runs, a ring when it does not): **Working** (green) while it works on your prompt, or on its own after a message from a helper agent or a background task once its first tool succeeds (a failed one leaves "Stopped" until a later one succeeds), with a question mark and a count while asks of it are open, **Stopped** (grey) when it finished its turn, **Agents working** (green, a two-person symbol, its dot pulsing — still if Reduce Motion is on) when it finished its turn but helper agents it started in the background are still at work ("1 agent working", "3 agents working"; with a question mark and the ask count while asks are open), which turns into Stopped once they are done and the agent has nothing left to do, **Needs approval** (orange) when it asks for a permission — the state switches back to working once you allow it — **Error** (red) when its turn stopped on an error (the caption names it in words, for example "Error: rate limit"; "Stopped on an error" when Claude Code gave no reason), **Waiting for you · ask #N** (orange) while one of its asks waits for your answer, and **Finished** (blue; orange while asks are open) once the agent said it is done. When several apply, Needs approval comes first, then Error, Working, Agents working, Finished, Waiting for you and Stopped. Agents working never sends a notification — you hear about the session once it stops — and an ask's answer goes into it right away, as into a stopped session. When no helper has reported for 30 minutes, Watchtower checks with Claude Code whether the session is still at work — and again every 30 minutes while it is — and shows Stopped once it is not, or when the check fails twice in a row. Finished and waiting for you stay on a session that is no longer running, as a ring: a blue ring is finished, an orange one still has an open ask, and answering the ask turns it grey right away. A running session that has not reported anything since it started shows "Running", and one that is not running shows a grey ring and "Not running" with how long ago it ran; a working, stopped or error state from before an exit, a Restart or a Watchtower restart is never shown. Under the caption, a Claude Code session of a workbench also has a report line once Watchtower has read it: a small progress bar and, for example, "#314 · 14/15 · PR #147 open" — the target it works on, how many of its board tasks are done, and its pull requests ("2 PRs merged", "no PR yet", or "not checked" while a branch was never checked). The line refreshes every 15 seconds while the Workbench tab is on screen and when you come back to Watchtower; if a refresh fails, the last line stays with "· stale" at the end. The session switcher in the title row shows the same dot and caption; **Go to…** shows just the dot. Only sessions started in Watchtower's terminal of a workbench report a state — not your own terminal app, and not the standalone **Terminals**. Pressing Esc or Ctrl+C to interrupt the agent reports nothing, so the session says "working" until your next prompt. A workbench set up by an older Watchtower needs **Repair install** (or **Re-run Setup**) first; until then its sessions show just the green dot. A workbench set up before Agents working existed keeps showing the other states, but needs **Re-run Setup** (or **Repair install**) once so the agent count drops as each helper finishes, not only at the agent's next turn; until then the install icon says "state hooks missing". The workbench's name at the top of that list is a switcher: click it for a list of all workbenches — the ones you worked in most recently first, the current one checked, each with its new comments, blocked tasks and sessions (and how many of them are running) — type in **Find workbench** to find one by name or folder, and click one to go straight to its most recent session (the one you last used if it is running; a workbench without sessions just opens its page). Below the **RECENT** list, **New Workbench…** creates one, and **All Workbenches** returns to the workbench list and shows the panel if it is hidden. The sidebar button in the title row above the page (or ⌥⌘S) hides and shows the panel; Watchtower remembers which. With the panel hidden, the title row of a workbench page carries the workbench's name and a session side by side — the session on screen, or, while Board or Files fills the page, the running Claude Code session you used last; "No session" only when the workbench has neither. The workbench name opens the same workbench list as the panel's switcher, and the session name (a green dot = running, a hollow one = not started) opens that workbench's sessions in the panel's order — type in **Find session** to find one by title or `#` and target number, click one to open it, or pick **New Session** or **Show Sessions Panel**. On a workbench page ⌘1…⌘9 open the first nine sessions of the list (in the order the panel shows them) and ⌘T starts a new session, with the panel shown or hidden. **Go to…** at the right of the title row (or ⌘K, on the Workbench tab) opens a search over every workbench and its sessions: type part of a session's or workbench's name, or `#` and a target number. Without typing, it lists the sessions of the workbench on screen in the panel's order, then the other workbenches, the ones you worked in most recently first, each with its state and its two latest sessions (`workbench › session`). ↑ and ↓ pick a row, Return opens it (a session of another workbench opens there; a workbench opens like a pick in the switcher), ⌘Return puts a session of the current workbench beside the one you are in, as a split, and Esc or a click outside closes it. It searches names only, not files. While the cursor is in the Files editor, ⌘K belongs to the editor: use the **Go to…** button there. Drag the panel's right edge to make it wider or narrower; Watchtower remembers the width. Board, Files and the Session view are picked with the buttons in the page header. **+** starts a new session, which appears at the top of the list. The list keeps its order while you switch between sessions; drag a session to move it, and Watchtower remembers that order (per workbench, and for the Terminals list). Every session has a name that says what is in it: a provisional "New session · 14:05" until Watchtower names it from what you first asked (right-click → **Rename…** sets your own, which is kept). Sessions keep running while you switch sessions, workbenches or tabs. Right-click → **Delete…** removes a session from the list (Claude Code's own history of it stays). After a Watchtower restart nothing starts by itself: a session shows "not running" and **Resume** continues it where it left off. **Restart** appears when one exits, and **Start fresh** when it cannot be resumed. Quitting Watchtower closes every terminal. You can equally work from your own terminal — both see the same board. In a workbench session's terminal, hold ⌘ and click a `path:line` (or `path:line:column`) the agent printed — `Sources/App.swift:12`, `./x/y.go:3:7`, a quoted `"a b.txt":1` — to open that file in the Files pane at the line, beside the terminal; only files inside the workbench folder open (a path outside it, a link inside it that points outside, or a file that is not there does nothing; a path the terminal wrapped onto the next line opens too, a line with right-to-left text opens nothing), and web (`http`, `https`) and mail links open as before — in any terminal, other kinds of links (file-sharing, System Settings and the like) and bare file paths open nothing.

**Work on it** — the play button on a board card (shown on hover and on the selected card) and in the selected target's details starts a Claude Code session for that target: it is named after the target and starts working on it at once, with a fixed instruction naming only the target's number. If the target already has a session, the button (an arrow then) opens that one instead — resuming it if it is not running. From a split, the session opens next to the board. It works on a feature (a target with sub-tasks) too — from its side panel, the compact button on its lane header in Kanban (not on the **Tasks** lane of a feature you entered), its row in the list, or a right-click on any card or row (**Work on It**, or **Open Its Session**): the agent then works the feature's sub-tasks one after another, in board order or by the plan its description names, skipping ones that are blocked, waiting for you, or already in progress or in review.

**Terminals** — under the workbench list, terminals that belong to no workbench: **New terminal** opens Claude Code or a plain shell in your home folder or a folder you choose. They have no board, asks or workbench tools — just the terminal, with its folder, **Rename…** and **Delete…** above it. You can have several; quitting Watchtower closes them.

**Layout** — a workbench page shows one pane, or two side by side. The **Terminal**, **Session**, **Board** and **Files** buttons in the page header show what you pick (a highlighted button is on screen): in one pane it takes the page; in a split it goes next to the terminal, so the terminal never disappears — **Session** always shows a session's report beside that session's own terminal: from one pane the page becomes the terminal and its report side by side, and in a split already showing that terminal the report takes the other pane (it reports on the session on screen, or the one you used last, and starts nothing); **Terminal** brings back the session you had, or resumes the most recent one (starting a new one if the workbench has none). The small arrow next to **Terminal** lists the workbench's sessions and **New session**: pick one to show it in the terminal pane (or in place of the second pane, if no terminal is on screen) — handy with the side panel hidden. Clicking a highlighted button in a split closes that pane. The split button next to them (two rectangles) adds a second pane (the Board next to a terminal, or a terminal next to the Board or Files), and the divider between them can be dragged. In a split each pane's title is a menu to show something else there — Board, Files, any of the workbench's sessions, or a new session (not the Session view: use the header's **Session** button). In a split every pane has an expand button that shows it alone over the whole page, and pressing it again brings the split back; the × closes that pane. Watchtower remembers each workbench's layout, divider position included.

**Session view** — the report of one Claude Code session: what it did, what it is doing, and what waits on you. From the top: the session's state (the same caption as in the panel), its target, how long it has run, its title and branches, and a large **X / Y tasks** with a progress bar; **On you** — the session's open asks, each with **Open** (the ask's drawer); **Now** — the tasks in progress, in review or blocked, with their branch and since when; **Pull requests** — each PR's title, state and size ("open · +180/−12", "merged Oct 2, 12:00 · +420/−35"), a branch with no PR yet ("no PR yet") or one not checked yet ("not checked"); a PR's title opens it on GitHub when the workbench folder's `origin` remote is on GitHub (otherwise it is plain text); **Done** — one line per phase (a parent target) with its count and when it ran, then the next to-do tasks; and **Agent's last word** — the summary the agent wrote when it finished, shown as **Previous summary** once you give it a new prompt. Every `#` target number opens that target on the Board. The report follows the session you pick: click another session in the panel and the terminal and the report both switch to it. With no Claude Code session to show (none yet, a plain shell, or the session was deleted) it says "Pick a session". It refreshes when it appears, every 30 seconds while it is on screen, whenever the session's state changes, and at once when you come back to its window after it was hidden or covered. If a refresh fails, the report stays with the error on an orange line at the top ("The last refresh failed; this report is from an earlier run." on hover); if the very first one fails, it says "Could not load the report" with the error. Pull request details need the GitHub CLI (`gh`); without it only whether a branch is merged is known.

**Board** — the workbench's targets as a tree (a feature and its sub-targets; a written plan becomes one sub-target per task). Each target is a card: its status icon and title, its number (`#163` — the number the agent uses in the chat and the terminal; right-click a card → **Copy #163**), a priority chip and a status chip, and badges — a blue bubble for agent comments you have not read, and an orange count of open comment threads. A feature's card also shows how many of its sub-tasks are done with a small progress bar, and a click anywhere on its left strip (the arrow's column, highlighted under the pointer) folds the sub-tasks away or opens them again without opening the card. Within a feature, higher-priority targets come first, then work in progress, blocked, to do and done. Finished cards are dimmed (done ones struck through). **Show done** reveals finished work (a finished feature with an open task under it always stays visible). **Archive:** a done or dismissed target leaves the board by itself once it has been closed longer than the workbench's setting (14 days unless you change it in the page header's **…** menu → **Archive Closed Targets After**); a feature leaves only when everything under it is closed and the last of it closed that long ago, and its sub-task counter keeps counting archived tasks. To tidy up at once, **Archive Closed Targets Now** in the same menu archives every closed target right away (one with an open sub-task stays), and **Undo Archive Now** brings back everything Archive Now archived, except what the setting archives anyway. Nothing is deleted. **Archive (K)** next to **Show done** (K = archived targets on this board) brings them back, dimmed with an **Archived** tag; to restore one for good, set its status back to an open one (status chip, or a drag in Kanban) and it is back on the board at once. The search field in the Board header narrows the board to targets whose title or intent contains the text, or to a number: `#163` finds target 163 only, a bare `163` also finds titles containing it; a match keeps the features it sits under and its own sub-tasks, finished and archived targets are searched too, and folded features open for it (Esc or ✕ clears the search). In Kanban the search keeps the matching cards and the cards under a matching feature. **Grouping:** drag a target onto another one in the list to put it under that target (its sub-tasks move with it), or right-click a card → **Move to** and pick the new parent, or **Move to Top Level** (a feature's right-click menu in the list also has **Open Group**, see **Entering a group**); a target can never go under itself or one of its own sub-tasks. Both the old and the new feature update their status and progress at once. The agent does the same: before filing a new target it looks for targets on the same topic and files it in their group, creating the group if there is none. Select a target and it opens in a panel on the right side of the board, over it, with no dimming: the board stays live, so a click on another card (or another row in the list) switches the panel to it. Drag the panel's left edge to make it wider or narrower (360 to 720 points, 460 by default); Watchtower remembers the width, one for all workbenches. At the top: **‹** (back, once you went from a target to its parent or a sub-task), its number (click it to copy `#163`), **Work on It**, the **⋯** menu (the same as right-clicking the card, minus **Work on It**: **Copy**, **Move to**, **Move to Top Level**) and ✕. Under it, a target inside a feature shows its parent as a link (the parent's number and title, with ›) that opens the parent in the same panel; **‹** goes back. Then the title (click it to rename, Return saves) and a row of chips — its status (**In Review** marks work whose review is under way) and priority are menus, and a further chip names any board drift found on it — then its progress, its branch and pull request when the agent linked them, and the description: longer ones are folded to six lines with **Show all**; click it to edit, ⌘↩ or clicking elsewhere saves, Esc cancels (a save that fails keeps your text). Below come its images, its **Asks** — every ask about this target, newest first, with its status; click one to open it in the ask drawer, as from **Waiting for you** (a review ask shows its document there) — and **Comments | History**: the comment threads, or every status change with who made it and when. The comment field is pinned at the bottom (Return starts a new line, ⌘↩ or ⌃↩ or the send button sends). A feature (a target with sub-tasks) opens in the same panel with a **GROUP** tag: its status is not a menu but reads "… · from sub-tasks" (it follows its tasks), with an "N of M done" bar over all the tasks under it and how many are in each status (dismissed ones are not counted in M; archived ones only with **Archive** on), **Work on It** with **Open group** (the square-arrow button) beside it (it shows only that feature on the board, see **Entering a group**), and a **Sub-tasks** tree: nested features fold with their arrow, done and dismissed sub-tasks fold into one "✓ N closed" row that opens in place, and a click on a sub-task opens it in the panel (**‹** comes back). Close the panel with its ✕ or Esc. Read or answer its comment threads there — agents use comments for a blocker and a short summary when a task is done; what they need from you comes as an ask (see **Asks**). Opening a target marks its agent comments read. A target the agent filed from a message where you shared a screenshot carries that image: its thumbnails show in the panel's Images section, and clicking one opens it full size (with **Show in Finder**). Watchtower keeps its own private copy of each image in its data folder, never in the workbench folder, so the original can be moved or deleted.

**Kanban** — the **List | Kanban** switch in the Board header lays the same targets out as columns: To Do, In Progress, In Review, Blocked and Done. Only tasks without sub-tasks become cards (a feature's status follows its tasks); a card shows its title in full and, below it, the features it sits under. **Lanes:** by default every top-level feature is a horizontal lane — a header with the arrow that folds the lane, its number, title, a done/total progress bar (the same count as the side panel's "N of M done": archived tasks only with **Archive** on) and its status, then the same columns holding only that feature's tasks (the line under a card names only the features below the lane's own). Click a lane's header to open the feature in the side panel; double-click it to enter the feature and close that panel again (see **Entering a group**). Top-level tasks without a feature share a **No group** lane at the bottom. The column titles and, per column, the number of cards over all the lanes shown (folded ones included) stay above the lanes. A lane with nothing left to show is hidden, unless its feature is still open and no search is active (it then says "No open tasks"). In each lane Done is folded to one "✓ N done — show" row that opens that lane's done cards until you leave the board; **Show done** or a search opens them all. Folded lanes are remembered for each workbench. The **Lanes** menu in the header switches between **By group** and **None** — the flat columns over the whole board. Within a column higher-priority cards come first; the Done column instead lists the most recently updated first. Drag a card to another column to change its status — the same change the status chip makes, so its feature moves with it; in lanes a card moves only between the columns of its own lane — dropping it into another lane says so; use **Move to…** to change its feature. With **Lanes: None** and **Show done** off the Done column shows the 10 most recently updated done cards plus how many more there are; with Show done on, Done shows everything and a Dismissed column appears. With **Archive** on, archived cards join the Done and Dismissed columns, never cut by the 10-card limit; in Kanban the K of **Archive (K)** counts only the archived cards, the ones the toggle adds (inside an entered feature, both views count only what is under it). A card with a status the board does not know lands in an **Other** column. Clicking a card opens it in the side panel, as in the list. Watchtower remembers the chosen view, the lanes setting and the feature you entered for each workbench.

**Entering a group** — to look at one feature alone, at any depth, click **Open group** in its side panel (the square-arrow button next to **Work on It**), double-click its lane header in Kanban, or right-click it in the list → **Open Group**. The board then shows only that feature: in Kanban its own tasks are the first lane (**Tasks**), each feature inside it gets a lane of its own, and the line under a card names only the features below the one you entered; the list shows the feature's sub-tasks as its top rows. A path bar under the Board header reads **Board › #249 Title › #252 Title**: click **Board** or any feature on the path to go back up to it. Next to it are the feature's "N of M" (done tasks over the tasks that count, as in the panel) and **✕ Leave group**, which goes up one level. Esc closes the side panel first; with the panel closed, Esc leaves one level. The feature you entered stays when you switch between List and Kanban, and the board remembers it for the workbench. A search inside it searches that feature only. If the feature is gone, has no sub-tasks any more, or is archived while **Archive** is off, the whole board shows instead. Opening an archived feature with **Archive** off says "This group is archived. Turn on Archive to open it."; one opened from a search drops back to the whole board when the search is cleared.

**Board drift** — when a target's status disagrees with its git branch or pull request, a line under the Board header says so: **Board drift: N out of step with git** (in orange) and, separately, findings that are only worth a look ("to review"). Click it for the list — each entry names the target, what was found (for example "branch feature/x is merged into main" while the target is still in progress, a done target whose branch has not been merged, a branch that no longer exists, or work in progress that has not moved for three days) — and click an entry to open that target. **Refresh** checks again; while the Board is open it also re-checks by itself every 30 seconds. If the check could not cover the board (the folder is not a git repository, or it ran out of time) the line says so instead of staying empty. The check only reads the folder's git state on your Mac (it never fetches, so a merge made on GitHub shows once the folder has fetched it). The agent sees the same list: at the end of each turn it is asked to bring the board in step with git before it finishes. A workbench set up before this check existed shows an orange install icon in the page header once; **Repair install** in the **…** menu adds it.

**Asks** — when the agent needs something from you — a review of a spec, plan or design it wrote, something to try by hand in the app, or a decision — it files an *ask* and keeps working on whatever does not depend on your answer. Every open ask of the workbench, from every session, waits oldest first in **Waiting for you (N)** at the top of the sessions panel: each row shows the kind (review, check or question), the title, what to do next (**Review doc**, **Answer 2 questions**, **Run 3 checks**), the session and how long ago it was filed. Each session row shows how many of its asks are open, and **▸ N closed** lists its past ones; asks filed from your own terminal app (outside Watchtower) are grouped as **Outside the app**. A new ask opens by itself in a drawer next to its session's terminal as soon as that session is on screen — or when you go to it — without taking the keyboard from wherever you are typing; with several open asks it shows the oldest one (**k of N**). Once you close it with **Later** or ×, it stays closed until the agent files another ask; meanwhile a banner over the terminal says **Agent asks: <title>** with **Open**.

Click an ask and Watchtower shows its session (it does not start it) and slides the ask out in a drawer next to that session's terminal, so you always see whose request it is; an ask from outside the app opens at the edge of the page. Drag the drawer's edge to make it wider or narrower (remembered); the expand button lets it cover the whole pane — the terminal keeps running underneath, and once you collapse the drawer a click in the terminal gives it the keyboard back. **k of N ›** in the drawer's header goes to the next open ask, switching session when needed. **Later** closes the drawer and keeps everything you entered. The drawer shows the agent's summary, the points it wants you to look at (click one to jump to it in the document), and, on a later round, what changed since the previous one (**Show diff** compares the two versions), then by kind:
- **Review** — the document as it was when the agent asked, in a readable column. Select a passage and click **Comment** to leave a note in the margin next to it (edit or **Remove** it there). Answer with **Request changes** or **Approve**.
- **Check** — the steps to try, each marked **Ok**, **Broken** (with a note on what broke — required) or **Skipped**; click a mark again to clear it. **Send** says how many steps are still unmarked; they go as skipped.
- **Question** — the same option cards as in the AI Chat: pick an option per question (or write your own) and click **Answer**.

Everything the agent writes in an ask — the summary, the points to look at, what changed, the questions and their options, the steps of a check and their hints — shows formatted: **bold**, *italic*, `code`, lists and line breaks, with clickable links. A web link (`http`, `https`) opens in your browser, and mail and Slack links open as they do elsewhere in Watchtower. A file of the workbench — a `path:line` such as `cmd/run.go:40`, or a link to a path such as `docs/plan.md` — opens in the Files pane at that line, beside the terminal (an expanded drawer steps back so you see it); a path that is not a file of the workbench folder does nothing but beep. Any other kind of link (a local file, a file share and the like) shows as plain text. A closed ask shows your note and your check notes formatted the same way. Text stays selectable for copying. Every field you type in — the **Note for the agent**, a step's note, your own answer to a question (**Other…**) and a margin comment — is a multi-line box about three lines tall from the start that grows as you write: Return starts a new line. What you type is kept in the draft as you type, as before; the answer itself goes with the buttons at the bottom.

Asks work from the keyboard. In the **Note for the agent**, a step's note or **Other…**, ⌘Return (or Control-Return) presses the ask's main button — **Answer**, **Send** or, on a review, **Approve** — and on a review ⌘⇧Return presses **Request changes**; a button that is greyed out (a question still unanswered, a broken step without its note, an answer already being sent) takes no key either, and the key never picks the verdict for you. In a margin comment ⌘Return leaves the comment (it is already kept as you type). With no field focused — after leaving a field, or with a passage of the review document selected — the same keys press the same buttons; each button's tooltip shows its key. While you type somewhere else — the terminal, the Files editor, a comment on a target — ⌘Return stays theirs.

Any ask may also carry questions, and every one has an optional **Note for the agent**. When something is still missing, the line above the buttons says what (for example "Answer every question"). When you answer, the answer is saved first; then one line — `Ask #12 answered (review: approved) — read it with get_ask 12 using the watchtower-workbench skill.` — is typed into that session's terminal and sent to Claude for you (if the agent is still working, Claude Code queues it for its next turn); the drawer closes, the terminal comes to the front with the cursor in it, and the ask says **Answer sent to Claude**. If Claude Code is waiting for a permission answer at that moment (or Watchtower cannot read the session's state just then), nothing is typed into the permission dialog: a highlighted line over the terminal says **Answer saved — it goes to Claude once the permission prompt is resolved**, and the answer is sent after the prompt is resolved (if you decline it or press Esc, that can take until your next message or about a minute; it is never sent on a timer — after a minute the line says **Still waiting for the permission prompt — Dismiss to send the answer through the session's brief instead**); **Dismiss** on that line cancels the sending, and the agent gets the answer in the session's next brief instead. If you answer a second ask of the same session while the first answer is being sent, the line says **Answer saved — sending after the previous answer** and it follows a moment later. Watchtower only types the line, without sending it, when Claude Code's input already holds unsent text — yours (also a line break you added with Shift+Return, Option+Return, `\` then Return or Ctrl+J) or an earlier line Watchtower typed there without sending (sending would send it all as one message) — when the session has not yet reported its state (for example a folder whose setup needs Re-run Setup), when Watchtower could not read the session's state, or when a permission prompt appears in the moment before sending; then the highlighted line says **Answer typed into the terminal — press Return to send**, and it goes away as soon as you type in that terminal — keys that answer a permission dialog don't count (or click **Dismiss**). When the terminal cannot take a pasted line, the line is copied to the clipboard instead and the highlighted line says **Answer copied — paste it into the terminal and press Return**; once you paste, it says to press Return. In a pane too narrow for the drawer beside the terminal, a new ask does not open by itself — use **Open** on the banner. If the session is not running, nothing is typed: the drawer says the answer goes to the session's brief, and the agent reads it when the session starts again. If the agent withdrew the ask, or replaced it with a newer round, while you were answering, nothing is saved or typed, your draft is kept and the drawer says so (**Discard draft** drops it). Drafts stay while you switch sessions, workbenches or tabs, and quitting Watchtower with unsent answers asks first. A closed ask opens read-only with your answer; a withdrawn one says "withdrawn by the agent" or "replaced by #N". In a workbench session Claude Code's own question dialog is turned off, and a check at the end of every agent turn sends a request written as plain text back to the agent to file as an ask — so **Waiting for you** is the one place to look. The same check reminds an agent that reports its work done to mark the session **Finished** with a short summary for you. While the agent works on after such a nudge, its session says "Stopped" until its first tool call — or **Waiting for you** as soon as it files the ask, **Finished** once it marks the session finished.

**Search** — the agent working in this workbench can search every `.md`, `.markdown` and `.txt` file of the folder (Watchtower's knowledge search) — nothing to attach. Files `.gitignore` ignores and the `.git`, `.build` and `node_modules` folders are left out, and up to 2000 of the most recently changed files are included. The files are indexed when you create the workbench and whenever you use Re-run Setup, and for a folder outside Documents, Desktop, Downloads and cloud storage also whenever a file changes. They are never searchable from the AI Chat, the Discuss chats or another workbench.

**Files** — look at and quickly fix the files of the workbench folder without leaving Watchtower. At the bottom of the sessions panel, **FILES** opens the folder as a tree (collapsed to one row until you click it; drag its top edge to make it taller). Folders open on click; `.git`, build output and `node_modules` are not listed. A single click on a file opens it in the **Files** pane as a *preview* tab (its name in italics) that the next single click replaces, so browsing does not pile up tabs; a double click on the file or on the tab, or your first edit, keeps the tab open. Tabs show the file name (with its folder when two open files share a name); drag them to reorder, and right-click for **Close**, **Close Others**, **Close All**, **Keep Open**, **Reveal in Finder** and **Copy Relative Path**. Watchtower remembers each workbench's open tabs. The editor highlights the syntax of most languages and config files (including `.env`, Makefiles, TOML, `.gitignore`, `go.mod`, patches, logs, and CSV with a color per column) and follows the light or dark appearance; each tab keeps its own undo, cursor and scroll position, also after a rename. **Your edits save themselves** a second after you stop typing, and at once when you switch tabs, leave the editor or Watchtower, close the tab or press ⌘S; the jump bar above the editor says **Edited** next to the file name until it is on disk, then **Saved**. When Claude changes a file you have open, it reloads by itself. Your edits are never written over a version you have not seen: if Claude changed the file while you were typing, deleted it, or made it unreadable, saving stops and a banner offers **Reload from disk** (take the version on disk) or **Keep mine** (write yours over it; **Write it back** for a deleted file). Closing a tab whose edits could not be saved lists the reason and asks before discarding them. **Create, rename, delete:** the **+** in the FILES header (or right-click a file or folder) offers **New File…** and **New Folder…** — type the name right in the tree (a path such as `internal/foo/bar.go` creates the missing folders; Return creates, Esc cancels) and a new file opens in a tab. **Rename…** edits the name in place (a path moves it); tabs of a renamed file or folder follow. **Move to Trash** asks first and puts the file or folder in the macOS Trash, where you can restore it; its tabs close. Files with changes that are not committed are marked in the tree and on the tabs: the name is colored and a letter says how — **M** modified, **A** added, **U** new (untracked), **R** renamed, **D** deleted, **!** merge conflict — and a folder holding such a file shows a dot, even when it is folded. The marks follow your commits and checkouts; a folder that is not a git repository has none, and if git fails the FILES header says the marks may be out of date. Files larger than 5 MB or that are not text are not opened (the arrow button opens a file in its default app).

**Open Quickly** — with a workbench page on screen, press Shift twice quickly (or ⇧⌘O, or **Navigate → Open Quickly…**) to find a file, a symbol or a line of text in the workbench folder; ⇧⌘F (**Find in Workbench…**) opens it on text. Type part of a name — the letters need not be next to each other (`cfbuf` finds `CodeFileBuffer`), and a `/` searches folder paths. The scope buttons pick **All**, **Files**, **Symbols** or **Text**; switching keeps what you typed. **All** shows the best match, then up to 8 symbols, 8 files and 20 text matches (**more…** lists every text match). Files open in a tab, files you opened recently and files with uncommitted changes come first. Each row shows a coloured kind badge (**M** method, **F** function, **C** class, **S** struct, **E** enum, **P** protocol or interface, **T** type alias, **K** constant, variable or field, **N** module) or the file's icon, with the matched letters in bold; the right side previews the selected symbol (its place, its doc comment and its first 12 lines) or the lines around a text match, in the editor's colours. ↑ and ↓ move, Return opens the file in a preview tab with the cursor on the line, ⌥Return opens it beside the pane on screen (splitting the page), and Esc or a click outside closes it and puts you back where you were. While you move through the rows with the arrows, Space shows the file in Quick Look (Space again hides it). ⌘Return, or the last row **✦ Ask AI: "…"**, asks the assistant what you typed: the answer appears right in the panel under **✦ AI answer** (about the file open in the Files pane at the cursor's line, or about the folder when no file is open). Type a follow-up and press Return to continue; a `path:line` reference opens that file and closes the panel; Esc closes it and the conversation stays in **Questions**. The footer says when the folder is still being indexed or the index failed. Symbols come from the folder's own index, built when the Files pane, FILES or Open Quickly first shows the workbench and kept up to date as files change.

**Go to definition** — in the Files pane, hold ⌘ over a name to underline it and ⌘-click it (or put the cursor on it and press ⌃⌘J, **Navigate → Go to Definition**) to jump to where it is defined. One definition opens in a kept tab with the cursor on the name; several show a menu at the word (**name — N definitions**, each with its kind badge, `Type.name` and `path:line`, and **Show All Usages…** last). Definitions in the same file come first, then the same folder, then the same top-level folder. In a file the index reads no definitions from — a language it does not read, or one it lists only for headings or keys (Markdown, HTML, CSS, YAML, JSON and the like) — a whole-word text search looks for lines like `func name`, `def name` or `class name` first, then any other mention; when nothing is found (in an indexed language: when the index has no definition) the Mac beeps and **No definition of `name`** shows at the end of the jump bar for 2 seconds (**Still indexing…** while the folder is still being indexed). ⌃⌘← (**Navigate → Back**) returns to the exact line and column you jumped from, ⌃⌘→ (**Forward**) goes again; the history keeps the last 100 places per workbench and outlives switching away.

**Jump bar** — the line above the editor: **‹ ›** (Back and Forward, greyed out when there is nowhere to go), then where the cursor is — the workbench folder › each folder › the file (with its git mark and **Saved**/**Edited**) › the type › the method, the type and method with their kind badges. Click a segment for a menu of its neighbours: a folder lists its folders (opening as submenus) and files, the file its sibling files, a type the other types beside it, and a symbol in the middle the other members of its type. The last segment lists every symbol of the file, nested ones indented, under a **Filter** field — type part of a name to narrow it and Return to open the first one; ⌃6 (**Navigate → File Symbols…**) opens the same list from the keyboard (the Mac beeps when the file has no symbols). Markdown headings and config keys are in that list; in a Markdown file the nearest heading is the last segment. Picking an entry opens it, and ⌃⌘← returns. At the right end, in grey: **Language X: text search** for a file the index reads no definitions from — a language it does not read, or Markdown, HTML, CSS, YAML, JSON and the like (X is its extension), **Indexing…** for a file the first indexing has not reached yet, or the index's error when indexing failed; the arrow button opens the file in its default app.

**Your own languages** — for a language the index does not read, you can teach it where definitions are: put a file `code-languages.yaml` in `~/Library/Application Support/Watchtower/`. Each language names its file extensions (or exact file names) and one or more patterns; the part of a line in the first parentheses is the name, and `kind` is one of `function`, `method`, `class`, `struct`, `enum`, `protocol`, `interface`, `type`, `const`, `var`, `field`, `module` or `macro`. For example, Tcl procedures and namespaces:

```yaml
tcl:
  extensions: [.tcl]
  definitions:
    - kind: function
      pattern: '^\s*proc\s+([A-Za-z_][\w:]*)'
    - kind: module
      pattern: '^\s*namespace\s+eval\s+(\w+)'
```

Such files then get symbols in Open Quickly, go to definition and the jump bar. The rules never override a language Watchtower already knows. Saving the file re-indexes the workbenches on screen. If anything in it is wrong (invalid YAML, a bad pattern, an unknown kind, an extension that is already known), the whole file is ignored and the jump bar says **Rules file:** and the problem at its right end on every file (hover for the full message) until you fix it.

**Usages** — put the cursor on a name and press ⇧⌘U (**Navigate → Show All Usages**), right-click it and choose **Show All Usages**, or pick **Show All Usages…** in the definition menu. The inspector opens on the right of the Files pane on its **Usages** tab: **Usages — name · N**, then one group per file in the order they are found, each with a disclosure triangle and its count, and rows `line  text` with the name in bold. The list fills while the search runs; a collapsed group stays collapsed as more matches arrive. It is a whole-word, case-sensitive text search of the folder, so same-named symbols are listed too. A click opens the file in a preview tab with the cursor on the name; ⌃⌘← (**Navigate → Back**) returns to where you were. A new name replaces the list and stops the search before it; closing the Files pane stops the search and keeps what it found. The button at the right end of the tab strip shows and hides the inspector.

**Questions** — the inspector's **Questions** tab lists this workbench's AI questions about code, newest first: the first question, the `path:line` it was asked from (or **No file open**) and when. Click one to continue it in the inspector, with the same provider and model menu; ‹ goes back to the list. **Delete** (right-click a row, or the trash button in a conversation) removes the conversation and its answers; deleting the workbench removes all of them. These conversations never appear in the main AI Chat. Codex and Ollama follow-ups (and a Claude one after you switch the model) get the conversation so far, so you can keep asking whichever model answers.

**Ask AI** — select some code in the Files pane and, half a second after you stop, a small orange ✦ appears at the right of the selection (it hides while you type, scroll or clear the selection). Click it, press ⌘I, right-click and choose **Ask AI**, or pick **Navigate → Ask AI**; with nothing selected the question is about the cursor's line. A popover opens at the selection: **Explain**, **Find problems**, **Where is it used?** and **Suggest a change** send a ready question, or type your own; the provider and model menu at the top picks who answers (the default is preselected, and the choice stays with the conversation). The answer streams below with its code coloured; `path:line` references open the file in the Files pane. Ask follow-ups in **Ask a follow-up…**; **Copy** copies the latest answer. The assistant answers from the code around your question and the definitions the index knows — for **Where is it used?**, also from Watchtower's own whole-word search of the folder for the selected name (or the name at the cursor), up to 30 places — and, with Claude or Codex, reads other files of the workbench folder with read-only tools when it needs them (Ollama cannot, and says so once); it never changes your files. Those tools can read outside the folder too, so macOS may ask whether Watchtower may access a protected folder such as Desktop or Documents. After **Suggest a change**, the editor shows the change over the selection — the old text struck through, the new text under it — and **Apply** makes it one edit in the editor (⌘Z takes it back), saved as usual; **Discard** removes it. Apply refuses, with a note, while the file has a conflict with its version on disk or when the selected text changed since you asked. **Pin to inspector** moves the conversation to the **Questions** tab, where the answer keeps going. Esc or a click outside closes the popover and its suggestion; the answer keeps going and the conversation is kept in **Questions**.

**Hand to Claude Code** — when a question turns into a task, press ⌥⌘↩ (or click **Hand to Claude Code**) in the Ask AI popover, in a conversation on the **Questions** tab, or in Open Quickly on what you typed. A sheet shows the text that will be sent — a first line "From a Watchtower code question:", where it was asked (`path:line`), each question with its finished answer, and the `path:line` references the answers cite; never the code of the file or the selection — and asks where to send it: one of the workbench's running Claude Code sessions (one waiting for a permission answer is shown but cannot be picked) or **New session**. **Send** confirms: a running session idle at its prompt (stopped, finished, stopped on an error or waiting for your answer to an ask) gets the text pasted into its prompt and submitted; a session that is busy working gets the text pasted without Return, and its terminal shows "Pasted into Claude — press Return to send"; if the session's prompt still holds something not sent — an answer to an ask typed in but not submitted, or text you typed — the hand-off is also only pasted, so the two are never sent by accident, and the terminal shows "Pasted into Claude next to text not sent yet — press Return to send them together" (if the session has not turned on bracketed paste, the text goes to the clipboard and the pane asks you to paste it with ⌘V); Cancel is greyed out while sending; a new session starts with it as its first message. The session opens beside the editor, splitting the page. The button is greyed out while an answer is still coming.

**Notifications** — Watchtower tells you when an agent asks something in a target comment, when it files an ask (**Agent asks: <title>**), when an agent finishes a target, and when it proposes something only you can approve. Several at once in one workbench arrive as one summary (**Waiting for you (N)** for asks). Clicking a single ask's notice opens that ask next to its session; the combined **Waiting for you (N)** notice and the other notices open the Board. While Watchtower is in the background, a running session that stops, needs approval, hits an error or finishes sends one notice ("<session> stopped" / "<session> needs approval", with the workbench name; "<session> hit an error", with the error; "<session> finished", with how many asks wait for you while any are open, otherwise the first line of the agent's summary); a session waiting on an ask sends none of its own, as the ask already sent one. Clicking it opens that session. A newer state of the same session replaces its notice, and the notice goes away once the session is working again or is no longer running. Settings → Notifications has the switch, **Workbench notifications** (on by default).

**Workbench header** — one slim row above the workspace: breadcrumbs — the folder path (shortened to `~/…` inside your home folder and cut in the middle when the row is tight; click it to reveal the folder in Finder, hover for the full path) and, when the folder is a git repository, `›` and the branch button (see **Branch**) — a small install icon (a grey seal = installed; an orange circle = the folder install is incomplete; an orange triangle = the Claude Code CLI was not found — click it to copy the command that connects the workbench by hand; hover any of them for details; a folder set up before the rename to Workbench shows the orange icon with "Set up by an older Watchtower — Re-run Setup to update"), the **Terminal | Session | Board | Files** buttons and the split button (see Layout), and the **…** menu with **Repair install** (enabled only when something is missing), **Re-run Setup**, **Archive Closed Targets After** (Never, 3, 7, 14, 30 or 90 days, 14 by default: how long a closed target stays on the Board before it is archived; a change applies at once, both ways), **Archive Closed Targets Now** (archives every closed target on the Board right away instead of waiting — done and dismissed alike, also when the setting is Never; nothing is deleted, the targets show again under **Archive (K)** and come back when reopened; work closed after the click stays until the usual period passes or you click again), **Undo Archive Now** (shown after a click: everything Archive Now archived comes back, earlier clicks included, except targets the age setting would archive anyway) and **Delete…**. The install icon's tooltip lists each part of the folder install as on or missing — the skill, the hook, the drift hook, the state hooks, the ask guard and the MCP connection — and **Repair install** adds what is missing. An install error shows as one line under the row only while there is one.

**Branch** — the button after the folder path shows the folder's current git branch: the branch icon and its name in bold (a long name is cut with `…`; hover for the full name), an orange dot when there are uncommitted changes (edited, staged or new files — ignored files do not count), `↑2`/`↓1` when the branch is ahead of or behind its upstream (nothing when level), and a chevron. On a detached HEAD it shows the short commit hash in grey. An orange question mark means a switch is waiting for your confirmation; an orange triangle means the last reads failed and what you see may be out of date (hover says why). The tooltip also names the upstream, how many changes are uncommitted and a merge, rebase or similar in progress. When the status could not be read at all and none is known yet, only an orange triangle shows in its place (hover for the reason). The button is not there for a folder that is not a git repository, nor on a Mac without git (the developer tools or Homebrew's git) — Watchtower never asks you to install anything for it. Click it for the branch popover: a search field (type part of a name), **Local branches** with the most recently committed first — a checkmark and highlight on the current one, when each was last committed, the board's `#163` badge on a branch a target names (`#163 +1` for more; hover lists them), "upstream gone" in orange when its remote branch was deleted, and a greyed-out row captioned "open in worktree …" for a branch checked out in another git worktree (git does not allow it twice); **New branch from current…** (type a name, **Create** — it starts from the current commit and takes your uncommitted changes along); and **Copy branch name** (the hash on a detached HEAD). Click a branch to switch to it. A switch first saves what you typed in the Files pane (so it counts as an uncommitted change); if an open file holds edits that cannot be saved — it changed or was deleted on disk while you were editing, or can no longer be read — the popover says "Save or discard the edits in <file> first — they are not on disk yet." and nothing is switched. If the editor does not hand over what you just typed, the popover says "The editor did not hand over its latest edits — try again." and nothing is switched either. If the folder has uncommitted changes, a dialog asks first: **Stash and switch** saves every change, new files included, in a git stash entry named `watchtower: switching from <current> to <branch> [<random tag>]`, then switches; **Cancel** leaves everything as it is. If a Claude Code session is running in Watchtower's terminal anywhere in the repository's folder (this workbench's session, or any embedded terminal at the repository root or below it), the dialog says the agent's files would be swapped under it and asks before **Switch anyway**; both questions can come in one dialog. Nothing is switched, stashed or written before you confirm. A switch is refused, with the reason in the popover, for a branch checked out in another worktree and while a merge, rebase, cherry-pick, revert or bisect is in progress (finish or abort it first). After a stashed switch the popover names the stash entry and the command that brings your changes back: `git stash apply <sha>`; the note stays — through closing and reopening the popover, with the button's tooltip saying "Stashed changes: …" meanwhile — until you dismiss it with its × (the entry itself stays on the stack), a later stashed switch replaces it, or Watchtower quits. After a restart, find the entry in `git stash list` by its `watchtower: switching from …` message and apply it by its id. Use exactly that — apply by its id, never `git stash pop`: the stash list is shared by every worktree and every session on that repository, and the top entry may be someone else's. Watchtower never re-applies the stash after a successful switch, and never pops or deletes it; if a switch fails after stashing, it puts your changes straight back and keeps the entry too (if that fails, a red line gives the same apply command). A git hook that fails after the branch changed shows git's words as a note, and the switch counts as done. The button follows the folder by itself: when the page opens, when you come back to Watchtower, right after a switch, whenever the branch or a ref changes (even from your own terminal or another worktree), and every 15 seconds while the page is on screen, so the dirty dot keeps up with edits. Limits: local branches only — no fetch, pull, push, delete or rename; a `claude` running in your own terminal app (outside Watchtower) is not detected, so check it yourself before switching.

**Re-run Setup** — in the page header's **…** menu. Brings a workbench created by an older Watchtower up to the current setup — including one set up while this tab was still called Projects, which keeps working until you do this: it re-installs whatever is missing or out of date in the folder (the skill — unless you edited your copy, which is kept — and the hooks, including the ask guard and the session state hooks that make a session show working / agents working / stopped / needs approval; a workbench without them shows the orange install icon in the page header, its tooltip says "state hooks missing", and **Repair install** in the **…** menu adds them) and registers the MCP connection afresh (for a folder set up under the old name it replaces the old `watchtower-project` skill — unless you edited it, which is kept and named in the summary — and its connection; Claude Code then asks once to allow each tool under the new name), then refreshes the search index of the folder's text files. It only ever adds: the board, its statuses, comments, asks, sources and the description stay exactly as they are, and it never creates targets. A summary under the header says what was added or what failed, and suggests what to ask the agent next (for example to propose a first board); dismiss it with ×.

**Delete…** — in the page header's **…** menu. The confirmation lists what goes: the board's targets, the asks and the comments, the AI questions about its code, Watchtower's copies of the targets' images, plus what Watchtower installed in the folder (the skill, the hooks, the MCP connection and its git-exclude lines). The files in the folder stay. The workbench's terminal is closed first. If the delete fails, the workbench stays and the reason is shown.

Workbench targets appear only on their board — never in Targets, the Day Plan, next steps or Catch Up. The daily briefing has its own mention of workbench activity.

### AI Chat
The main assistant: ask about your work, keep long conversations organized, attach files, get documents back, and have it do things — every change to the outside world waits for your Approve.

**Opening the Chat tab** — you land on a start page: a "What can I help with?" composer front and centre, the starter prompts, and a short list of your pinned and most recent chats to jump back into (chats you opened but never wrote in are left out). If you were in a conversation less than two hours ago — counting both its last message and the last time you had it on screen (while the Chat tab is open and the app is in front, Watchtower notes that about once a minute, and again when you leave the tab, switch chats, switch to another app or quit) — the tab reopens straight into it instead. A conversation whose reply is still being written, or where you left a half-typed message or an attached file unsent, always reopens, however long you have been away. This also works across an app restart, per workspace. Typing your first character on the start page already starts warming up the assistant, so the first reply comes fast; you stay on the start page until you send. If you leave the start page without sending — opening another chat, a search hit or a project, or quitting — that unused new chat is thrown away, so nothing is left behind as an empty "New Chat". Only that chat is ever thrown away: it is kept if you pinned or renamed it, and if you had attached a file it stays in your history with the file waiting, ready to pick up where you left off. Unsent files always stay with the chat you attached them to; they never ride along into another chat. **New chat** (⌘N, the **New chat** button in the chat toolbar, or the **New chat** row at the top of the chat history column) brings you back to the start page.

**Layout** — By default the tab has just the conversation (or the start page) next to the app's own sidebar. Your chat history is a column you open with the sidebar button in the chat toolbar; Watchtower remembers whether you keep it open or closed. In it your chats are grouped Pinned / Today / Yesterday / Previous 7 days / Previous 30 days / Older, with a **New chat** row at the top and your Projects below it (**+** next to the PROJECTS label starts a new one). The column is styled like the Workbench sessions panel: small uppercase group labels, and the chat — or project page — on screen is highlighted as a tab that runs into the conversation beside it; other rows light up faintly under the pointer. Click a chat to open it; once you have, ↑/↓ move through the list. Chats with neither a message nor an unsent file are not listed unless they are the one on screen. Right-click a chat to rename, pin, move it to a project, archive or delete it (archiving or deleting the chat on screen takes you to the start page, and so does deleting the project page you are on); double-click the title in the toolbar to rename. ⌘K searches the text of every message and jumps to the hit, with the history column open or closed. A chat gets a short title automatically after its first reply — a title you set yourself is never replaced.

**Answers show their work** — while the assistant works you see each step live ("Searched knowledge: payments rollout", "Opened PROJ-123"); when it is done the steps fold into "Worked for 12s · 4 steps" (click to expand the arguments and results; a failed step is marked red). Once the answer is finished, one compact line below it sums up what it used — a few source-type icons, "12 sources", the busiest groups ("#payments ×6, PAY ×2") and a chevron. Click it to open the **Sources** panel on the right: the answer's sources, duplicates merged (several hits in one Slack thread are one item), grouped by Slack channel, Jira project, Confluence space, Mail, Meetings and Other, each group collapsible with its count; every item shows its title, a short excerpt and a date, and opens its Slack thread, Jira issue, email or page when it has a link (items without one are listed but not clickable). The Sources panel shares the right side with the artifact panel — when both are open, an Artifact | Sources switch at the top flips between them without closing either. Topical questions search everything Watchtower has synced and indexed (Slack, mail, Jira with comments, calendar, meeting transcripts and recaps, digests, decisions and ideas) — controlled by the Knowledge search feature toggle.

**Streaming and control** — text and formatting appear as they are written: tables, lists, quotes, and code blocks with a language label and Copy. Enter sends, Shift+Enter adds a line, Esc stops, ↑ in an empty composer edits your last message. Stop keeps what was already written ("Stopped" + Continue); an error shows a card with Retry and your message is never lost; the card also shows the provider's own reason under the summary (e.g. which attachment was refused, or that no Ollama model is set), and a sign-in error names the command for the chat's provider. Hover a reply for Copy, Regenerate and its model/time; hover your own message for Copy and Edit. Regenerating or editing keeps every version — switch between them with ‹ 1/2 ›.

**Provider and model** — the pill in the composer picks Claude, Codex or Ollama and the model (**Auto** = the provider's configured strong model; suggestions come from `watchtower ai models`, nothing is hardcoded). Claude gets the full experience: live token streaming, visible steps, images and PDFs. Codex and Ollama answer in the same chat but may show the reply all at once, may not show steps, and cannot read images or PDFs. Recently used chats stay warm (up to three, for ten minutes) so the next reply starts fast.

**Attachments** — paperclip, drag & drop, or paste an image. Images (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP) up to 5 MB, PDFs up to 32 MB, and text-like files (txt, md, csv, json, yaml, logs, source code) up to 256 KB; anything else is refused in the composer with the reason. Files are kept privately in the workspace folder and deleted with their chat.

**Artifacts** — anything you'll copy, send or keep (a document, a table, an email or Slack draft, an event, code) opens in the artifact panel on the right instead of cluttering the chat. Each change is a new version you can switch between; you can edit it (your edit becomes a version too), copy it, or export it (.md / .csv / .txt). Drafts open ready in their app but are **never sent** by Watchtower: an email opens in Gmail compose, a Slack draft is copied and its channel or thread is opened, an event opens Google Calendar's new-event page.

**Commenting on an artifact** — the artifact panel shows the latest version the same way whether you are reading or commenting: markdown rendered with its headings, lists and tables, a table artifact as a table, code as a code block. Select a passage and click the **Comment** button that appears next to it (or right-click → **Comment…**) to leave a comment — the note field opens with the cursor in it, Return starts a new line, ⌘↩ or ⌃↩ saves (the popover's **Comment** button stays greyed out until you type something) — and the text stays where you were reading. Like every popover in Watchtower, the note's popover is the system's translucent popover with no backing of its own. Comments are highlighted in the text and listed beside it (below it when the panel is narrow); the **Comments (N)** button in the panel's header shows or hides that list, and with it hidden a click on a highlight opens the list on that comment. A new comment stays a private draft until you click **Send N comments**, which sends every draft comment as one message to the assistant. The assistant answers with a new version of the same artifact, and each comment follows its passage there; a passage the new version drops instead marks the comment **Outdated** rather than attaching it to the wrong place. Nothing is sent one comment at a time.

**Quote in reply** — on a finished answer, quote a passage to open it in a sheet, select the part you mean, and optionally add a note; it joins a batch shown above the composer. Quote as many passages as you like — nothing is sent until you send your own message, which carries every quote plus whatever you typed as one turn.

**Question cards** — when your request can reasonably be read two ways, the assistant may ask instead of guessing. The question appears as a card with 1–4 questions. Each question has 2–4 options with a short explanation; one may be marked **Recommended**, and some questions let you pick several. Every question also has an **Other** field for your own answer: Return starts a new line in it, and ⌘Return sends the answers once every question has one (the lines of your own answer go out joined into one). Questions and option explanations show formatted, with clickable links. Pick your answers and press **Send answers**; they go out as your next message. Once answered, the card shows what you chose, also after a restart. A card can only be answered on the latest reply. Question cards appear in this chat and in every assistant chat listed below.

**Assistant chats elsewhere in the app** look and behave like this one: the target's Discuss tab, the track chat, an idea's or decision's Discuss section, a recording's Chat tab, and the Setup Assistant in Settings → Add account (calendar or email). Each one works the same way:
- Replies stream in, and **Stop** keeps what was written so far.
- Every message has **Copy**.
- A failed reply shows what went wrong (with a sign-in hint when the provider is logged out) and a **Retry** button.
- An empty chat suggests a few starter questions.

These chats have no attachments, @-mentions, branches or edits. Those stay in the AI Chat.

A reply keeps going when you leave the screen or collapse the section; come back and it is there. At most three of these replies run at once. A fourth waits, marked **Queued**, and Stop takes it back into the input. In the target, track, idea/decision and recording chats, a queued message you never sent also comes back as a draft after a restart. The Setup Assistant keeps nothing once its window closes.

**Projects** — group chats that share context. A project has instructions (given to the assistant in every chat of the project), files (text files are read in full; images and PDFs are shown to Claude at the start of each session, as far as they fit in one message — 30 MB together with the files you attach yourself), and pinned sources — Jira projects, Slack channels, Confluence spaces, targets, tracks and people — that tell it where to look first. Pinned Slack channels, Jira projects and Confluence spaces also steer the assistant's knowledge search: in the project's chats their messages, issues and pages come first. Start a chat from the project page with "New chat in this project", or move an existing chat into a project. Changes to a project's instructions, sources or files reach every chat in it on that chat's next message (a reply still being written finishes first). Deleting a project deletes its files and keeps its chats.

**@-mentions and / skills** — type `@` to point at a person, Slack channel, Jira issue, target or track from your synced data (↑/↓ and Enter to pick, Esc to close); the pick appears as a chip and the assistant knows exactly which one you meant. Type `/` at the start of a message to pick one of your enabled skills; the assistant loads it before answering.

**Write tools (proposals):** the assistant can propose changes as well as answer — `create_target` (a task), `create_idea`, `create_track`, `remind_me`, `connect_jira_board`, `create_jira_issue`, and on existing Jira issues `add_jira_comment`, `transition_jira_issue` (move to a status), `assign_jira_issue` and `update_jira_issue` (summary, priority, labels, due date), and `edit_confluence_page` (see below). A write tool never does anything by itself: calling it records a proposal and shows a card in the chat saying what it wants to do and why. Most wait for your Approve — Reject discards the proposal, and a failed action can be retried from the same card; a done card links to what was created or changed. Two tools, `create_idea` and `remind_me`, run immediately by default and just show what they did (no click needed) — everything that writes outside this Mac, all the Jira tools included, always waits for your Approve regardless of the switch. The **Assistant tools** card in Settings → Profile lists every write tool with an "Execute without approval" switch so you can flip either behavior per tool. Proposals left over from an interrupted turn are gathered at the bottom of the chat, so nothing is stranded without a decision. The target chat's Discuss tab works the same way, alongside its own task-edit cards.

**Writing to Slack from the chat:** in the main AI Chat, ask the assistant to write something in Slack — "tell #ops the deploy is done", "reply in this thread: <link>", "DM Alice that I'll be late". It first reads your writing style (the one the Profile tab builds from your own messages: language, tone per audience, typical phrases) and drafts the message the way you would write it, then shows a **Send to Slack** card: who it goes to ("To: #ops in Acme", "To: a thread in #ops in Acme", "To: @Alice in Acme") and the text in an editable box. Change the text if you like ("Edited — Approve sends your version"), then **Approve & send**: the message is posted as you, from your own Slack account. Nothing is sent before you approve, and the assistant can never be set to send on its own. Only channels and people Watchtower has synced can be addressed. When the same channel exists in more than one of your workspaces, the card shows a **Send from** menu — Approve stays off until you pick one. A sent message's card links to it in Slack. If a send fails, **Retry** first checks whether the message reached Slack after all and posts it only if it did not. Sending needs a permission Slack workspaces connected before October 2026 did not grant: the first send then fails with "sign in again to grant send" and the card offers **Reconnect Slack** (the same as Settings → Connections → Slack → **Reconnect**); approve Slack's page in the browser, then press **Retry**. The same tool works from a workbench's Claude Code terminal: there it only proposes — the card waits in the Inbox, and a notification ("Send to Slack awaits your approval") takes you there.

**Editing Confluence pages from the chat:** in the main AI Chat and a target's chat, the assistant can read a Confluence page live (by its link, its page id, or its exact title — when a title matches several pages it lists them and asks which one) and propose an edit to it: replace a passage, or rewrite a whole section. The proposal card shows the page with a link, then each change — where it is ("text in <section>" or "Section: <heading>") and a word-by-word diff, removed words struck through in red and added words in green — plus a "Removes: …" line when the change drops a rich element such as an @mention. If the user names on the page could not be looked up, the card says "User names unavailable — mentions show account ids". Nothing is written until you **Approve** (a proposal the card cannot read offers only Reject). The page is saved only if nobody edited it since the preview — same version and same content: otherwise the card turns Failed with "the page was edited after the preview … nothing was written", and a Retry cannot write twice for the same reason (ask the assistant for a fresh edit instead). If a Retry finds this very edit already on the page (its first save went through but the answer was lost), it writes nothing again and the card turns saved ("Saved as version N"). An archived page can be read but not edited: restore it in Confluence first. If the page is exactly one version past the preview but its content does not match this edit, the card says the edit "may have been saved" and asks for a fresh read of the page before trying again. A saved edit shows "Saved as version N" and appears in the page history as "Edited via Watchtower". Macros, tables, mentions and every part of the page the edit does not change stay byte-for-byte as they were — a section rewrite keeps every block it leaves unchanged exactly as it was, formatting included, even when it only moves the block to another place in the section, and refuses to change a block whose formatting a chat edit cannot carry (an aligned paragraph, a table with column widths or a layout, a code block with a title, a noformat block) — also when the edit moves such a block and changes it, or deletes it while changing another block of the same kind anywhere in the section, since the text alone cannot tell which of the two was edited: keep such a block as it is, or change it in Confluence yourself. Deleting such a block outright, with no other block of its kind changed, is allowed and shows in the diff. Editing needs the site's **Allow editing** permission (Settings → Connections → Jira → Confluence); without it the assistant tells you to allow it. The button is part of the Confluence section, which only shows while Confluence sync (the Knowledge connectors feature) is on; with sync off, run the command the assistant names instead (`watchtower jira login --account N --with-confluence-write`).

The Discuss chats (target, idea, meeting, track) share the new message rendering; they keep their own history and behavior.

### Tracks
Auto-generated narrative summaries of ongoing initiatives and projects across your Slack workspace. Tracks are created and updated automatically by the AI pipeline — they group related digest topics into living stories that evolve over time.

**How tracks work:** After each sync, the tracks pipeline collects unlinked topics and decisions from recent digests, compares them against existing tracks, and either creates new tracks or updates existing ones. Each track tells a narrative story about an initiative — who's involved, what happened, what the current status is.

**Updates:** When a track is updated after you've read it, it appears in the "Updates" section at the top of the list with an orange badge. Opening a track automatically marks it as read and cascades read status to its linked digests.

**Priority:** High (red), Medium (orange), Low (blue). Click the priority badge on a track to change it.

Each track shows:
- **Current Status** — highlighted card summarizing the latest state
- **Narrative** — full story of the initiative
- **Timeline** — chronological list of events with dates and channels
- **Participants** — people involved with role badges (driver=green, reviewer=blue, blocker=red)
- **Key Messages** — quoted Slack messages with authors, timestamps, and links to open in Slack
- **Related Digests** — expandable section showing linked digests via source references. Each digest can be expanded (DisclosureGroup) to see its summary, decisions, and feedback buttons
- **Tags** — topic tags for categorization
- **Channels** — which Slack channels the track spans (clickable deep links)

**Actions:** Change priority, rate quality with thumbs up/down, chat with AI about a specific track.

**Dismissing in bulk:** the checklist button in the filter row opens **Select tracks** — rows get a checkbox, a bar shows how many are selected with **Select all** (every track the list currently shows) and **Dismiss selected** — and **Dismiss all auto tracks…**, which dismisses every track the pipeline created (your custom tracks stay). Both ask to confirm with the exact count first. Dismiss is soft: dismissed tracks leave the list, the archive button shows them again, and **Restore** on any one brings it back. You can also ask the main AI Chat ("dismiss every track except the newest one", "how many auto tracks haven't moved in a month?"): it counts tracks without listing them, and a bulk dismiss always arrives as one card stating the count and a few sample titles — nothing is dismissed until you **Approve**, and Approve dismisses exactly the tracks the card counted.

### Digests
A cross-source overview of what happened, plus the settled decisions ledger. Two sub-views:

**Digests** — One chronological feed, grouped by day, spanning every source: Slack channel/daily/weekly digests (summary, extracted topics, ongoing topics with status; rate quality with 👍/👎), Gmail and Jira stream digests (scope/period, topic titles, and the ideas/decisions each one mined, with deep links back to the Gmail thread or the Jira issue), and meeting recaps (any recording that has a recap; opens straight into the recording). Rows show title, a glyph per source it's been mentioned in, relative time, and an unread dot; opening a row marks it seen. Detail shows the essence, status (**active** / **superseded** / **reversed**), **Supersede**/**Reverse** actions, a 👍/👎 rating with an optional comment, the full mentions chronology with deep links back to each source, and a collapsed Discuss chat. "Mark all read" clears the segment's unread count in one click.

## Menu Bar & App Lifecycle

Watchtower is a menu-bar app first: it keeps syncing in the background even when its window is closed.

**Menu bar icon** — always present while Watchtower is running, whether or not the window is open. Click it for a status line and the tray's actions.

The status line says what the daemon is actually doing, not merely whether it is alive: "Syncing: Messages · 34/105 channels" while a sync runs (the phase and its counter, refreshed every few seconds), "Daemon running · idle" between syncs, or "Sync daemon not running" when there is no daemon at all. A daemon or CLI-install error, if either happened, is shown underneath.

Actions: **"Update to <version> available…"** (only shown once a new version has been found; opens Settings → System, where it downloads and installs), **"Sync Now"** (asks the running daemon to start a sync immediately instead of waiting for its next poll — disabled when no daemon is running; a sync already in progress finishes first, since the daemon syncs one at a time), "New Voice Idea" (opens Quick Capture and starts dictating), **"Voices to label (N)"** (only shown while the queue is non-empty; opens the Voices window's Queue segment), **"Review voices"** and **"Train voices"** (always shown; open the Voices window's Review/Train segments — see "Voices window" under Calendar above), "Open Watchtower" (brings back the window and Dock icon), "Settings…" (opens the Settings window directly, without reopening the main window), and "Quit Watchtower" (full exit, see below).

The Slack sync also shows up in **Usage & Pipeline Progress** now, as a "Slack Sync" run alongside Digests, Tracks and the rest — previously it was the one background job that ran invisibly.

**Closing the window ≠ quitting** — the red close button on the main window just closes the window; Watchtower leaves the Dock but keeps running in the menu bar with background sync still active. Reopen it from the tray's "Open Watchtower", or from the Dock if it's still showing. If Settings or another Watchtower window (Logs, Voices) is still open, the Dock icon stays until that window is closed too.

**Quitting stops the daemon** — Cmd+Q or the tray's "Quit Watchtower" is the one full-exit path: it stops the background sync daemon (no further syncing or AI token spend) and then quits the app. If a recording is capturing, or a finished recording is still being transcribed, Watchtower asks first — "Stop & Quit" ends that work before quitting, "Cancel" aborts the quit and lets it finish. Audio already captured is kept on disk and offered again on the next launch.

**Several workspaces, none picked** — if more than one workspace folder under `~/.local/share/watchtower` holds a database and `active_workspace` is not set in the config, Watchtower does not guess which one to open (the background sync refuses to start on that config too). Instead of the app, the window names the workspaces it found and the command that picks one — `watchtower config set active_workspace <name>` — with a **Try Again** button to reload once you have run it.

**Starts at login, into the tray** — Watchtower registers itself as a login item so background sync is already running by the time you'd open the app; a login launch starts windowless, straight into the menu bar (no Dock icon) rather than opening the main window. To turn this off, disable Watchtower in System Settings → General → Login Items — there is no in-app setting for it.

## Background Processes

Watchtower runs a daemon (`watchtower sync --detach`) that periodically syncs Slack data. After each sync, AI pipelines run automatically:
1. **Calendar sync** — fetches Google Calendar events for the configured days ahead (runs after Slack sync, before pipelines)
2. **Attention detection** — finds the mentions, DMs, thread replies, Jira comment mentions, calendar invites and mail addressed to you, and closes the ones you have already answered in the source. **Makes no AI calls at all.** Its output has no screen of its own; it feeds Catch Up's "needs you" list and the Briefing's attention section
3. **Digest pipeline** — generates per-channel summaries (MAP phase for people signals)
4. **Tracks pipeline** — auto-creates and updates tracks from unlinked digest topics, then injects track context into rollup digests
5. **Rollup digests** — generates daily/weekly cross-channel rollups (track-aware)
6. **People pipeline** — builds team member profiles from extracted interaction patterns (once per day)
7. **Briefing pipeline** — generates a personalized daily briefing from all of the above (once per day, after the configured hour)
8. **Maintenance** — unsnoozes tasks and attention items whose snooze period has passed, returning them to active status; deletes meeting-recording audio files past the retention window (transcript text is kept)

When Memory is enabled (see below), a **Memory consolidation** step also runs each cycle, right after attention detection.

## Memory

An optional long-term memory for your assistant — off by default, and turned on by setup when you pick the **Work communication** goal. Instead of only reacting to the last few days of messages, the assistant gradually builds a knowledge base about your world: a page per person, channel, and project (who/what it is, current state, links), and episode notes recording what actually happened, each linked back to the real Slack messages it came from. Message links are validated before they are written, so every note is traceable to a source you can open. The AI chat and MCP tools can consult this memory when you ask questions; it does not change how digests or briefings work.

The assistant can also turn its memory back onto your own work: it keeps a page per Target and Track, folds the open threads it notices into your day plan, and briefs you for meetings from its own notes on who's attending. All of this is dark by default; see below.

**Where it lives:** the memory is plain markdown files in a git repository at `~/.local/share/watchtower/<workspace>/memory/`. The files are the memory — the app's database only keeps a search index over them, which can be rebuilt from the files at any time.

**Open it in Obsidian (or any editor):** the vault uses standard wiki-links and frontmatter, so you can open the folder as an Obsidian vault and browse it like a personal wiki — but any text editor works too. **Your manual edits are respected:** before writing anything, the next consolidation run saves your changes as a separate "owner edit" entry in the vault's history. Nothing you wrote is ever silently overwritten, and the git history always shows what you changed versus what the assistant wrote.

**How to enable:**

```
watchtower config set memory.enabled true
```

Once enabled, the daemon runs a consolidation pass each cycle: it seeds skeleton pages for active people/channels/projects and extracts notable episodes from raw channel history in bounded chunks (so a long backlog is worked off gradually).

**Deeper understanding (optional):** a second tier can be turned on that makes the memory smarter over time — it rewrites entity pages as new episodes arrive, merges duplicate notes, ages and archives cold history into compact rollups (never losing a source link), and forms falsifiable **beliefs** about people and projects (e.g. "Alice owns the deploy pipeline") with a confidence that rises and falls as evidence accumulates. It is off by default:

```
watchtower config set memory.semantic.enabled true
```

**Preferences learned from what you do (currently has nothing to learn from):** one more switch lets the assistant turn the patterns in how you react into ordinary beliefs about what you actually care about (e.g. "the owner does not seem to care about #deploys-noisy"). It would reason about these exactly like any other belief: the same rising-and-falling confidence, capped modestly at birth, and never outranking something you actually said. **It is inert today.** The signal it read — your verdicts on the old Inbox situations Dashboard — went away when that screen was removed in September 2026, and no replacement has been wired yet. Leave it off; the switch stays only so the machinery is ready when a new signal is chosen.

```
watchtower config set memory.semantic.preferences true   # no effect until a new signal is wired
```

**Surfaces — where the memory shows up (all off by default):** five independent switches decide whether the memory is allowed to *reach* you. Each is dark until you turn it on:

```
watchtower config set memory.surfaces.chat true          # Discuss chat sees memory + your replies feed it
watchtower config set memory.surfaces.briefing true      # briefings note what the memory revised
watchtower config set memory.surfaces.reflection true    # a weekly self-review of the memory
watchtower config set memory.surfaces.day_plan true      # day plan pulls in open loops from memory
watchtower config set memory.surfaces.meeting_prep true  # meeting prep reads its own notes on attendees
```

- **Discuss chat (`chat`)** — when you open a Discuss chat, the assistant's prompt gains a short MEMORY section: the hot map plus the people/projects and beliefs relevant to what you are discussing (a shaky belief is marked "uncertain — evidence conflicts"), and the chat can look things up with its memory tools before asking you. It is framed as notes the assistant built from Slack/Jira, not your own words. **And it listens back:** what you type is treated as your own high-trust statement — if you tell the assistant something that contradicts a belief, that correction is recorded as *your* evidence and the belief updates on the next consolidation. This is the only place your literal words become memory (that, and your manual vault edits).
- **Briefing revisions (`briefing`)** — your morning briefing may carry a short *Memory revisions* note when a belief meaningfully changed since yesterday (shaken, retired, or a clear confidence swing), phrased as something the memory noticed. Nothing changed worth mentioning → the briefing stays silent about memory.
- **Weekly reflection (`reflection`)** — about once a week the assistant reviews its *own* recent history and flags the areas that keep flip-flopping: an unstable belief is marked disputed, a churning page gets a dated note. Reflection never rewrites a belief's confidence on its own — it only points at what looks unsettled. (Until September 2026 a disputed belief was also brought to you as an item on the old Dashboard, so you could settle it. That screen is gone: today the mark shows only in the Memory tab — its badge counts disputed beliefs and the belief carries a banner — and waits there.)
- **Day plan open loops (`day_plan`)** — your day plan already schedules straight from your Targets; with this on it also folds in open threads the memory has noticed elsewhere — a stalled Track sub-item, or (for older items) a story converted from one of the old Inbox Dashboard situations, removed in September 2026 — without ever scheduling the same thing twice. It has nothing to add until you also turn on `memory.sources.operational` below, which is what gives Targets and Tracks their own memory pages to draw loops from.
- **Meeting prep from memory (`meeting_prep`)** — when the assistant preps you for an upcoming meeting, it can add a short excerpt of what it already knows about each attendee: their memory page and any beliefs it holds about them, clearly framed as the assistant's own notes rather than the attendee's words. An attendee it has no page for yet is simply skipped, no error.

Turn on only what you want; the five switches have independent effects, and with all of them off the memory quietly builds itself without ever changing how day plans, meeting prep, or briefings look.

**The Memory tab** — browse and search the vault's pages and beliefs, sorted by **Recent** or **Important**. A belief the memory marked as unsettled (it keeps flip-flopping, or new evidence contradicts something you said) carries a **Dispute pending** banner with the reason, and the Memory tab's badge counts these marks; nothing else brings them to you. **Focus** in the toolbar edits the vault's `focus.md`: list people, channels or projects by name as `- ` bullets under **Now** to rank them higher in the **Important** sort and in what is kept when old notes are archived, or under **Cooled** to rank them lower. A bullet matches pages whose name or title contains it, so keep bullets specific. Focus takes effect on the next memory run and only while it is turned on — `watchtower config set memory.focus.enabled true` (off by default); turning it off later undoes its effect.

**Sources — what the memory learns from (all off by default):** by default the memory is built from Slack/Jira. A few more independent switches let it learn from more of your world:

```
watchtower config set memory.sources.gmail true       # build memory from Gmail threads too
watchtower config set memory.sources.calendar true    # build memory from your past meetings
watchtower config set memory.sources.chats true       # pin facts from any Discuss chat with "remember this:"
watchtower config set memory.sources.operational true # give your Targets and Tracks their own memory pages
```

- **Gmail (`gmail`)** — once your Gmail is connected and synced, the assistant reads your mail the same way it reads Slack: each email **thread** becomes one episode (its participants, the question, how it resolved), linked back to the real messages, and each sender becomes a person page. A sender whose email already belongs to a known Slack colleague is recognized as the same person, not a duplicate. Gmail memory builds on its own schedule, independent of the Slack side.
- **Calendar (`calendar`)** — once Google Calendar is connected, the assistant turns your **past meetings** into memory: each ended event becomes one episode (who attended, when, where, and what it was about), with the attendees resolved to their people pages and recurring meetings grouped under a series. Where the meeting already produced AI notes (a recap), those decisions and action items are folded into the episode — the assistant reuses what it already wrote, it does not invent a summary. Because the calendar only keeps about a day of past events on hand, memory captures a meeting shortly after it ends; a recap that lands a little later still gets folded in. No AI cost is added for this — it is a mechanical fold of what you already have.
- **Pin a fact with "remember this:" (`chats`)** — this switch lets the Discuss chat on a **Target** or **Track** feed the memory. Because most of what you type there is drafting instructions ("reword this", "make it firmer"), the assistant only records a fact when you explicitly say so: start a line with **`remember this:`** (or just `remember:`) and the rest becomes your own recorded statement about that target/track's people and projects. Without the prefix, a target/track Discuss turn is treated as drafting and never becomes memory.
- **Your Targets and Tracks, as memory pages (`operational`)** — the assistant mirrors each of your Targets and Tracks into its own memory page: what it is, its current status/priority/next step, and an **Open loops** list of what's still outstanding — kept in sync automatically as the Target or Track changes, and cleared once it's done. A page for a Target or Track that was converted from one of the old Inbox Dashboard situations (historical — the Dashboard was removed in September 2026 and no new situations are created) links back to that original situation, so the story that started it stays reachable. This is what feeds the day-plan open-loops surface above, and it also means a bare Target's Discuss chat can pin facts (via `remember this:`) even when it has no Track linked to it yet. Purely a mirror — nothing about how Targets or Tracks themselves work changes, and the assistant never writes back into them.

**Roadmap note — rendering from the memory (developer-only, nothing user-visible yet):** the assistant is beginning to render its channel digests *from the memory substrate* (the episodes it already built) instead of from raw messages, which lets every quoted message link be validated at write and kills the "digest cites a message that doesn't exist" class. This is running only in a **dark compare-mode** for now: behind `memory.renders.digest_compare` (default off) the assistant renders each channel's digest from memory alongside the normal one, stores the render in a private side table, and produces a diff report for hand-review — **the normal digests you see are completely unchanged**, and nothing switches over until the comparison earns it. `watchtower memory digest-compare` runs that comparison on demand and writes the report. If you are not developing on this, you can ignore it.

**CLI commands:**
- `watchtower memory status` — node counts, extraction progress, remaining backlog, and the last run
- `watchtower memory consolidate` — run a single consolidation pass right now (the daemon owns the recurring schedule)
- `watchtower memory open <ref>` — print a memory page by ID or any alias (a name, a Slack ID, `situation:<id>`, ...)
- `watchtower memory recall <query>` — full-text search across all memory pages
- `watchtower memory index` — print the full mechanical world index (`index.md`); `map.md` is the shorter hot summary
- `watchtower memory seed --dry-run` — preview which entity pages would be created (drop `--dry-run` to create them)
- `watchtower memory reindex` — rebuild the search index from the vault files (safe anytime; files are never touched)
- `watchtower memory digest-compare` — (diagnostic) render recent channel digests from memory and diff against the normal pipeline into a report; the normal digests are read-only and untouched

## Key Concepts

- **Running context** — AI maintains a "memory" per channel, carrying forward active topics, recent decisions, and open questions between digest runs
- **People-pipeline situations** — Extracted interaction patterns (conflicts, collaborations, bottlenecks) used to build people cards. Not to be confused with dashboard situations below — same word, two different features.
- **Dashboard situations (historical)** — Clusters of signals (Slack/Jira/Calendar) plus target/track updates, composed into a single narrative unit with a summary, a why-it-matters and a chronology. This was what the old Inbox feed showed; it was removed in September 2026. Existing situations are kept and stay reachable from any Target or Track converted from one, but no new ones are created.
- **Skills** — Reusable instruction sheets that teach the assistant how to handle a kind of request: untangling a tangled thread, drafting a status update, breaking a target down. Watchtower ships three and you can write your own in Settings › Profile › Skills. Every enabled skill is available in the main AI Chat (type `/` to pick one explicitly) and in every Discuss chat (Meeting, Target, Track, Idea); when you ask for something a skill covers, the chat loads it and follows it. Skills change how a chat works, never what it can do — a Meeting chat still has no way to send or post anything, and the Target chat still routes every change through the usual proposal/Approve step. They apply to chats only; background pipelines (digests, tracks, briefings) ignore them.
- **Feedback loop** — Your thumbs up/down ratings are stored and used to improve AI quality: `watchtower tune` suggests prompt changes from them (there is no Training screen in the app), and Catch-Up topic ratings also become Learned rules
- **Starred items** — Star channels and people to prioritize them in analysis and filtering
- **Muted channels** — Channels excluded from AI processing (digests, tracks, briefings). Use the Statistics tab to mute noisy or bot-heavy channels and reduce token costs
- **Multi-provider AI** — Watchtower supports multiple AI providers: Claude (Anthropic), Codex (OpenAI), and Ollama (any local OpenAI-compatible server — Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM). Switch providers in chat toolbar or Settings. All pipelines (digests, tracks, people, briefings, catch-up, ideas) respect the configured provider and route each call to the configured light or strong tier model (`ai.models.light`/`ai.models.strong`; empty = provider default, which for Claude is an auto-updating alias). CLI flag `--provider claude|codex|ollama` overrides per-command. Config field `ai.provider` sets the default; `watchtower ai models` shows the registry and resolved models
- **Google Calendar** — Optional integration that syncs Google Calendar events to the local database. Enables meeting prep (AI-generated talking points, people notes, open items) and injects schedule context into briefings and AI chat. Connect via Settings or `watchtower calendar login`
- **Gmail** — Optional integration that syncs your Gmail Inbox to the local database. A thread addressed to you becomes an attention item (surfacing in Catch Up and the Briefing, closing itself once you reply); mail also feeds the Ideas registry via the email stream digests, and the Memory when enabled. Sending is always manual in Gmail — this release has no channel digests, tracks, or write-back for email. Connect via Settings or `watchtower gmail login`
- **Jira Cloud** — Optional integration that connects one or more Jira Cloud sites via OAuth. Select boards to sync, LLM analyzes each board's workflow and generates a profile (stages, stale thresholds, health signals). Issues sync incrementally every 15 minutes, each site on its own authorization — one site's expired grant never stops the others. Jira key patterns (PROJ-123) in Slack messages are auto-detected and linked. Feature toggles control which Jira-powered features are active, with role-based defaults. Connect via Settings › Connections › Jira → Add Jira Site, or `watchtower jira add`. CLI, site management: `watchtower jira add [--label L] [--site URL]`, `watchtower jira accounts`, `watchtower jira enable/disable <id>`, `watchtower jira login [--account N]` (re-consent an existing site), `watchtower jira remove <id>`, `watchtower jira status`. Removing a site is **non-destructive**: the authorization is dropped and syncing stops, but its synced issues, boards, and releases stay in Watchtower — the same applies to the legacy `watchtower jira logout`, which now just removes your first site instead of wiping all Jira data. CLI, per-site work (all take `--account <id>`, defaulting to your single connected site): `watchtower jira boards`, `watchtower jira boards select/deselect`, `watchtower jira boards analyze`, `watchtower jira boards override`, `watchtower jira sync`, `watchtower jira fields`. CLI, cross-site views (these aggregate every connected site and reject `--account`): `watchtower jira workload`, `watchtower jira blockers`, `watchtower jira project-map`, `watchtower jira releases`. Plus `watchtower jira users`, `watchtower jira users map`, `watchtower jira features`, `watchtower jira features enable/disable`, `watchtower jira features reset`
