# Mobile companion (POC)

Specs: [business](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-mobile-poc-business.md) and [technical design](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-07-mobile-poc-design.md).

Section labels name the plan task that built them: A<n>, B<n> and C<n> are Task n of `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-08-mobile-poc-a-skeleton.md`, `…-b-workbench-remote.md` and `…-c-calendar-recording.md`; E2E<n> is Task n of `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-12-mobile-e2e-encryption.md` (spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-12-mobile-e2e-encryption-research.md`; D1–D14 and F1–F37 are that plan's decisions and preflight rulings). Sections are grouped hub (the Mac) → Kit (shared Swift package) → phone; each half of a phone feature links its other half.

## Architecture

- **The hub lives in the Desktop process** (`WatchtowerDesktop/Sources/Services/MobileHub/`). The Go daemon never touches CloudKit. Only the Desktop executable target (and its test target) links the Kit's `WatchtowerSync`; `WatchtowerCore` stays Kit-free (`PublicAPISurfaceTests`).
- **Zones and scopes.** The hub writes `DataZone-<hub_id>` (its only writer) and reads phone requests from `RelayZone-<hub_id>`, both custom zones in the Mac user's private database (`hub_id` from `hub_meta`, multi-hub spec §3.1), through `CKSyncEngine` (a hub-role `CloudKitTransport`, `private` scope, on those zones only). A same-Apple-ID phone syncs that private database; a different-Apple-ID phone reaches both zones through zone-wide shares in its `shared` scope (DataZone read-only, RelayZone read-write).
- **Kinds.** DataZone records are `SliceKind`s (`heartbeat`, `device_grant`, `recording_job` and the workbench kinds); RelayZone records are `RelayRecordKind`s (`action`, `recording_upload`, `device`, and the link request `hello`). Inside the app a record's plain name is `<kind>-<id>`. On the wire the kind and the name are opaque (see End-to-end encryption): every record is a `WatchtowerEnvelope` with `kind` = `"wt"`, and its name is an HMAC under `K_meta`. Only `heartbeat`, `keybox-<device_id>` and `hello-<device_id>` keep fixed names that contain no key.
- **`SecureTransport` sits above the seam.** Both sides talk to CloudKit only through a `SecureTransport` (Kit, `WatchtowerSync/Secure/`) that wraps the `CloudSyncTransport` (`CloudKitTransport` live, `InMemoryCloudTransport` in tests and the demo). It seals everything its role sends and opens what its role reads in RelayZone. It passes DataZone reads through still sealed, so the phone's replica verifies and opens them itself. A hub without E2E keys (no CloudKit entitlement: `keyring` nil) publishes and reads nothing. It never falls back to plaintext.

## End-to-end encryption (E2E1–E2E18)

Since 2026-10-12, every record the hub and a phone exchange through CloudKit is sealed with keys that exist only on the Mac and on the paired phones. CloudKit's `encryptedValues` stays as the outer layer (spec §3, §7), so ADP is no longer what protects the content.

### Threat model in short (spec §1, §4)

- **Covered.** Apple and anyone who compels Apple see ciphertext only, whether or not ADP is on. So does an attacker holding the Apple ID or iCloud Keychain, because no key is synchronizable or backed up. A share participant and the storage layer see ciphertext too. None of them can forge a phone action or a hub record. A swapped, replayed or rolled-back record fails authentication or is ignored as stale. A photographed QR alone cannot link a phone: the owner must confirm on the Mac, comparing a code that hashes a nonce the Mac draws only after it accepted the phone's hello, so an attacker who also sees the owner's phone cannot pick keys that reproduce its code (SAS v2). A phone removed on the Mac reads nothing published after the removal. One edge: a save that `CKSyncEngine` had queued before the rotation committed can still land once under the old epoch; it carries only content from before the removal, and the next cycle reseals it.
- **Not covered.**
  - An unlocked phone that is stolen or runs malware, and any malware on the Mac (the Mac is the plaintext source of truth). This reaches past the data on the phone.
  - **No forward secrecy against a later device-key compromise.** The relay keys are static-static: X25519(`hub_kx`, `dev_kx`) with the link nonce as salt, and the epoch is only an HKDF label. The nonce is in the QR and in the phone's `UserDefaults`. Keyboxes are HPKE base mode to the long-lived `dev_kx`. Suppose someone kept the ciphertext: Apple retaining deleted records, a backup of the container, or a future relay's logs. If that person later reads a phone's `dev_kx` (an unlocked phone, malware, a forensic extraction of an unlocked device), they can derive every relay key of every epoch. They can then open:
    - every recording and action that phone ever sent, even after the phone deleted the plain audio on `received`;
    - every keybox it ever received, which gives each epoch's `K_data` and `K_alert`, so the DataZone content of all those epochs.
    
    A leaked `hub_kx` opens every phone's past relay traffic the same way. A ratchet (an ephemeral X25519 per hello and keybox exchange, so a relay key does not derive from static keys alone) is left for when a relay is built.
  - **`K_alert` is readable after the first unlock.** The shared alerts group keeps the current and previous `K_alert` and the hub's public signing key `AfterFirstUnlock` (the NotifyService extension must open alerts while the phone is locked). A forensic read of a phone that has been unlocked once since boot yields them. With the owner's CloudKit access, they reveal the title, workbench name and kind of every open ask alert.
  - The platform itself: the OS, CryptoKit and the Keychain are trusted.
  - Availability: records can be deleted, withheld or delayed. The phone detects staleness; it cannot prevent it.
  - Metadata (below).
  - Shared zones have no per-record ACL, so a RelayZone participant can trample other phones' records until swept (denial of service only).

### Primitives and keys (E2E1, E2E5, E2E10)

- **Primitives.** CryptoKit only. Suite 1 is X25519, Ed25519, HKDF-SHA256, ChaCha20-Poly1305, and HPKE RFC 9180 `DHKEM(X25519, HKDF-SHA256), HKDF-SHA256, ChaCha20Poly1305` (`SealedBox.ciphersuite`). Suite byte `2` is reserved for a hybrid ML-KEM wrap and refused today, as is any unknown version or suite (`EnvelopeError.unsupportedVersion`/`.unsupportedSuite`, the phone's "Update Watchtower" path).
- **Hub keys.**
  - `hub_sig` (Ed25519) signs every DataZone envelope, keyboxes included.
  - `hub_kx` (X25519) receives hellos and derives the relay keys.
  - A 32-byte wrap key.
  - `K_meta` (32 bytes) makes record names.
  - Per epoch `e`: `K_data[e]` for DataZone content and `K_alert[e]` for lock-screen ask alerts. `K_alert` opens only ask alerts, but it is readable after the first unlock (see Not covered).
  - Per linked phone, epoch and direction: a relay key, `KeyDerivation.relayKey` = HKDF-SHA256 over X25519(`hub_kx`, `dev_kx`), with the link nonce as salt and info `"wt/relay/v1/" ‖ p2h|h2p ‖ 0 ‖ hub_id ‖ device_id ‖ epoch`. The epoch is part of the info (D10), so a rotation rotates the pairwise keys too.
- **Phone keys.** `dev_sig` (Ed25519) and `dev_kx` (X25519). The phone's `device_id` is the UUID form of `SHA-256("wt/device-id/v1" ‖ dev_sig_pub)[0..<16]` (`KeyDerivation.deviceID`, D7). It is written as the uppercase `uuidString` (F30); the hub compares ids case-insensitively. The hub's `sender_id` is its `hub_id`.
- **Where the hub keeps them (D2, D3).**
  - `hub_sig`, `hub_kx` and the wrap key are Keychain items (`KeychainHubKeyStore`), in the data protection keychain, `AfterFirstUnlockThisDeviceOnly` (the hub must publish and answer while the screen is locked) and never synchronizable.
  - `K_meta` (`hub_meta.k_meta_sealed`) and each epoch's `K_data ‖ K_alert` (`hub_epochs.sealed_keys`) live in `hubstate.db`, sealed with ChaChaPoly under the wrap key, with hub id and epoch in the AAD. A rotation is then one SQLite transaction.
  - `HubKeyring` (Desktop, `MobileHub/HubKeyring.swift`) is the hub's `SecureKeyProvider` and its relay `RollbackGuard`. It holds one `SeqAllocator` over `hub_meta.envelope_seq_ceiling`, so there is one keyring per sidecar.
  - The hub identity is deliberately not backed up: **a new Mac is a new pairing**.
- **Where the phone keeps them.**
  - `KeychainKeyStore` (the Kit's `PhoneKeyStore`), all `ThisDeviceOnly` and never synchronizable.
  - Secrets (the device identity, each hub's `HubLinkKeys`: hub public keys, link nonce, `K_meta` and at most two `EpochKeys`) are `WhenUnlocked`.
  - The non-secret `deviceid` item and an `AfterFirstUnlock` copy of the hub's public signing key are readable while locked (F14), so apply can verify signatures.
  - Every item goes to the app's private access group explicitly (`WTKeychainPrivateGroup` in Info.plist, F20).
  - Only the alert material (`AlertMaterial`: the `K_alert` epochs with the data keys emptied, plus the hub's public signing key, F21) goes to the shared alerts group (`WTKeychainAlertGroup`), `AfterFirstUnlock`, which the NotifyService extension reads.
  - The app reads every key through one `CachingPhoneKeyStore` (`PhoneKeychain.store()`). It caches only while the app is known to run unlocked: it drops everything on `protectedDataWillBecomeUnavailable`, `willResignActive` and `didEnterBackground`, never caches a failed read, and cannot put stale keys back after a drop (a generation counter).
- **Keychain writes.**
  - A write is update-or-add (`SecItemUpdate`, then `SecItemAdd` on `errSecItemNotFound`), never delete-then-add, so a failed write cannot destroy a key.
  - Creation is add-only. A creator that loses the race loads what the winner stored.
  - A locked keychain is its own error, `KeyStoreError.locked`, and is never read as "absent".
- **Sequence numbers.** `SeqAllocator` hands out u64 values from 1 (0 means "none" for the rollback guards). It persists the block ceiling before using a new block, so a crash skips ahead and never reuses a seq. The phone keeps its ceiling in each hub's replica (F29) and carries it to a rebuilt session of the same hub within the process (`AppRoot.seqFloors`, `HubSession.raiseSeqFloor`). The new replica stores the carried floor at once (`HubSession.persistSeqFloor`), so two removals with no seal between them keep it. Seqs that restart anyway (a reinstall, an unreadable ceiling) are absorbed by the hub at the re-link: `HubSyncState.linkDevice` deletes the phone's `relay_seen` rows and resets `devices.last_seq` (final review Kit I2, Desktop I-1). Those marks protect nothing once the link nonce is replaced.

### Envelope, inner frame and record names (E2E3, E2E7)

- **Envelope.** `Envelope`/`EnvelopeHeader` (`Secure/Envelope.swift`). The 68-byte header is `"WTE" | ver u8 (1) | suite u8 (1) | flags u8 (bit0 signed, bit1 has-asset, bit2 padded) | hub_id 16 | sender_id 16 | epoch u32 | seq u64 | nonce 12 | class u16 | body_len u32`. Then the body (ciphertext ‖ 16-byte tag, or for HPKE `enc` 32 ‖ ciphertext ‖ tag), then a 64-byte Ed25519 signature when signed.
  - The AAD is the header plus the zone byte and the length-prefixed wire record name (`EnvelopeContext`). A record moved to another name or zone does not open.
  - The signature covers `"wt/sig/v1" ‖ AAD ‖ body`.
  - The u16 after the nonce is the **class** (`EnvelopeClass`), not the slice kind (D1): `data` (K_data, signed by `hub_sig`), `alert` (K_alert, signed), `keybox` (HPKE to `dev_kx`, signed by `hub_sig`), `hello` (HPKE to `hub_kx`, signed by `dev_sig`), `relayToHub` and `relayToPhone`.
  - `EnvelopeSealer.openSymmetric` refuses a `data` or `alert` envelope that is unsigned or opened without a verifier. Every DataZone envelope must carry a valid hub signature.
  - Nonces are 96 random bits. For the HPKE classes the header nonce is 12 zero bytes.
- **Inner frame** (`InnerFrame`, the encrypted plaintext). It carries the plain `kind`, the plain record id, the notify level, an optional 101-byte `AssetDescriptor` (key ‖ asset header ‖ SHA-256 of the plaintext) and the payload, then zero padding.
  - The frame is padded to the next of 256 B, 1 KiB, 4 KiB, 16 KiB or 64 KiB, then to 64 KiB multiples (`Padding.paddedLength`).
  - Padding is clipped so the envelope stays at or under `Envelope.maxBytes` = 900 000 (D12, the single source of `SlicePublisher.maxPayloadBytes`). A record that does not fit even unpadded is skipped as oversized, as before.
- **On CloudKit.** A sealed record is a `WatchtowerEnvelope` (`CloudKitTransport.recordType`).
  - `kind` is the constant `"wt"` (`WireKind.envelope`).
  - The envelope is `encryptedValues["payload"]`.
  - `modifiedAt` stays plaintext.
  - The asset file rides as the record's `CKAsset`, already sealed.
  - The notify level travels only inside the frame (F28).
- **Record names** (`KeyDerivation.recordName`, `SecureTransport.wireName`) are 26 lowercase base32 characters of `HMAC-SHA256(K_meta, "wt/name/v1" ‖ zone ‖ kind_len ‖ kind ‖ id)[0..<16]`, with the plain name split at its first `-`. Fixed names that contain no key: `heartbeat`, `keybox-<device_id>` (the plain `device_grant-<device_id>`), `hello-<device_id>` and the signal `sig-<wire name>`. The hub keeps each record's wire name beside its plain name in `slice_state.wire_name` (F19, `SecureTransport.plainName(forWire:)`).
- **Assets** (`ChunkedAssetCipher`, E2E2). Every asset gets a fresh random `K_asset` and is sealed in 1 MiB chunks. Chunk `c` uses nonce `u32 0 ‖ u64 c` and AAD = the 37-byte `AssetHeader` (`"WTA" | ver | suite | asset_id | chunk_size | total_len | chunk_count`) ‖ `c` ‖ last flag. The last chunk is zero-padded, so a sealed asset is a whole number of MiB.
  - **The sealed file has no plaintext header**, only the sealed chunks. A clear `total_len` or `asset_id` would cancel the padding. The header travels in the encrypted `AssetDescriptor` and is bound into every chunk's AAD.
  - Files are sealed and opened in a stream, one chunk in memory. Decryption writes through an owner-only (0600) temp file and an atomic move.
  - `ReplicaStore.maxAssetBytes` is the sealed length of 20 MiB (F33).
- **Signals** (`WireKind.signal`, the CloudKit type `WatchtowerSignal`, E2E11, E2E18) are the one plaintext record type. Each is a payload-free `sig-<wire name of an ask alert>` that `SecureTransport` passes through unsealed (F12). It names only the alert's opaque record. A `sig` record in any other shape is dropped as `malformedSignal`.

### Who seals and opens what (E2E7–E2E9, E2E11, E2E12)

- **The hub** (`SlicePublisher` through `MobileHubStorage.secure`) seals each DataZone record as `data` (or `alert` for `ask_alert`), signed, one record at a time. One record that cannot be sealed, such as a keybox without a recipient key, is skipped alone and never blocks its batch.
  - Slice hashes stay hashes of the plaintext (random nonces make every envelope differ).
  - Staged slice assets (`SliceAssetStore`) are sealed files with a `<fileName>.desc` descriptor beside them. The Mac keeps `K_asset` next to its own plaintext; FileVault is the at-rest story there. A crash between the file and its descriptor reads as "not staged" and is staged again.
- **The phone writes RelayZone only.** `device` records, actions and uploads are sealed `relayToHub` under the phone's p2h key. The hub's echoes come back sealed `relayToPhone` to the authenticated sender (F16).
  - The hub opens a p2h record only from a linked phone, in an accepted epoch: the current one, or the previous one for 7 days (D10).
  - The hub opens its own h2p echoes by trying each linked phone's key (F2, `relayDeviceIDs`).
  - Anything that opens under no key is dropped and reported once per wire name (`onRejected`), and is **never echoed**: the hub holds no key to answer it with. Relay hygiene deletes such records by wire name after the action age window (`relayHygieneScan`, `deleteWire`). Hygiene ages every record, opened or not, by the server's `CKRecord.modificationDate` (`CloudRecord.serverModifiedAt`, buffered in `events.server_modified_at`), never by the sender's plaintext `modifiedAt` claim, which stands in only for an event buffered before the column existed; the hub's buffer sweep uses the same date (target 168).
- **Rollback and replay (D4).** CloudKit gives no ordering, so there is no strict per-device sequence gate. Instead:
  - The AAD binds the opaque name, zone, hub, epoch and seq.
  - The exactly-once ledger keys on the wire name.
  - The authenticated `created_at` gives the 7-day / 24 h age window, and the ledger outlives it by a day. A `created_at` more than 5 minutes ahead of the Mac (`RelayProcessor.maxClockSkew`) is `expired` too, so a far-future date cannot stretch the window (target 168); that echo carries "Your iPhone's and Mac's clocks differ by more than 5 minutes — check Date & Time on both." (`clockAheadMessage`; either clock may be the wrong one), a plain expiry none.
  - A per-record `(epoch, seq)` guard, keyed by wire name and sender (F3, `relay_seen` on the hub and in the phone's replica), ignores an older version of the same record. An identical re-delivery is `.same` and idempotent.
  - `devices.last_seq` is only a high-water mark for diagnostics.
  - Uploads have no age window (a legitimate upload can be days late). Instead their ledger row and `relay_seen` row are kept while the record's epoch is still accepted, so a replayed upload can never open and also find an empty ledger.
- **Deliberate re-sends are not replays** (Review Focus 2). The outbox seals an action once at enqueue and keeps the envelope as its `pending_actions` row. The uploader seals a recording once. Every retry sends the stored envelope byte for byte (`SecureTransport.saveSealed`), and so does CloudKit's conflict re-save.
- **The device gate is key-based** (`RelayProcessor`). An action or upload is applied only when:
  - the phone whose relay key opened it (`sealed.senderID`) is the payload's `device_id`;
  - that phone is in `devices`;
  - `LinkedDevice.accepts(creator:)` holds. The creator check stays as defense in depth (spec §5).
  
  Otherwise the record fails `device_not_linked`, echoed to the authenticated sender. A sealed `device` record acts only for the phone whose key opened it (`device_id` equal to the sender), so one phone can never unlink or regrant another. A mismatch is dropped and logged, never echoed. Likewise an action or upload whose payload id does not give the record name the envelope authenticated (`action.recordName`/`upload.recordName` ≠ the frame's name) is dropped and logged before any ledger read, claim or echo (target 148): the ledger and the echo key on that name.

### Pairing with the code (E2E4, E2E13, E2E16)

1. **The QR** (`LinkPayload` v3) adds the hub's public keys `hub_sig` and `hub_kx` (base64url, 32 bytes; an all-zero key is refused) to the multi-hub v2 fields. `MobileLinkCenter.issueCode()` refuses without hub keys: `MobileLinkError.noKeys`, "Mobile isn't available on this Mac: its encryption keys are missing."
2. **The phone** checks the code and creates its device identity on first use (only the link flow ever creates one). It stores the scan's `HubLinkKeys` (no `K_meta`, no epochs yet) and restarts on an empty replica (`prepare(fresh: true)`, so the old link's rollback marks, outbox and sealed recordings go). It then writes `hello-<device_id>`: a `DeviceHello` (its `DevicePayload` plus both public keys), HPKE-sealed to `hub_kx` with info `"wt/hello/v1" ‖ hub_id ‖ link_nonce`, and signed with `dev_sig`.
3. **The hub** (`MobileLinkCenter.handleHello`; the relay hands hellos over still sealed, F4) tries each kept code's nonce, newest first. It checks:
   - the frame is the hello its record names;
   - the envelope is signed by the enclosed key;
   - the device id, the header's sender and the record name all hash to that key (D7);
   - the creator (and in `shared` scope, both shares accepted).
   
   A hello that opens under no kept code, or fails a check, is logged and dropped. An unknown code is therefore unanswerable, and the phone ends on its wait timeout. A used or expired code gets a refusal keybox sealed to the hello's key (F5, `link_refusals.agreement_key`). A valid hello goes to `pending_devices` with its keys, a fresh `confirm_nonce` and its four-digit code, and the code stays unused.

   **Attempt cap.** One code hands out at most `MobileLinkCenter.maxNoncesPerCode` (3) confirm nonces in total, across all devices (`link_codes.confirm_nonces_drawn`). A known device's key change counts too: `device_id` hashes only `dev_sig`, so re-rolling `dev_kx` would otherwise buy a fresh nonce and code per hello. Once the nonces are spent, every further hello for that code is refused with `too_many_requests` (sealed to its key) and never shown; the owner shows a new code. A waiting phone re-sending its hello unchanged (same code and keys) draws none and keeps its nonce and code; a changed name or typing request on such a re-send is ignored. The owner's Refuse gives no try back. So guessing the owner's code online costs at most three tries per code, each visible on the Mac. Refusals are bounded too (target 168): a new phone's refusal draws from the same per-code counter, up to `maxNoncesPerCode + maxRefusalsPerCode` (3 + 10) answers per code, and at most `maxLinkRefusals` (50) phones hold a refusal at once (`HubSyncState.saveBoundedLinkRefusal`); past either bound the hello is logged and left unanswered. A code pruned while its hello was being checked has no counter, so only the total cap applies and its phone still gets its refusal. A phone already refused is rewritten in place. Hellos also count toward the relay pass budget (`batchLimit`), so a flood spreads over passes.

   **Names.** `MobileLinkCenter.displayName` cleans the hello's name before it is stored: control and format characters (newlines, bidi overrides, zero-width marks) go, except the emoji joiner; whitespace runs become one space; the result is clipped to 60 grapheme clusters, and an empty one reads "iPhone".
4. **The code (SAS v2, D6; final review I-1).** The hub draws `confirm_nonce` (32 random bytes) only after it accepted the hello, whose device keys are thereby committed, and keeps it with the request (`pending_devices.confirm_nonce`; a hello re-sent unchanged keeps the stored nonce and code, a hello under another code gets a new one). `KeyDerivation.sas` hashes the transcript `"wt/sas/v2" ‖ hub_id ‖ link_nonce ‖ hub_sig ‖ hub_kx ‖ dev_sig ‖ dev_kx ‖ confirm_nonce`, takes 4 bytes from HKDF (salt `link_nonce`, info `"wt/sas/v2"`), and reduces them mod 10 000 to four digits. The waiting keybox carries the nonce to the phone (`DeviceGrant.confirm_nonce`, sealed to its `dev_kx`, signed by `hub_sig`); the phone shows no code until it has a well-formed one. Every SAS input but the nonce is in the QR, so v1 let a QR photographer who could also read the owner's phone grind about 10 000 key pairs offline to match its code; now each try costs a hello and one of the code's three nonces. A request stored before v2 has no nonce and is never offered. Both screens show the code:
   - The Mac sheet: "Link "<name>"?" and "Code 7342 — check that your iPhone shows the same code".
   - The phone: "Check that your Mac shows **7342**, then click Link on the Mac."
5. **The owner decides on the Mac.**
   - **Link** runs `confirmLink(deviceID:sas:)`. It is bound to the code the row showed: if a new hello replaced the request meanwhile, nothing is linked and the sheet says "This iPhone's code changed. Check the new code and choose again." (`MobileLinkError.linkCodeChanged`). Otherwise the code is re-checked (it may have expired or been used meanwhile), the phone is linked with its keys, and the post-link close refuses every other waiting request with `used_code` (F17).
   - **Refuse** (`refuseLink`), closing the sheet, or expiry give `not_confirmed` (or `expired_code`).
   - Keys are never wrapped for an unconfirmed device. Two hellos for one code (a photographed QR racing the owner's phone) show as two rows with different codes, and only the confirmed one gets keys (Review Focus 5).
   - **Duplicate warning.** When two waiting rows share a name, each says "Two iPhones named "<name>" are waiting — link only the one whose screen shows this code."; when they share a code (1 in 10 000, nothing to compare), "Two iPhones show code <code> — refuse both and show a new code." (`MobileLinkSheetViewModel.duplicateWarning`). The name check compares cleaned names case-insensitively, so a homoglyph name (a Cyrillic "і" for "i") passes it; the code comparison, and the same-code warning, remain.
6. **The keybox** (`Keybox`, published by `DeviceGrantSlice` under `keybox-<device_id>`) is HPKE-sealed to the phone's `dev_kx` with info `"wt/keybox/v1" ‖ hub_id ‖ device_id`, and signed by `hub_sig`. It carries:
   - the `DeviceGrant` the readers already decode;
   - once linked, `KeyboxKeys`: the current epoch with `K_data` and `K_alert`, `K_meta`, and after a rotation the retired epoch's keys (`previous*`) while its grace runs.
   
   A pending request reads `awaiting_confirmation: true` with its `confirm_nonce` and without keys ("Your Mac sees this iPhone" and the code on the phone). A waiting keybox also carries `link_code_tag` = base64url(SHA-256(`"wt/link-code/v1"` ‖ link_nonce)[0..<16]) of the code the request was made with (`KeyDerivation.linkCodeTag`), inside the sealed, signed keybox. The phone shows "Your Mac sees this iPhone" and the code only from a waiting keybox whose tag is its own scan's: the same QR scanned again (an unchanged hello, so the keybox equals the scan's baseline) still shows the Mac's code, and one left from an earlier code never shows one. A refusal or a link still answers the scan only when it differs from the baseline. A refusal reads `link_refused` (`not_confirmed` and `too_many_requests` are new) without keys.
7. **The phone's wait** polls the keybox every 2 s (`grantPollInterval`) until the code's `exp` + 120 s (never longer than a full code lifetime plus the grace from now), because the owner needs time to compare and click.
   - A `linked` keybox with keys is this scan's answer only when its `linked_at` is at or after the code's `iat`, both on the Mac's clock. An older link's keybox is not this scan's confirmation (review M-3).
   - On success the keys join the stored `HubLinkKeys` and the link is saved.
   - A failed or timed-out scan removes its keys (unless they belong to the saved link). A re-scan of the Mac the phone is already linked to, which does not end linked, ends the old link (I-1). It never leaves a "linked but unusable" phone.

### Rotation and revocation (E2E14)

- **Triggers.**
  - **Remove** on the Mac, or the phone's own `unlinked` record (`RotationReason.removed`).
  - The cap: 90 days or 2^28 seqs (`rotationDue`, checked at each publish cycle start, `.cap`).
  - `.manual` (a suspected leak) exists in the keyring but no UI calls it yet.
- **One transaction** (`HubKeyring.rotate(reason:)`, the only rotation path; Review Focus 4). It does all of this together or not at all:
  - creates the new epoch's `K_data`/`K_alert`;
  - retires the old epoch;
  - removes the device (for `.removed`);
  - blanks every `slice_state` hash. The hashes are blanked, not deleted, so records that left the window are still deleted next cycle.
  
  If another process rotated first, or the wipe fails, nothing is written: the old epoch stays current and the device stays linked. A killed rotation resumes on the same epoch. A cycle sealed under the old epoch checks the epoch again before saving, so a removed phone never gets post-removal data under the old key.
- **Then** the next cycle republishes the window under `e+1`. The remaining phones' keyboxes carry the new keys plus the previous epoch's. Open ask alerts are resealed under `K_alert[e+1]` without a new signal. Relay envelopes of the previous epoch are accepted for 7 days (D10).
- **The removed phone** loses its relay keys, so the hub can no longer echo to it. Instead, for 7 days (`revokedLifetime`), its keybox reads `revoked: true` without keys, signed and sealed to its own key (`revoked_devices`, D9). The phone reads that as "This Mac removed this phone" and wipes, in either scope, including a same-Apple-ID phone that sees no zone vanish.
- **Settings → Mobile.** Removing a same-Apple-ID phone now says "Removed <name>. It can no longer read anything new from this Mac." (`MobileSettingsViewModel.removedSameAppleID`).
- **`K_meta` does not rotate** (D5): a removed phone could still map future opaque names of kinds and ids it already knows (metadata only).
- **Keys reach the phone's Keychain by a reconcile** (final review Phone I-1). The hydrator's keybox signal is one-shot, so the merge into the Keychain does not rely on it alone. `LinkingViewModel.reconcileKeyboxKeys(hubID:)` runs, per hub, after every fetch (from the grant check's keybox read), at unlock (`HubSession.onKeysAvailable`) and on a re-scan of the Mac the phone is already linked to. It merges the stored keybox's epochs that the Keychain lacks; it compares epoch numbers, never key material. It then has the host open the stored rows (`LinkHost.keysMerged(hubID:)` → that session's `indexStoredRows`). With nothing missing, it writes nothing.
- **Hygiene and `hello`/`device` records.** Relay hygiene deletes `hello` records older than 7 days. A linked phone's `device` record sealed under an epoch past its D10 grace no longer opens, so it is deleted after 7 days of plaintext age like other unopenable records. Before E2E, device records were kept. Nothing is lost: the link state lives in the sidecar, and the phone's next settings change writes a new record.

### The locked phone (E2E8, E2E9, E2E17)

- **Hydration while locked** (Review Focus 1). `ReplicaStore.apply` needs no secret key. It verifies each envelope's hub signature with the `AfterFirstUnlock` public key, checks the hub id and the class, and stores the ciphertext with its `(epoch, seq)` (`slice_records.epoch`/`seq`, `needs_index`).
  - A bad signature, another hub's record or an older version is refused and counted (`rejectedCount`), never stored.
  - The `data_seen` table remembers the newest version per wire name across deletions, so a deleted record cannot come back older. It is dropped only by the format wipe.
  - A newer envelope version or suite is stored unindexed and counted (`newerFormatCount`; Settings shows "Update Watchtower on this iPhone: your Mac uses a newer format.").
  - Signals and other phones' keyboxes are skipped silently (F12, F13).
  - A key that cannot be read at all (before the first unlock, no link yet) aborts the batch with its token kept (F15).
  - A record whose epoch keys are missing is still stored. It opens once its keybox lands, so a batch carrying new-epoch records plus their keybox never deadlocks.
- **Indexing.** `indexPending()` opens the stored rows with the epoch keys and fills their plain `kind` and `plain_name` (D11). Every read opens the payload again; only the kind and name are kept in the clear. It runs:
  - after each hydration;
  - on `protectedDataDidBecomeAvailable` (`HubSession.keysBecameAvailable`, which also seals waiting recordings and re-adopts held ones);
  - on foreground (`setActive(true)`).
  
  `ReplicaHydrator.onRecordsApplied` reports what indexing opened, with each frame's notify level (F11). A keybox indexed this way reaches the link flow.
- **Relay while locked.** `SecureTransport.changes` throws `SecureTransportError.keysUnavailable` on a locked keychain. `RelayFeed` keeps its token and replays the echoes after unlock. An enqueue while locked throws `KeyStoreError.locked` before anything is sent.
- **Decoded values.** `ReplicaDecodeCache` drops everything it decoded on `protectedDataWillBecomeUnavailable` and `didEnterBackground`, so decoded plaintext does not outlive the unlocked foreground (final review Protocol M-9). A decode that began before a drop returns its value but does not keep it.
- **On screen.** `ResilientObservation` does not count `locked`/`keysUnavailable` as failures. It keeps the last value and restarts every live observation on unlock, because list reads silently leave out locked rows. Settings shows "Unlock this iPhone to see the latest from your Mac.", the session detail "Unlock this iPhone to see the latest report.", and the recap "Unlock this iPhone to read the transcript."
- **Boot.** The device id is read from the non-secret `deviceid` item. Before the first unlock since a restart even that is unreadable, and the boot waits for the unlock (`Boot.waitingForUnlock`). A launch that is not the link flow never creates an identity.

### File protection on the phone (E2E17, D8)

`FileProtection` (`WatchtowerMobile/Sources/App/`):

- **Class C** (`completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication`, the spec's allowed fallback) for anything background work must reach after the first unlock, all of which is ciphertext or metadata: the app group directory and each hub's directory in it (the replica; `HubPaths.prepare`), each hub's transport store folder (`<appSupport>/hubs/<hub_id>/`; `AppEnvironment.liveTransportStore(path:)` sets the class before the store opens), the transport's incoming asset stash (`TransportStore.stashDirectory`), each hub's recordings folder (sealed `.enc` files), `.held` and its manifest. The hold manifest is class C so a locked background wipe can still hand audio over.
- **`completeUnlessOpen`** for plaintext audio, for its whole life on the phone: the capture in progress, then the finished recording. Plain audio stays on the phone until the Mac confirms receipt (owner ruling ask #89, option A): sealing writes the ciphertext `<id>.enc` beside it and only that ciphertext goes to iCloud; the hub's `received` echo deletes both, a `failed` echo keeps both, the owner's discard deletes both. This is what lets main's #63 hold carry an undelivered recording across a wipe.
- Apart from that plain audio, no plaintext content is left at rest after sealing. The simulator stores no protection class, so tests read back what was set; the real attributes need a signed device run.

### Migration: the hard cut (E2E15, D13)

- **The hub.** Record type `WatchtowerEnvelope` replaces `WatchtowerRecord`, which is ignored on fetch. A hub that ran before E2E deletes its two zones once through the pending zone delete, forgets its phones and starts on fresh keys (`hub_meta.e2e_v1`, see **E2E hard cut** under the zone lifecycle). The same cut runs again whenever:
  - the Keychain identity was recreated (`HubKeyring.identityWasRecreated`);
  - the stored keys no longer open (`storedGroupKeysUnreadable`);
  - a partial or damaged identity (`KeyStoreError.corrupt` twice) is deleted and recreated.
- **The phone.** A replica of format 1 (plaintext) is dropped and re-hydrated. Plaintext `pending_actions` rows are deleted with `secure_delete` and a WAL checkpoint. At launch, a saved or pending link without a hub signing key (a v2 link) is dropped, its hub removed (`dropUnencryptedLinks`, per registry row) with "Watchtower now encrypts your data. Scan the code on your Mac again." Main's #62 device id (a random UUID in the Keychain plus a `UserDefaults` copy) is deleted at every launch (`LegacyDeviceID.remove`), replaced by the key-derived id.
- **Every pre-E2E phone re-pairs once.** Restoring a phone to new hardware, or reinstalling it, also means re-pairing (keys are `ThisDeviceOnly`). A new Mac re-pairs every phone.

## Building a hub-capable Mac app (A10)

The mobile hub syncs through CloudKit, so it needs a Developer ID build that carries the iCloud entitlements and an embedded provisioning profile (design §10).

- **The profile.** A Developer ID provisioning profile (developer.apple.com → Profiles → Developer ID) for App ID `com.watchtower.desktop` — the app's `CFBundleIdentifier` (`BUNDLE_ID` in `scripts/build-app.sh`), not `com.aiwatchtowers.watchtower` — on the signing identity's team, with the iCloud capability and the container `iCloud.com.aiwatchtowers.watchtower`. Point `WATCHTOWER_PROVISION_PROFILE` at the downloaded `.provisionprofile`; a relative path resolves against the project root, so the variable can live in the build profile (`.env`, or the `ENV_FILE` of a flavor).
- **Early check** (`provision-profile-check`, before the Swift build): the profile is decoded with `security cms -D`; the build fails if the file is missing or undecodable, if its `com.apple.application-identifier` is not `<its team-identifier>.com.watchtower.desktop`, or if it does not grant the container.
- **Signing** (`app-codesign`): with a real identity and a checked profile, the profile is copied to `Contents/embedded.provisionprofile` and the bundle is signed with a temporary copy of `scripts/Watchtower-cloud.entitlements` into which the profile's `com.apple.application-identifier` and `com.apple.developer.team-identifier` are merged. A hand-signed app with restricted entitlements needs both (Xcode adds them; `codesign` does not). The team id is never written in the repo.
- The cloud entitlements are the base `scripts/Watchtower.entitlements` plus `com.apple.developer.icloud-container-identifiers` = `iCloud.com.aiwatchtowers.watchtower`, `com.apple.developer.icloud-services` = `CloudKit`, `com.apple.developer.icloud-container-environment` = `Production` and `com.apple.developer.aps-environment` = `production`. Keep the base keys in sync; the test checks it.
- Every build flavor (`BUILD_FLAVOR`, e.g. `ENV_FILE=.env.corp`) uses the same bundle id and container: both are signed by the same Developer team. The signing blocks have no flavor logic.
- A real identity without a profile signs with the base entitlements and prints `WARNING: mobile hub disabled: no provisioning profile`; the app then shows "Needs a signed build" in Settings → Mobile.
- Ad-hoc signing (`make app-dev`, or no identity) always keeps the base entitlements and never embeds a profile: amfid kills an ad-hoc app that carries restricted entitlements. A set `WATCHTOWER_PROVISION_PROFILE` is still checked early, then ignored with the note `provisioning profile ignored: ad-hoc signing`. Hub logic is tested on `InMemoryCloudTransport` and needs no signing.
- Whether amfid accepts the signed bundle and CloudKit reaches Production can only be shown on a real device (the A14 device smoke).
- Covered by `scripts/tests/test-build-app-cloud.sh` (`make test-scripts`), which runs both blocks against stubbed `security` and `codesign`.

### CloudKit environments

CloudKit keeps two environments per container, Development and Production, and a record saved in one is never seen from the other. The Mac and the phone must talk to the same one, or the phone sees no hub and the hub sees no phone actions — with no error on either side.

- **Which build lands where.** The Mac's Developer ID build is Production (`com.apple.developer.icloud-container-environment` = `Production`, `aps-environment` = `production`, from `scripts/Watchtower-cloud.entitlements`). The phone run from Xcode (the Debug configuration, `WatchtowerMobile/WatchtowerMobile.entitlements`) is Development; a TestFlight or App Store phone build is Production.
- **Pairs that work.** A Developer ID Mac ↔ a TestFlight/App Store phone (Production). A Development-signed Mac ↔ a phone run from Xcode (Development). A Developer ID Mac with an Xcode-run phone never meets.
- **A Development Mac.** Build with an Apple Development identity, a macOS development provisioning profile for `com.watchtower.desktop` with the container, CloudKit and Push Notifications, and `WATCHTOWER_CLOUDKIT_ENV=Development` (environment or build profile). `build-app.sh` then rewrites `icloud-container-environment` to `Development` and `aps-environment` to `development` in the temporary signing copy; the repo's entitlements file stays Production. Any other value than `Production` or `Development` fails the build.
- **One app ID per sidecar.** The hub identity lives in the data protection keychain under the app ID, but the sidecar folder (`~/Library/Application Support/Watchtower/MobileHub/`) is fixed. Two signed builds with different app IDs on one Mac therefore cut each other. Each one finds the other's sidecar with an identity it cannot read (`identityWasRecreated` / `storedGroupKeysUnreadable`) and runs the hard cut: zones deleted, every phone unlinked. Same-app-ID flavours (Developer ID and a Development-environment build) share the identity and do not cut.
- **Early profile check.** Besides the App ID and the container, the profile must grant CloudKit (`com.apple.developer.icloud-services` lists `CloudKit`, or is `*`), the chosen environment (`com.apple.developer.icloud-container-environment` lists it; a Developer ID profile lists Production only), and an `aps-environment` equal to the chosen one (`production` / `development`). Otherwise the build fails before the Swift build, naming the missing grant.
- **Before the first Production run**, deploy the schema from Development to Production in the CloudKit Console (Deploy Schema Changes): Production refuses record types it has not been given, and records are created in Development first by any Development run. Only the Console can do it: `cktool import-schema` and `validate-schema` answer `endpoint not applicable in the environment 'production'` (checked 2026-10-12). `make cloudkit-check` reports what is missing.

## Mac hub

### Hub skeleton and heartbeat (A6, A7; multi-hub F1)

- **Opt-in.** `Constants.mobileSyncEnabledKey` (`mobileSyncEnabled`, off by default in every flavor). `AppState.initMobileHub(dbPool:)` runs at the end of `initWorkbenches`: it stops and drops the previous hub, and builds a new one only while the toggle is on, because the hub holds the centers `initWorkbenches` replaces. With the toggle off nothing is opened or written. `AppState.setMobileSyncEnabled(_:)` starts or stops the same hub object. A replaced hub is `dispose()`d (it never starts again, even from a queued start), and the new one starts only after the old one's teardown has drained.
- **Storage.** `MobileHubStorage` opens `~/Library/Application Support/Watchtower/MobileHub/` (`transport.db` behind the CloudKit transport, `hubstate.db` = `HubSyncState`) once per app run; hub rebuilds and toggles reuse it, so two sync engines never drive one store. A failure to open it is kept in `AppState.mobileHubInitError` and no hub is built.
- **`MobileHubService`** (`@MainActor`): starts the transport, gates on iCloud availability (`.unavailable` runs no loop and re-probes every 600 s), then runs the publisher loop and the relay loop. Relay cadence: 3 s while a QR code is open (`linkCenter.openCode`, so a scanning phone's `hello` is read and its four-digit code shown within seconds) or a phone action or `device` record was handled in the last 300 s, otherwise 30 s. Each cycle's `transport.pull()` reaches the server on its own (the transport's zone poll, below), so the cadence does not depend on push delivery. Pushes: `AppState.initMobileHub` calls `MobileHubStorage.registerForPushes` once per run with the live storage (`NSApplication.registerForRemoteNotifications()` when `CloudKitTransport.entitlementPresent()`; silent pushes need no notification permission, so no prompt), and `TrayAppDelegate.application(_:didReceiveRemoteNotification:)` turns a CloudKit push into `MobileHubService.wakeRelay()`: the relay loop's wait ends and the next cycle runs now (a wake during a cycle skips the next wait; no-op while the loop is not running). Registration success or failure is logged under the `CloudKitPush` category. `stop()` stops the transport first (so a hung fetch is cancelled), then lets the cancelled relay loop end: each record's work runs in an unstructured task that does not inherit the loop's cancellation, so a stop takes effect only between two records and never turns an apply into a false `write_failed`. The transport stop (`HubTransport.stop()`; `CloudKitTransport.stop()` cancels and drops the engine, so a turned-off hub neither fetches nor sends); the next `start()` awaits that teardown. An account-change reset wipes the slice hashes and the relay change token but keeps the exactly-once ledger: relay record names are stable per action (opaque wire names since E2E), and on a same-Apple-ID sign-out/sign-in the re-fetched zone still holds actions whose echo never landed. A record CloudKit rejects even alone (`.limitExceeded`) has its `slice_state` hash cleared.
- **`SlicePublisher`.** Every DataZone save becomes a silent push to the phone, so the cadences are what the phone's battery pays for (board #724, owner decision Q3). One loop serves every cadence, so two sends never interleave (`takeDueCycle(now:)` picks the next `Cycle`, `run(_:)` runs it): a full hash diff every 30 s over every kind — the only cycle that publishes the normal kinds, so they cost at most one save batch per 30 s, sent when CKSyncEngine schedules it; a diff of the urgent kinds alone every 10 s (`urgentKinds`: `owner_ask`, `ask_alert` and `device_grant`, which the phone's link flow waits 60 s for — this tick catches Go's writes, which no nudge sees); and the fast lane — `nudge(kinds:)` keeps only the urgent kinds (any other kind waits for the full tick), coalesces them for 1 s, then diffs only them, with at least 2 s between two fast sends. Any cycle that saved or deleted an urgent record then calls `sendNow` (below); the normal records pending in the engine go with that send, and that send counts as a fast send for the 2 s spacing. The full and urgent ticks count from the end of the cycle that took them, so a slow cycle never runs back to back. **No phone, no publishing:** while `HubSyncState.hasLinkedDevice()` is false (`MobileLinkCenter.hasLinkedPhone`; an unreadable sidecar counts as linked, logged once per failure streak and again when it reads) a cycle diffs only `device_grant` (`unlinkedKinds`, the link flow's answer; the heartbeat goes on, linking needs it), and the first cycle that sees a phone linked runs in two passes: the urgent kinds alone, sent at once — so the new phone's grant goes out alone, ahead of the backlog — then every kind. Every cycle diffs the urgent kinds first (`publishOrder`), since the transport sends pending records oldest first. Until such a full pass runs to its end (a save failure or a mid-cycle reset), the next cycle does both passes again. The git refresher and both session report runners skip their passes on the same gate (`isActive`), so no CLI runs without a phone. **No generic "kind unchanged" skip** (board #724 looked for one): `PRAGMA data_version` is per connection and database-wide (the daemon and the Desktop's own writes, e.g. owner presence every 30 s, would bump it on nearly every tick), SQLite keeps no per-table change counter across processes, and a `max(updated_at)`/`count(*)` fingerprint misses writers that do not stamp `updated_at` and deletions in joined tables. Most kinds also read inputs outside the DB that must invalidate them (session liveness, the git and report-summary caches, the sidecar, `workbench.default_agent`, the clock for the archive, calendar and report windows). So each tick still projects every kind and `SliceDiff` sends only changed records; the one gated source is `meeting_transcript`, per row, by its light key (below), where the cost was. `sliceSQL` is empty: every published kind is a capped projection behind a `SliceSource` (`kind`, `records(_ db: Database) -> [SliceRecord]`, the kind's whole window — a record missing from it is deleted from the zone). Payload guard: a record whose sealed envelope is over `maxPayloadBytes = 900_000` (`Envelope.maxBytes`; padding is clipped to fit, D12) is skipped and not hashed, with a warning throttled per (record name, payload hash); exactly 900_000 bytes publishes. A mid-cycle account reset (generation bump) aborts the cycle before stale hashes are recorded.
- **`RelayProcessor`** (background): decode, exactly-once, age, echoes. It reads RelayZone through the hub's `SecureTransport`, so only records a linked phone's relay key opened (and the hub's own echoes) reach it, and every echo is sealed to the authenticated sender (see End-to-end encryption). `relay_processed(record_name, phase, outcome, updated_at)` is the ledger: a `done` record is skipped; a non-idempotent kind (`board_comment_add`, `board_comment_reply`, `board_target_create`, `session_start`, `session_input`, `session_finish_request`) is claimed `begun` atomically (`INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`) before its handler and marked `done` after its echo; a record a later pass finds still `begun` is echoed `failed` / `outcome_unknown` and never re-applied. Age: older than 7 days after `created_at` → `expired` (24 h for `session_input`, `session_finish_request`, `session_start`). `session_start`, `session_input` and `board_target_create` are echoed `received` before any work. `probe` is answered by the hub itself (`applied`, `{nonce, hub_id}`; no nonce → `invalid_params`). `target_done`, `target_snooze`, `task_create` and every kind without a registered handler fail `unsupported_in_poc`; a throwing handler fails `write_failed`. Passes are single-flight across every processor sharing the sidecar (`RelayPassGate`, also covering hygiene), and cancellation is checked between records. A pass handles at most 200 records and reports the rest; the service re-runs at once until the backlog is empty (a long Mac sleep drains in one cycle), `relayBacklog` counts down, and the change token is persisted only when a pass leaves nothing behind. Daily hygiene deletes action and recording-upload records older than 7 days (an unhandled pending one waits for its `expired` echo), then sweeps the local buffer and prunes the ledger one day past that window.
- **`MobileHubCommandDispatcher`** (`@MainActor`): `register(_ kind:handler:)` with `@MainActor (ActionRequestPayload) async throws -> ActionOutcome`. Empty in A; B registers its workbench handlers in `AppState.buildMobileHub`. Rules for handlers: a handler must never call back into the relay processor (`processOnce`, `runHygieneIfDue`) — the pass gate is not re-entrant, so the pass would wait on itself forever; and each handler owns its timeout — a hub stop (and a rebuilt hub's start) waits for the record being applied, so a hung PTY write or DB call would hang them.
- **Hub id.** `HubSyncState.ensureHubID()` creates one UUID per install in `hub_meta` (`hub_id`), kept across account resets.
- **Heartbeat** (spec §4.1, multi-hub spec §3.2). `HubIdentity` keeps `hub_id` and `enabled_at` (set by the first enable, kept across relaunches, off/on and account resets) in `hub_meta`. `enable()` (= `start()`) writes this hub's heartbeat (its own DataZone, record `heartbeat`, every field of `HeartbeatPayload`, stamped with this Mac's clock; sealed and signed like every DataZone record, under its fixed name) after the availability gate; a failed first write leaves the hub `.unavailable` and re-probing. The heartbeat loop rewrites it every 15 min (`heartbeatInterval`, 900 s since board #724, owner decision Q2; was 300 s): each write is a silent push to the phone, which shows the Mac offline after 35 min (`RelayFeed.heartbeatStaleAfter`), so one missed write still reads online. Between two writes the loop checks every 60 s (`defaultHeartbeatFactsCheckInterval`) whether the accounts' rows or `sharing` differ from what the last write carried (`refreshHeartbeatIfFactsChanged`): a change rewrites the heartbeat at once and restarts the 15 min interval, so the phone's Settings does not wait for the tick; no change writes nothing. One attempt per change: a failed rewrite is logged and left to the next tick, never counted toward `maxTickFailures`. The accounts read is async (`HubHostInfo.accounts`; the live one through the pool's async read), so the check never blocks the main actor. A failed accounts read (`HubHostInfo.accounts` throws) is not an empty list: the check skips (no change, no write, no push), and a tick written meanwhile carries the accounts of the last write. Three failed writes in a row (`maxTickFailures`, 45 min — the phone has shown the Mac offline before the hub gives up) stop the hub as `.unavailable` and the reprobe loop restarts it. No other hub's heartbeat is read and there is no take over: each Mac publishes into its own zones (I-1 replaced, multi-hub spec §2). Old `heartbeat_cursor` / `heartbeat_seen` rows in `hub_meta` are dead: nothing reads them. `accounts[]` is `HubAccountRows` — kind, label (falls back to team/email/site name, ≤ 80) and status of Slack, Google and Jira rows, removed ones left out, ≤ 20; no other column is read. `mac_name` is `Host.current().localizedName`, cut to 60 grapheme clusters; `flavor` is `default` for a build with no `WTBuildFlavor` or `dev`, `corp` for any other; `owner_user` is looked up once (bounded by `cloudTimeout`, 30 s) through `MobileHubStorage.ownerUser` and looked up again after the transport's account reset (an iCloud sign-out or switch: `MobileHubService.accountChanged()`, which also tells the link center through `onAccountChanged`; a lookup still running then is discarded); `sharing` is set by the link center (`none` until then).
- **Zone lifecycle (multi-hub F1)** (multi-hub spec §3.3, §3.6):
  - **First start on the per-hub build.** The transport store (bound to no hub) wipes itself, and `adoptPerHubZones` wipes the sidecar's sync state once (`hub_meta` `per_hub_zones` = `1`): slice hashes and the relay change token go, the exactly-once ledger stays. A sidecar that cannot be read or written leaves the hub `.unavailable` and re-probing. After the availability gate, `removeLegacyZonesIfDue` deletes the fixed-name `DataZone`/`RelayZone` only when they hold this hub's heartbeat or none (`HubIdentity.legacyZonesAreOurs`; another Mac's or an unreadable one is kept). The outcome is stored in `legacy_zones_checked` (`none_found` | `deleted` | `kept`), so the check runs once. It is bounded by `cloudTimeout`, never blocks the hub, and a failure or timeout leaves the marker unset, so the next start checks again.
  - **Settings off.** `disable()` stops the hub, sets `hub_zones_delete_pending` in `hub_meta`, then deletes its two zones (with their shares) through `HubTransport.deleteHubZones()`, bounded by `cloudTimeout`. On success it calls `onZonesDeleted` (the link center forgets codes and phones; it throws when the phones could not be cleared), wipes the sync state and clears the marker. A delete that was definitely refused before anything was sent or applied clears the marker, and zones, sync state and phones stay consistent; the next start comes back on the existing zones. "Definitely refused" means `HubZoneError.notHub`/`.engineRunning`/`.noEntitlement`, or a CKError of `.networkUnavailable`, `.notAuthenticated`, `.permissionFailure`, `.quotaExceeded`, `.missingEntitlement`, `.badContainer`, `.managedAccountRestricted`, `.incompatibleVersion`, `.accountTemporarilyUnavailable` or `.invalidArguments` (`MobileHubService.isDefinitelyRefused`). Any other error (`.networkFailure`, `.serverResponseLost`, `.serviceUnavailable`, throttling, `.serverRejectedRequest`, a non-CloudKit error) may have landed and is handled like a timeout. A delete that removed something but did not finish — one zone gone while the other failed (`CKDatabaseZoneAdmin` throws `PartialZoneDeleteError`), or both gone and the transport-store wipe failed — throws `HubZoneError.partlyDeleted` and, like a timeout, keeps the marker. A delete that times out, or ends in such an open error, is outcome-unknown: the marker stays ("iCloud didn't confirm…") and a timed-out one is not cancelled. The delete is serialized on the transport (`CloudKitTransport.hubZoneDelete`), which lives for the app run with `MobileHubStorage`: a second `deleteHubZones` joins the running one (its outcome, no second server delete) and `transport.start()` waits for it — from this hub object or one an `initWorkbenches` re-run built after it — and the delete re-checks that no engine runs before it wipes. The next `start()`, before the per-hub adoption and before `transport.start()`, deletes again (a missing zone counts as deleted), then forgets the phones, wipes and clears the marker. If that does not settle within `cloudTimeout`, the hub stays `.unavailable` with no engine running and the reprobe loop retries. A phone forget or wipe that fails after a delete also keeps the marker, so the next start redoes it. A sidecar that cannot record the marker keeps the zones (no delete runs). Anything that did not go through shows in `hubOffProblem`, under the Settings → Mobile toggle while it is off.
  - **E2E hard cut (`e2e_v1`, mobile E2E spec §2.8, D13).** With hub keys (`keyring` non-nil), `start()` checks `hub_meta.e2e_v1` before the pending zone delete (`markE2ECutIfDue`). The cut is due when the marker is absent on a hub that ran before (`enabled_at` set or a phone linked; a hub that never ran gets `1` straight away), when it is `pending` (an interrupted cut), or when it is `1` but `HubKeyring.identityWasRecreated` is true or the stored `K_meta`/current epoch no longer open (`storedGroupKeysUnreadable`, the crash window between creating an identity and marking the cut). A due cut sets `hub_zones_delete_pending` and `e2e_v1 = pending` in one transaction and rides the Settings-off path above: the hub's two zones go with their shares, `onZonesDeleted` forgets the phones and the group keys, the sync state (slice hashes included) is wiped. Only then `finishE2ECut` removes the staged asset files, resets the group keys once more (also clears `identityWasRecreated`) and writes `e2e_v1 = 1`; `ensureGroupKeys` follows. A failure or timeout keeps both markers (hub `.unavailable`, the reprobe loop retries), a crash before `1` reruns the cut (a missing zone counts as deleted). Every pre-E2E phone re-pairs once. `removeLegacyZones` stays for the single-hub build's fixed names only. A damaged Keychain identity (`KeyStoreError.corrupt` on two reads in a row: a crash between the three adds, an item that does not parse) is deleted and recreated in `HubKeyring.init`, logged once, and counts as recreated. "Ran before" means `enabled_at`, a linked phone, a `slice_state` row or `per_hub_zones`; `finishE2ECut` also clears the link state itself, so `1` never sits beside a pre-E2E device row. Error-level logs mark only a real loss (sealed keys under a lost identity); the first E2E start over pre-E2E phones logs a notice. `make cloudkit-check` also fails while Production lacks `WatchtowerEnvelope` or `WatchtowerSignal`.
  - **No cloud cleanup.** A reinstall that loses the sidecar leaves its zones behind (spec §3.3).
- Tests: `MobileHubServiceTests`, `RelayProcessorTests`, `SlicePublisherTests`, `HubHeartbeatTests` (on a stub transport over `InMemoryCloudTransport`).

### Linking, hub half (A8, E2E13, E2E14)

- **`MobileLinkCenter`** (`@MainActor`, `@Observable`; `MobileHubService.linkCenter`, built by `AppState.assembleMobileHub` only when `MobileHubStorage.shares` is set — the live storage has `CloudKitShareService`, a stub storage none). One center per app run (`AppState.mobileLinkCenter`, `MobileLinkCenter.forRun`): an `initWorkbenches` re-run attaches the same center to the rebuilt hub, so a QR on screen keeps its code and its public link; every other `attach` runs the `.restart` close (a link an earlier run left open, a pending sweep). Its slice nudges go to the attached hub's publisher. `issueCode()` wakes the hub's relay loop (`wakeRelay()`), so the 3 s cadence starts at once instead of after the idle wait; a code that cannot be stored after its link opened closes that link (`.issueFailed`) and rethrows. The iCloud user (the QR's `owner_user`) is cached until the account changes: `accountChanged()` forgets it, reports `sharing` none (the new account has no shares yet) and closes an open code (`.accountChanged`); a code still being issued then is closed instead of shown (`MobileLinkError.accountChanged`), and the sheet says "This Mac's iCloud account changed. Show a new code." rather than expired. The center keeps the last `sharing` it reported and re-applies it to a rebuilt hub. The QR sheet drops a second **New code** while one is being issued. Observable state: `openCode` (the QR on screen), `pendingLinks` (phones whose hello waits for the owner's Link or Refuse, with their four-digit codes, oldest first; kept on the center and in the sidecar, so they survive the sheet) and `devices` (the linked phones, oldest link first). Settings → Mobile (A9) uses `accountAvailability()`, `issueCode()`, `confirmLink(deviceID:sas:)`, `refuseLink(deviceID:)`, `closeLink(reason: .sheetClosed)`, `remove(deviceID:)`, `setTypingAllowed(_:deviceID:)` and `setStartSessionsAllowed(_:deviceID:)`. The center holds the hub's `HubKeyring` (nil: no QR is issued).
- **`ShareService`** is the CloudKit seam: account status, `ensureShares()` (the two zone-wide `CKShare(recordZoneID:)`, created on first use and reused), `setPublicLink(open:)` (DataZone `.readOnly`, RelayZone `.readWrite`, or `.none`), `participants(in:)`, `removeParticipants(in:where:)`, `deleteShares()`. `CloudKitShareService` runs on the Mac user's private database; tests use `FakeShareService`.
- **`issueCode()`** refuses without hub keys (`MobileLinkError.noKeys`), without iCloud (`MobileLinkError.account(.noAccount / .restricted / .unavailable)`, sentences per spec §2.3) or without the Mac's user record name, and then creates nothing. The QR carries the hub's public keys (`LinkPayload` v3). Otherwise it creates or reuses the shares, marks `share_sweep_pending` in `hub_meta`, opens the public link and stores the code in `link_codes(nonce, issued_at, exp, used_by_device, used_at)` (`exp = iat + 600`, the newest 50 kept). A new code retires every unused code still open (its `exp` set to just before the new one's issue), so only the newest code links; a phone scanning the old QR reads `expired_code`. A share failure leaves `data_share` / `relay_share` out of the QR (only a same-Apple-ID phone can link then) and sets the heartbeat's `sharing` to `unavailable`; a success sets `available`.
- **`closeLink(reason:)`** runs on use, at expiry (a timer at `exp`; `closeLinkIfExpired()`), on `.sheetClosed`, on `.issueFailed` (the code could not be stored after its link opened) and on `.accountChanged`. It first refuses every request still waiting (F17): `used_code` after a link, `expired_code` at expiry, `not_confirmed` otherwise. Then it sets `publicPermission = .none`, then removes every participant whose iCloud user is not a linked `shared` phone (spec primary path; the F2 fallback is not built). The sweep runs only while `share_sweep_pending` is set (the link was opened, or a Remove failed to drop its participant), so no share call is made otherwise; a failed sweep keeps the mark and is retried in the background after 1, 5, 15 and 60 minutes (`sweepRetryDelays`, one retry task at a time, `sweepRetry`; it stops once the sweep goes through, the mark is cleared, or a QR is on screen, and `MobileHubService.stop()` cancels it through `cancelSweepRetry()`; target 153), then by the next close, including `.restart` at each hub build (a crash with the QR shown) — except a rebuild while a QR is on screen, which keeps that code's link. The expiry timer detaches itself before it closes, so the close is never cancelled by its own timer.
- **Bounds.** Every share call runs under `shareTimeout` (`MobileHubService.defaultCloudTimeout`, 30 s; a call still running is cancelled and left to end): offline CloudKit waits for connectivity, and `handleDevice` runs inside the relay pass, which a hub stop waits for. The close after a link runs outside the pass (`closeAfterLink`). `CloudKitShareService` checks the iCloud entitlement first: without it (an unsigned dev build) the account reads `.unavailable` and no `CKContainer` is touched.
- **`handleHello(_:)`** decides a link request (the sealed `hello-<device_id>` the relay hands over unopened, F4). It is described step by step under End-to-end encryption → Pairing with the code. In short:
  - it opens the hello under a kept code's nonce and checks the signature, D7 and the creator (in `shared` scope `creatorUserRecordName == user_record_name`, and that user has accepted both shares; in `private` scope the Mac's own user, with `CKCurrentUserDefaultName` or unknown passing);
  - then the nonce decides (`HubSyncState.LinkDecision`): a known, unused code with `exp ≥ now` saves the request in `pending_devices` with its keys and code and publishes `awaiting_confirmation`; a code used by another device or expired gets a refusal keybox (`used_code`, `expired_code`) from `link_refusals`; an unknown code gets no answer;
  - `confirmLink(deviceID:sas:)` links (`confirmPendingDevice`, over `linkDevice`, marks the code used and upserts `devices` with the phone's public keys and nonce in one transaction, keeping the grants of a re-linking phone) and closes the link; the hello's `typing_requested` rides in the waiting request (`pending_devices.typing_requested`), and a phone linked with it on while typing is not allowed asks the owner (`onTypingRequested`, spec §10, PROJ-16);
  - `refuseLink` refuses with `not_confirmed`.
  
  A linked phone keeps its link whatever code it sends later, and a removed one is never re-linked by its old nonce. Names are clipped to 60 grapheme clusters.
- **`handleDevice(_:)`**: the relay hands it every `device` record that opened under a linked phone's relay key and names that phone (`RelayProcessor.DeviceRecords`, unbudgeted). A record that carries a nonce changes nothing: a link needs a hello. A rewrite without a nonce is accepted only when its scope is the linked one and its creator passes both the creator check above and `LinkedDevice.accepts(creator:)`, kept as defense in depth. Then:
  - `unlinked: true` removes the phone exactly as Remove does, rotation included. In `shared` scope the phone has already left the shares, so a missing participant is no error; a share failure is logged and swept by the next close, never failing the relay pass.
  - Otherwise its `start_sessions` is mirrored into `start_sessions_allowed` (`setStartSessionsAllowed`, only when it differs, so `decided_at` moves only on a change), and its `typing_requested` is stored (`setTypingRequested`; never a grant): turned on while typing is not allowed, the owner is asked (`onTypingRequested`: Settings → Mobile shows it, and a macOS notice asks while the app is in the background, OD-2); turned off while allowed, the grant goes (a revoke from the phone's side, spec §10).
  - Revoke, Remove and the phone's own revoke or unlink tell `onTypingRevoked`, which fails the phone's held lines (PROJ-16).
- **`devices(device_id, name, scope, user_record_name, linked_at, typing_allowed, start_sessions_allowed, decided_at, typing_requested, signing_key, agreement_key, link_nonce, last_seq)`** in `hubstate.db` is the device gate's table (`typing_requested` and the E2E columns are added in place on an older sidecar); the E2E columns hold public keys and the QR nonce text, never a secret. Its E2E siblings are `pending_devices` (waiting requests, with their `sas`), `revoked_devices` (a removed phone's agreement key, name and scope, for its `revoked` keybox), `link_refusals.agreement_key` and `relay_seen` (see `HubSyncState+Keys.swift`). `remove(deviceID:)` deletes the row and rotates the epoch in one transaction (`HubKeyring.rotate(reason: .removed)`; a failed rotation removes nothing and throws). The gate refuses the phone at once, its relay keys are dropped, and its keybox reads `revoked` for 7 days (D9). Then, in `shared` scope, removes that iCloud user from both shares unless another linked phone is on it; if that removal fails it sets `share_sweep_pending` (the next close sweeps the participant) and rethrows the removal's error (a mark that cannot be written is only logged). Every mark bumps the center's sweep epoch, so a close suspended meanwhile never resets it to `0`. The grant setters stamp `decided_at`. `SessionStartStopHandlers` reads the phone's grants through `MobileLinkCenter.sessionGrant` (`denied` when the row is missing or unreadable).
- **`DeviceGrantSlice`** publishes each phone's `Keybox` under the plain name `device_grant-<device_id>` (wire name `keybox-<device_id>`), sealed to that phone's agreement key and signed by `hub_sig`. Precedence:
  1. linked phones: `linked: true`, their grants and the epoch keys;
  2. pending requests: `awaiting_confirmation`, no keys;
  3. refusals, for an hour (`decided_at` = the refusal's time, so a second identical refusal still changes the payload and the phone reads it as an answer);
  4. removed phones: `revoked: true` for 7 days.
  
  Every linked phone's keybox is published, never capped (#64: the phone reads a deleted grant as its removal); the pending requests, refusals and revoked phones fill the room left under 20, in that order, latest first. Keys are never wrapped for a device that is not linked. A row without a usable recipient key is skipped alone.
- **Device gate** (spec §5.2 rule 4) in `RelayProcessor`: an action (the `probe` included) or a recording upload is applied only when:
  - the phone whose relay key opened it is its `device_id`;
  - that phone is in `devices`;
  - `LinkedDevice.accepts(creator:)` holds (`shared`: the record's creator equals the phone's `user_record_name`; `private`: unknown, `CKCurrentUserDefaultName` or that name).
  
  The key is the gate; the creator check is defense in depth. Anything else that opened is echoed `failed` / `device_not_linked` to its authenticated sender and never dispatched. A record that opens under no linked phone's key is dropped, never echoed. The creator rides on `CloudRecord.creatorUserRecordName` (see Transport).
- **Hub off:** the hub's zones are deleted when it is turned off (see **Zone lifecycle** under the hub skeleton). `MobileHubService.onZonesDeleted` then calls `MobileLinkCenter.hubZonesDeleted()`, which clears the link state (`link_codes`, `devices`, `link_refusals`, the pending and revoked devices, and `held_session_lines`: their records went with the zones) and the group keys (`HubKeyring.resetGroupKeys`: the next pairing starts on a fresh `K_meta` and epoch 1) and sets `sharing` to `none` (the shares went with the zones); the next QR creates new shares. `CloudKitShareService(hubID:)` shares this hub's own two zones.
- Tests: `MobileLinkCenterTests` (fake share service, fake clock; hellos, codes, the two-hellos race, rotation on Remove); the gate also in `RelayProcessorRecordingUploadTests`. The creator-gate tests stay, each with a key-auth twin. Suites that drive actions through the relay link a test phone first (`HubSyncState.linkTestDevice`).

- **`workbench`.** The ≤ 100 workbenches with the latest session activity (`WorkbenchSlice.publishedWorkbenches`, over `WorkbenchQueries.switcherSummaries`); the target and comment slices publish only these boards. Everything that needs only which workbenches are published (the target, comment, session, ask and alert slices, the git refresher, the summary runner) reads `publishedWorkbenchRefs` instead: id and name from one light query, the same set and order (board #724; a test pins the equality). `folder_display` is `WorkbenchBranchPresentation.displayPath` (the Mac's own: the home prefix becomes `~`, a path that merely starts with the same letters is not under home, anything else as-is), cut at 300. Workbenches order by parsed `last_session_activity`. `done_targets` counts done targets the archive view leaves unarchived. `targets_more` counts board targets outside the target window. `session_counts` counts the published `terminal_session` records per state (`TerminalSessionSlice.sessionCounts`; `working` counts working and running, `not_running` counts not started).
- **Git status.** `WorkbenchGitRefresher` runs `watchtower workbench git status --workbench N --json` (`WorkbenchCLI.gitStatus`, PROJ-10's git; the hub never runs git) for every published workbench (listed by `publishedWorkbenchRefs` at each 5 s wake, no board counts), once every 120 s, and on `sessionStateChanged(workbenchID:)` at most once per 30 s. Each run is bounded (20 s). A failed, timed-out or `status_ok: false` run keeps the last value. A detached HEAD publishes `detached: true` and an empty branch. A changed value publishes with the `workbench` kind on the next 30 s full tick. The refresher is a `HubCompanion`: `MobileHubService` starts it with the publisher and stops it with the hub, off the main actor. A stop ends the pass in flight: no workbench gets a CLI run after it, and the run in flight stores nothing. Without a workbench CLI no refresher is built, and the branch stays "". The fast lane calls `sessionStateChanged` for the workbench of every session whose state changed. A stop mid-pass puts back the cadence state of every workbench the pass did not finish (the one in flight included), so a restart runs them at once and keeps their session-change requests.
- **Target window** (`WorkbenchTargetWindow`, one SQL read of `workbench_target_archive` for all boards; target, comment and workbench slices share it). Board targets only (`project_id IS NOT NULL`, PROJ-01). Per workbench it holds every non-archived target, ≤ 2000, newest `updated_at` first, plus archived targets whose last close is within 90 days, ≤ 500, newest close first. An archived target's last close is its subtree's newest close (a node's close is its newest status move, else `updated_at`), as the view decides the archive. A group is thus never older than its children, and on equal close times the larger subtree ranks first, so an archived child's `parent_id` names a published group. The archive verdict is the view's (PROJ-15, read only).
- **`workbench_target`.** Fields come from `WorkbenchQueries.board`: the tree (`parent_id`, nil for a root or an orphan, and `children_count`), `open_comments`, `unread_for_owner`. `open_asks` counts open asks on the target. `session_ids` holds `claude` sessions with this `target_id` or linked through `terminal_session_targets`, the most recently active first, ≤ 20 with `session_ids_more`. `last_status_at`/`_actor` come from the newest `target_status_history` row. `work_on_prompt` is `TerminalLaunch.workOnTargetPrompt` with the current skill name: the folder's install status lives on the main actor, so a folder still on the legacy skill name gets a prefill naming the current one.
- **`workbench_comment`.** Comments whose target (a reply naming only its parent counts toward its root's target) is published, the newest 200 per target. `read` means `read_at != ''`.
- **Cost.** Each tick evaluates the archive view once per slice, plus once per published workbench through `board()`. The view itself is computed over every board's targets (PROJ-15 v1 note), so the cost grows with workbenches × targets.
- **A deleted workbench** drops out of every source, so the next diff deletes its workbench, target and comment records (review focus 1).
- Tests: `WorkbenchSliceTests`, `WorkbenchTargetSliceTests`, `SliceClipTests`, plus `MobileHubServiceTests.testCompanionsRunOnlyWhileTheHubPublishes`.

### Sessions and the fast lane (B3)

- **`terminal_session`** (`TerminalSessionSlice`): the `claude` sessions of the published workbenches, every live one plus the newest 50 by `last_active_at` per workbench. Shell sessions and sessions of a workbench outside the 100 are not published.
- **States are the Mac's.** The slice reads `fetchAgentStates` and resolves it with `SessionAgentStatus.resolve` and `SessionSwitcherPresentation.state(of:)`, the center's and the switcher's own functions, against a copy of `TerminalCenter.liveIDs` and `startedAt` and of the center's `displayOver` (`SessionLivenessBox`, written by the fast lane on the main actor), so a background agent count the Mac shows over after two failed staleness probes (#411 §10) goes out as Stopped too. The count is judged at the slice's own clock, so a zero count whose 120 s grace runs out reaches the phone at the next 30 s full tick with no write or nudge; the center's 1 s poll sees the same change and nudges the fast lane's ask kinds only (`FastLane.stateChangeKinds`, board #724). `SessionStatePresentation` renders the tone, glyph (`""` for none), ring and caption. The caption carries no age (the Mac's "Not running · 5m"), so a record does not change every minute. `open_asks`/`oldest_ask_id` come from the resolved state; `closed_asks` is `OwnerAskQueries.closedCounts`, the count behind the Mac's "▸ N closed". `state_at` is the trusted hook stamp, else a finished session's `finished_at`. `agent_error` is set only on a failed state.
- **Report fields.** `SessionReportSummaryRunner` runs `watchtower workbench session-report --workbench N --summary --json` every 60 s for each workbench with a live session, and on a session state change of a workbench at most once per 5 s. Each run is bounded at 20 s, runs off the main actor and never touches the relay processor. A failed, undecodable or hung run keeps the last values; the `report_*` keys are absent before the first good run. A changed summary publishes with `terminal_session` on the next 30 s full tick. A workbench that leaves the published list is forgotten with its summaries and any pending request. It is a `HubCompanion`, and a stop mid-pass keeps the unfinished workbenches due, as with the git refresher.
- **Fast lane** (`FastLane`, main actor, a `HubCompanion`). It chains onto `SessionAgentStateCenter.onChange` and `onRead`: the closures `WorkbenchesViewModel` set (the PROJ-12 held-answer delivery) still run first, and a stop puts them back. A change asks the git refresher and the summary runner to refresh the changed sessions' workbenches and tells the session report runner (B6), and nudges `owner_ask` and `ask_alert` (`stateChangeKinds`): an agent asking usually flips its session's state, so an ask Go wrote reaches the phone in 1–2 s rather than on the 10 s urgent tick. The session kinds themselves ride the next 30 s full tick (board #724, owner decision Q3 — a session's state reaches the phone within 30 s, not 1–2 s). GRDB `ValueObservation` over `owner_asks` alone nudges `owner_ask` and `ask_alert` (`observedTables`); no other table is observed, since a nudge of a normal kind cannot bring its full tick forward. It sees only the Desktop's own writes; a Go ask with no state change arrives on the 10 s urgent tick. The hub starts its companions (the fast lane's liveness copy among them) before the publisher, so the first full cycle never publishes live sessions as not running. The publisher decides how fast: only the urgent kinds (asks, ask alerts, link grants) take the fast send, and any other kind a caller nudges is dropped. The publisher's one loop does the coalescing (1 s window, ≥ 2 s between fast sends); there is no second send loop. A cycle that saved or deleted an urgent record then calls `HubTransport.sendNow()` (`CloudKitTransport.sendNow()`, the `resend()` path: `CKSyncEngine.sendChanges()` at once; a no-op while stopped, unlinked, paused for quota or inside a server-requested wait). The ≥ 2 s spacing and the 10 s urgent tick bound the call rate; a cycle that saved only normal records, and an empty diff, leave the timing to CKSyncEngine (`save` enqueues and nudges the engine, whose automatic send carries it).
- Tests: `TerminalSessionSliceTests`, `FastLaneTests` (Needs approval within one window plus one send; a 30-session storm sends at most 30 times, ≥ 2 s apart, ending on the last DB state, review focus 5), `WorkbenchSliceTests.testAStopMidPassKeepsTheUnfinishedWorkbenchesDue`.

### Owner asks and ask alerts (B4)

- **`owner_ask`** (`OwnerAskSlice`): every open ask of a published workbench, plus its answered, delivered and withdrawn asks with `created_at` in the last 7 days, the newest 50 per workbench. Rows are read raw, not through Core's `OwnerAsk(row:)`, so an ask the Mac's card decoder refuses (a question with 1 or 5 options) is still published. `withdrawn_reason` is absent unless set. Caps: `title` 200, `summary`/`changes` 4000, `doc_path` 300 (`SliceClip`). `payload` is the stored JSON as an object (`JSONValue`); over 64 KiB serialized it is dropped with `payload_clipped: true`. `answer` (closed asks only) is the stored `OwnerAskAnswer` object, dropped over 64 KiB.
- **Snapshot.** `doc_snapshot` is sent on open review asks only. Up to 256 KiB of UTF-8 it is sent whole. Over that it is cut after the last newline before the cap, or at the cap's grapheme boundary when there is no newline. It then carries `doc_clipped: true` and `doc_bytes` (the full size). No `…` is added, because the phone anchors review comments on the text as shown.
- **`quick`** `{question_id, options: [{label, recommended}]}` is set only on a `question` ask whose payload was published and has exactly one question, `multi` false, 2–4 options and at least one recommended. Labels are trimmed the way the Mac's card decoder reads them.
- **`ask_alert`** (`AskAlertSlice`, `SliceKind.askAlert`, record `ask_alert-<ask id>`): the push trigger (design §7). Payload `{ask_id, workbench_id, workbench_name ≤ 60, session_id?, kind, title ≤ 120, quick}`, cut with `…` and no `_clipped` flags. An open ask with `created_at ≥ enabled_at` (`HubIdentity.ensureEnabledAt`) is remembered once in the sidecar's `alerted_asks(ask_id, at, generation)`. Its record is published while the ask is open, in the sync generation that wrote it, for up to 7 days after `at`. So a rebuilt hub or a full republish finds nothing new, and after an account reset (`wipeSyncState` keeps `alerted_asks` and bumps the generation) the new zone gets no alert for an old ask. A remembered row older than 7 days is forgotten once its ask is no longer open. While the ask stays open its row is kept, so it never alerts twice.
- **Sealing (E2E11, E2E18).** The record is sealed under `K_alert` (class `alert`, signed), the only key the phone's notification extension holds. When an alert first reaches the zone the publisher also writes a payload-free `WatchtowerSignal` `sig-<wire name of the alert>` (`AskAlertSignal`), deleted with the alert; a CloudKit subscription fires on it, since the alert's kind is no longer visible. A rotation reseals open alerts under the new `K_alert` without a new signal.
- **Fast lane.** A write to `owner_asks` also nudges `ask_alert`.
- Tests: `OwnerAskSliceTests`, `AskAlertSliceTests`, `FastLaneTests.testTheDesktopsOwnTableWritesNudgeTheirKinds`.

### Session reports and timelines (B6)

- **Window.** Both slices cover the `terminal_session` window's sessions that are live or had `last_active_at` in the last 7 days (`SessionReportSlice.window`). A session that leaves it loses both records.
- **`session_report`** (`SessionReportSlice`, record `session_report-<session id>`). `SessionReportRunner` (a `HubCompanion`, off the main actor like the summary runner) runs `watchtower workbench session-report --workbench N --session S --json` — `SessionReportCenter`'s argv — so PROJ-14 holds through the CLI's own scoping. Runs with `--no-network` (appended): every 120 s while the session is live, once when it has no stored report, and on a resolved state change no sooner than 5 s after its last run. Without `--no-network`: on the phone's `session_report_request`, at most once per 60 s per session (requests run first in a pass). Each run is bounded at 30 s (the CLI process is terminated on the cancel); a failed, undecodable or hung run keeps the stored report.
- **Caps.** `on_you` 30, `now` 20, `next` 20, `phases` 30 with ≤ 50 items, `prs` 10, each with `<list>_more` (`items_more` per phase); free texts 500 (no per-text `_clipped`, as the Kit mirror). Over 128 KiB the hub drops phase items oldest phase first (by `started_at`, a not-yet-started phase counting as the newest; a phase's last items first), then whole phases (`phases_more`), and sets `phases_clipped`.
- **Stored payloads.** The capped payload is kept in the sidecar's `session_reports` (`hubstate.db`), so a restart republishes the last good report instead of deleting the record until the next run. The record changes only when the payload bytes change; a stored change publishes with `session_report` and `session_timeline` on the next 30 s full tick.
- **`session_timeline`** (`SessionTimelineSlice`, record `session_timeline-<session id>`). Newest first, ≤ 100 (`milestones_more`), `text` ≤ 200 with `text_clipped`. Sources: `state` — the hub-observed transitions in the sidecar's `session_milestones` (only `state` rows, a CHECK; ≤ 100 per session, pruned after 14 days); `ask_opened`/`ask_answered`/`ask_withdrawn` from the session's `owner_asks` (a withdrawal has no stamp of its own: the superseding ask's `created_at`, else the ask's); `target_linked` from `terminal_session_targets.first_at`; `target_status` from `target_status_history` of the linked targets and the session's own target, from the session's `created_at` on; `phase` (`started_at`, `finished_at`) and `pr` (`merged_at`, else `checked_at`; branch entries skipped) from the stored report; `finished` from `finished_at` and `finish_summary`. A session with none of them has an empty list. No subagent events (OD-3), never transcript or terminal text (I-4).
- **State milestones.** The fast lane tells the runner of every state change and of a liveness change seen on an unchanged read (`sessionStatesChanged`, the same chain on `SessionAgentStateCenter`; no second observation loop). The next pass resolves the window's states as `terminal_session` does and stores a milestone when the text differs from the session's last one: "Started", "Working", "Waiting for you", "Needs approval", "Stopped", "Finished", "Error: …" (never-started sessions have none). A transition is stamped with the time the lane reported it, a first sighting (or a hub start) with the state's own time. A hub start resolves once. The pending-change stamp is taken before the window read, so a change reported while the read is out is resolved by the next pass. `session_timeline` changes from the Desktop's own `owner_asks` and `targets` writes publish on the next full tick.
- **`session_report_request`** (`SessionReportRequestHandler`, `MobileHub/Handlers/`, registered only when the CLI exists). Entity: the session id, `{}`. A session outside the report window (not a `claude` session of a published workbench, or neither live nor active in 7 days) → `not_found`, since no report would reach the phone; else `applied` `{}` at once — idempotent, a second request inside 60 s runs nothing more. The new report reaches the phone through the slice. Bounded at 10 s (one read).
- Tests: `SessionReportSliceTests`, `SessionTimelineSliceTests`; `FastLaneTests`, `TerminalSessionSliceTests` and the PROJ-14 guards stay unchanged.

### Ask answers from the phone, hub half (B8, B9)

- **Handler.** `AskAnswerHandler` (`MobileHub/Handlers/`, main actor) is registered on the dispatcher for `ask_answer` in `AppState.buildMobileHub`. Entity: the ask id. Params: `{workbench_id, answer: {verdict, answers, checklist, comments, note}}`, decoded into Core's `OwnerAskAnswer`. It loads the ask with `OwnerAskQueries.ask(id:projectID:)`. An ask that is missing or belongs to another workbench fails `not_found`, checked before the answer is decoded. A read error throws and is echoed `write_failed`, never treated as "not found". An ask that is no longer open fails `ask_not_open` before anything runs. Otherwise the handler calls `OwnerAsksViewModel.answer(_:with:)`, the Desktop's structured entry. That entry validates, does the guarded write, then delivers, holds and shows notices as a Desktop answer does (PROJ-12 unchanged), and discards a Desktop draft on success.
- **Echoes.** `.stored(d)` → `applied` with `result.delivery` ∈ `submitted|typed|held|queued|copied|no_session`. `.notOpen` → `ask_not_open` (an ask superseded or answered meanwhile). `.invalid` → `invalid_answer`, with Go's message: an unknown label, a review without a verdict, a blank comment, or an answer the decoder refuses (an unknown verdict or check state). `.failed` → `write_failed`. `.busy` (the owner's own Desktop answer is being saved at that moment) → `conflict`. Missing or mistyped params → `invalid_params`.
- **Exactly once.** `ask_answer` is idempotent, so the relay ledger does not claim it. A record is re-run when its echo never landed: the echo's save failed (a transient CloudKit or network error, so the record stays pending and is not `done`), or the hub stopped between the store and the echo. Right after a store, before the echo, the handler keeps the wire `delivery` in the sidecar's `ask_answer_deliveries(record_name, delivery, at)` (pruned with the ledger). A re-run finds the ask `answered` (or `delivered`) holding this very answer (`OwnerAskAnswer.normalized(for:)` equals the stored one). It is then `applied` again with the kept delivery, with no second write and no second line. If the hub stopped before the delivery was kept, the echo is `applied` with `result` empty. Any other ask that is not open fails `ask_not_open`.
- **Timeout.** The handler races its work against `defaultTimeout` (30 s). The work runs in its own task. If the timer wins, the echo is `outcome_unknown` and the answer keeps running to its end, never cut mid-write or mid-delivery. The handler never calls back into the relay processor.
- **Anchor fixture.** `WatchtowerKit/Tests/Fixtures/asks/anchor-fixtures.json` is shared with the phone's `CommentAnchorBuilder`. Each case has a snapshot's markdown, the text Core renders it to (headings with UTF-16 offsets), a UTF-16 selection, and the anchor `CommentAnchor` makes of it (`quote`, `prefix`, `suffix`, `heading`). The cases are a selection at the document start, one at the end, one spanning a heading (with a non-BMP emoji earlier, so UTF-16 offsets differ from characters), and one in a clipped snapshot's last shown line. The end case has a ZWJ family emoji and a combining accent inside its 64-character prefix, so a port that counts scalars or UTF-16 units instead of characters fails it. `CommentAnchorFixtures` in `Tests/Support` loads the file. `OwnerAskSliceTests` checks that the clipped case is what `OwnerAskSlice.clipSnapshot` leaves at the end of a snapshot over 256 KiB, and that the same anchor results.
- `AskAnswerHandlerTests` also sends Go's `internal/asks/testdata/answers` files (`valid_question`, `valid_review_changes`, `invalid_review_no_verdict`) through the handler, so Go, the Kit and the hub share one wire shape.
- Tests: `AskAnswerHandlerTests`, `CommentAnchorFixtureTests` (Core), `OwnerAskSliceTests.testTheAnchorFixturesClippedCaseIsTheEndOfASnapshotTheSliceCut`.
- Phone half: [Ask answers, phone half (B9)](#ask-answers-phone-half-b9).

### Board writes from the phone, hub half (B12)

- **Shared writer.** Core's `WorkbenchOwnerWrites` holds the board's owner writes: `setStatus` (claims the owner, PROJ-06, and returns the parents the PROJ-05 rollup moved), `setPriority`, `addComment` and `reply` (a reply to a resolved or outdated root reopens it). Each runs inside the caller's write transaction and returns a `WorkbenchOwnerWrite` (`target`, `rolledUp`, `touched`); the comment writes also return the new comment id. `WorkbenchBoardViewModel` calls it for the same four edits, keeping its own guards and its `write` hook.
- **Handlers.** `BoardHandlers` (`MobileHub/Handlers/`, main actor) registers `board_target_status`, `board_target_priority`, `board_comment_add`, `board_comment_reply` and `board_target_create` in `AppState.buildMobileHub`. Every write reports each touched target, the rolled-up parents included, through `WorkbenchesViewModel.onOwnerWrite` → `WorkbenchNotificationCenter.recordOwnerWrite`, so the Mac never announces the owner's own phone edit.
- **Scope (§5.2 rule 3).** A workbench that is gone (deleted on the Mac while the phone still had the action queued), a target, parent or comment that is gone → `not_found`. A row of another workbench, or a target on no board → `not_on_board`. The checks and the write share one transaction.
- **Status and priority.** The value must be one of `WorkbenchBoardCard.editableStatuses` / `editablePriorities`, else `invalid_params`. A target with children on its board (a group) refuses a status with `invalid_params`. A current value equal to the request → `applied` with no write. Otherwise a `from_*` that differs from the current value → `failed` / `conflict` with `result.current`. `result` on `applied`: `{status}` / `{priority}`.
- **Comments.** The body is trimmed, non-empty, at most 4000 characters, else `invalid_params`. A reply's entity is a thread root; a reply id there is `invalid_params`. `result`: `{comment_id}`.
- **New target.** `text` is trimmed, non-empty and at most 200 Unicode scalars (Go's `target add` counts runes); `intent` (trimmed) at most 4000 characters; `priority` one of the editable priorities; `parent_id` optional. The workbench and the parent are checked first, then the hub runs `watchtower workbench target add --workbench=N --title=… [--intent=…] --priority=… [--parent=…] --json` through `WorkbenchCLI.addTarget` (values as `--flag=value`, so one starting with "-" stays a value) and decodes `{"target_id": N}`. A CLI failure → `write_failed`; an unreadable success output → `outcome_unknown`; no CLI on the Mac → `write_failed`. Parents whose status the new child re-rolled are reported with it. `result`: `{target_id}`.
- **Exactly once and timeout.** The relay claims the comment and create kinds `begun` before they run, so a crash between `begun` and the insert is echoed `outcome_unknown` and never applied again. Status and priority are idempotent and simply re-run. Each handler races its work against 30 s (`withHandlerTimeout`, shared with `AskAnswerHandler`); a timeout is `outcome_unknown`, the work keeps running to its end.

### Session start and stop from the phone, hub half (B14, B15)

- **Handlers.** `SessionStartStopHandlers` (`MobileHub/Handlers/`, main actor) registers `session_start` and `session_stop` in `AppState.buildMobileHub`. It holds `WorkbenchesViewModel` weakly (none → `write_failed` "The workbench is reloading on the Mac") and the app's `TerminalCenter`.
- **Start.** Entity: the target id. Params `{workbench_id, mode: new|open_existing, plan_first, bring_forward, brief?}`; a missing flag or an unknown mode is `invalid_params`. Order: the device grant (`start_sessions_allowed` false → `device_not_allowed`), then the board scope (workbench or target gone → `not_found`; a target of another workbench or of no board → `not_on_board`), then `WorkbenchesViewModel.startForTarget` — Work on it's read, reuse rule (`open_existing`) and launch path. The brief is `TerminalLaunch.workOnTargetPrompt`, or the phone's `brief` only when the device has `typing_allowed` (otherwise ignored); `plan_first` appends `TerminalLaunch.planFirstSuffix`. The brief rides `$WATCHTOWER_FIRST_PROMPT` as on the Desktop, so one starting with "-" is never read as a flag. Both apply to a new session only: an `open_existing` that finds the target's session resumes it and echoes its id — the Kit has no result key saying it was reused.
- **Placement.** `bring_forward` false → `.background` (nothing on the owner's screen moves). True → `AppState.bringWorkbenchForward` first (Workbench destination, the workbench drilled into, the app activated, the main window brought front or opened), then `.keeping(.board)`, as Work on it.
- **Outcome.** `applied` with `{session_id, stage: "starting"}` once the process runs. A new launch is watched for 3 s (100 ms steps): an exit 127 (`exec claude` found no Claude Code on the login shell's PATH) → `claude_not_found` with `TerminalLaunch.exitMessage(127)`; any other early exit is the session's own end and stays `applied`. A session already live before the start is not watched. `TargetStartError.notOnBoard` → `not_on_board`; `.inProgress` (a start for the target already running, here or a Work on it) → `conflict`; `.failed` (a db error, no terminal, a refused launch) → `write_failed`, never a success.
- **Exactly once.** The relay echoes `received` on dequeue, claims the record `begun` before the handler, and expires it 24 h after `created_at`; a record found `begun` after a restart is `outcome_unknown` and never starts a second session. There is no retry of a `new` start.
- **Stop.** Entity: the session id, `{}`. Idempotent. A session that is not a `claude` session of a workbench (unknown, standalone, shell) → `not_found`. A live one gets `TerminalCenter.close(sessionID:)` (SIGHUP, SIGKILL after the grace); one that is not live is `applied` with no signal. The row and its transcript stay.
- **Device grants.** The grants come from the `deviceGrant(device_id)` seam (`SessionStartStopHandlers.DeviceGrant`); AppState answers `MobileLinkCenter.sessionGrant`, the linked phone's row in `devices`.
- Each handler races its work against 30 s (`withHandlerTimeout`); a timeout is `outcome_unknown`.
- Tests: `SessionStartHandlerTests`; `BackgroundStartTests`, `TerminalCenterTests`, `RelayProcessorTests` and the PROJ-11/12 guards stay unchanged.
- Phone half: [Session start and stop, phone half (B15)](#session-start-and-stop-phone-half-b15).

### Session input from the phone, hub half (B18, PROJ-16)

- **Contract.** PROJ-16 in `docs/inventory/workbench.md`: a phone line reaches a session only from a linked device the owner allowed to type, only while the agent is idle and the prompt holds no draft, only through `SessionLineDelivery` (PROJ-12), and never into a permission prompt.
- **Handlers.** `SessionInputHandlers` (`MobileHub/Handlers/`, main actor, a hub companion) registers `session_input` (entity: the session; `{text}`), `session_finish_request` (the session; the fixed line "Please wrap up: update the board, then call finish_session with a short summary.") and `session_input_cancel` (entity: the original action's id) in `AppState.buildMobileHub` (`makeSessionInputs`). It uses the workbench state's `SessionLineDelivery`, the app's `TerminalCenter`, `SessionAgentStateCenter.statuses` and `MobileLinkCenter.sessionGrant`.
- **Order.** Text: `SessionInputText.normalize` (Kit, the phone applies the same rule): a CRLF and every other line break or control character one space, format characters kept, trimmed; 1–4000 characters, else `invalid_params`. The paste keeps format characters (`submitPrompt(keepingFormatCharacters:)`, phone lines only). Then `typing_allowed` (else `device_not_allowed`), a board `claude` session (else `not_found`), live (else `session_not_running`), then `SessionLineDelivery.deliverLine` with a per-line gate asked right before every paste, after the delivery's fresh state read: the line's age (24 h → `expired`), the grant again, the session still listed after the read (deleted → `session_not_running`), not `needsApproval`, idle (`SessionInputHandlers.isIdle`: a hook state of this run that is Stopped, Finished, Waiting for you or Error, or the run's mark with no turn; Working, Agents working and Running are not), not just submitted into (`SessionLineDelivery.submittedAt`: busy until a hook stamp newer than the submit, 30 s at most), and `TerminalCenter.promptHoldsText` false. A held line's retry goes only while the handler runs and only after claiming its sidecar row for typing (`claimHeldLineForTyping`); a row gone or already claimed, a stopped hub or a handler gone answers `GateAnswer.drop` — nothing typed, the line leaves the queue (the next hub start queues the row again).
- **Outcomes.** `submitted` → `applied` `{delivery: submitted}`; `typed` → `applied` `{delivery: typed}`; `copied` → `cannot_type`; `noSession` → `session_not_running`. Held: `held` with `agent_busy`, `needs_approval`, `prompt_has_text` or `state_unknown` (a failed read; a line queued behind another line reads `agent_busy`).
- **Held lines.** Kept in memory by `SessionLineDelivery` and in the sidecar's `held_session_lines` (the action, session, line, run and app launch, reason, `typing`). Each try's result: a new reason is stored and echoed; a final outcome removes the row and is echoed. Later echoes go through `RelayProcessor.lateEcho` — under the relay gate, so they land after the pass that echoed `held`, one after another — and replace the ledger's stored echo, so a lost ack re-echoes the final outcome. Like every echo they are sealed `.relayToPhone` by the hub's `SecureTransport` (`storage.secure`), to the action's `device_id` — the sender the device gate admitted, since the held row keeps no sealed info; a removed phone has no relay key, so its late echo fails and is logged (D9: a removed phone is never answered), and a hub without keys writes nothing. A hub stop drops the handler's lines from memory; a hub start queues every row again: one marked `typing` is `outcome_unknown` (never typed twice), an expired one `expired`, one whose session stopped, started a new run or belongs to an earlier app launch `session_not_running`.
- **Cancel.** Only from the phone that sent the line (else `device_not_allowed`). Drops a held line not being typed and echoes the original `cancelled`; a line that already went (or is being typed) is left as it is, and the cancel is `applied` either way.
- **`SessionLineDelivery` (PROJ-12 unchanged).** `deliverLine` takes the optional gate (an ask's answer passes none). A delivery's end retries the session's held lines only when one was queued behind it; the others wait for the next state read. `follow(_:)` wires it to `SessionAgentStateCenter` (the app's wiring, moved out of `WorkbenchesViewModel`): its reads and changes call `statesRead()`, which ignores the reads a delivery makes itself — a line held by its gate used to set off its own retry with each read.

### Calendar events (C2)

- **`calendar_event`** (`CalendarEventSlice`, record `calendar_event-<calendar_events.id>`), registered in `AppState.buildMobileHub`. Rows are read by an explicit column list; `raw_json` is never selected, and attendees keep `email`, `display_name` and `response_status` only.
- **Window.** Events overlapping local today 00:00 − 1 day … now + 14 days, as `CalendarQueries.fetchEvents` overlaps (start ≤ end of window, end ≥ start of window), so an event from 23:30 the evening before runs into the first day and is published. Both bounds are calendar steps in the Mac's injected `Calendar`: the start stays on local midnight across a DST change, and the end is 14 calendar days after now (not 14 × 86 400 s, so it moves by an hour when a DST change falls inside the window). An all-day event is stored as UTC midnight; the hub places it by that calendar day read as local midnight (end exclusive, the Kit mirror's `localStart(in:)` rule), so it neither leaks into the window nor drops out of it a day early west or east of UTC. `cancelled` events are left out. At most 500, earliest first (by local start, then id; each event's local start is computed once before the sort — board #724). `windowEventIDs` (the transcript window) reads only the columns the window, the dedup and the order need.
- **Dedup.** Same non-empty `ical_uid` and same `start_time` → one record: the copy with a `conference_url`, else the lowest id. A cancelled copy never takes part. Prep and linked targets are read across every copy, so a transcript recorded against the dropped copy still links.
- **Fields.** Times travel as the stored ISO8601 strings. `title`/`location` 300, `description` plain text (Core's `CalendarEvent.stripHTML`) 2000, `attendees` ≤ 100, all with `SliceClip` (`_clipped`, `_more`). `prep_bullets`: `meeting_prep_cache.result_json` `talking_points[].text`, then `suggested_prep[]`, blanks dropped, first 8, each ≤ 300; `prep_generated_at` only when a readable prep exists (an empty `result_json` is no prep; an unreadable one is left out). A readable prep with no usable bullets still sets `prep_generated_at`, so "No prep yet" keys on empty bullets. `linked_targets` `{id, text ≤ 200, status}` ≤ 20: the `converted_target_id`s of the event's transcripts' chapter action items, oldest transcript first, joined to `targets` with `project_id IS NULL` (PROJ-01), so a board target or a deleted one drops out.
- **Unreadable sources.** Attendees `null` (Go's nil slice, an event without guests) or `""` are an empty list. A prep, attendee list or `chapters_json` that fails to decode is left out and logged once per row and content (with the decoding error), not on every tick; a changed value warns again.
- **Wire shape.** The Kit pins its `calendar_event` literal inline in `CalendarMirrorFixtureTests`; `CalendarEventSliceTests` reads that literal from the Kit test file and checks the hub payload's keys and literal types against it.
- Tests: `CalendarEventSliceTests` (window edges, Europe/Kyiv DST day, all-day west and east of UTC, over midnight, dedup, caps, prep, PROJ-01, HTML description, no `raw_json`).

### Phone recordings, hub half (C3, C4)

- **Ingest** (`RelayProcessor`, spec §5.3, §6.4). A `recording_upload` still `pending` goes through the device gate first: no `device_id`, or one `RecordingUploads.isDeviceLinked` refuses, is `failed` (`device_not_linked`) with nothing written. Until A8 reads the linked devices, `AppState` keeps the default gate (any non-empty `device_id`). Then the record is claimed `begun` (`HubSyncState.claimRelayRetryingFailure`) and its asset checked (missing or empty → `failed`, ledger `not_found`). Since E2E the gate is key-based (see End-to-end encryption), and the asset is sealed: it is decrypted with the descriptor from the record's frame into an owner-only (0600) temporary plaintext file beside the stash, which is removed whatever the outcome. A sealed asset over `RecordingUploads.maxSealedBytes` (the phone's 90 MB cap, sealed) is refused before decrypting (an unreadable size fails `not_found`): `failed` / `invalid_params` with the phone's own too-large message (target 149). An asset that does not open (tampered, truncated, or restaged under a new key while this envelope was in flight) is `not_found`: retry, not poison. `PhoneRecordingJobs.ingest` drops an `event_id` whose `calendar_events` row is gone (review focus 4: the transcript is saved ad-hoc, never failed) and calls `MeetingRecorderCenter.ingestPhoneRecording`, which writes `rec_<ts>.m4a` + `.meta` into the recordings directory and enqueues the job. `ingestPhoneRecording` first checks the file is an audio recording (an `ftyp` box at offset 4, then at least one audio track through `AVURLAsset`: `isAudioRecording`) and refuses anything else, a video or HEIF image included, (`PhoneIngestError.notAudio`) before reserving a name (target 168); a file it cannot read (an I/O error) is `PhoneIngestError.unreadable` with the error's description, not "not audio". A throw (an unwritable directory, a full disk, not audio) is `failed` / `write_failed`, and the transcriber's ingest leaves no partial `.m4a` or `.meta` (review focus 3). The ingest runs under `withHandlerTimeout` (300 s, `recordingIngestTimeout`); a timeout is `failed` / `outcome_unknown`.
- **Echo.** The hub rewrites the same record `received` (or `failed` with a phone-facing `error_message`) with **no asset**, which frees the iCloud storage; the phone deletes its file only on `received`. The wire payload has no reason field, so the reason code lives in the ledger outcome (`received`, `failed:<reason>`). After `received` the transport's copy of the asset is removed (best-effort, logged).
- **Exactly once.** A `received` record is `done` for good: a duplicate delivery ingests nothing. Its ledger row can be pruned while the phone still holds the recording, for example after a rotation's grace when the phone wiped before it read the echo. The hub also checks the upload id against `phone_recordings` (`HubSyncState.hasPhoneRecording`, kept 31 days). A hit is answered `received` with no second ingest (final review Desktop M-1). Uploads have no age window; their ledger and `relay_seen` rows are kept while the record's epoch is still accepted (E2E12). A failed attempt wrote nothing, so the phone's Retry (same record name, `pending` again) is claimed again and ingested; `claimRelayRetryingFailure` takes over only a `done` row whose outcome starts with `failed`. An upload found `begun` at the next pass (the hub stopped mid-ingest) is echoed `failed` / `outcome_unknown` and not re-ingested by the hub; the owner's Retry ingests it, and if the first copy had landed that is the accepted duplicate (spec §9).
- **`recording_job`** (`RecordingJobSlice`, record `recording_job-<upload id>`, a normal kind: it publishes on the 30 s full tick). `PhoneRecordingJobs` (main actor, a hub companion) remembers each ingested upload in the sidecar's `phone_recordings(upload_id, audio_path, transcript_id, status, percent, error, updated_at)`: `queued` once the ingest returns (in the same main-actor turn, so the job's first callback finds it), then from the transcriber's `onJobPhase`/`onJobFinished` (chained on `start()`, restored on `stop()`): `transcribing` with `percent` = done × 100 / total (0 for the first 0-of-0 report), `diarizing`, `summarizing`, `failed` with `error`, `queued` again when the owner retries a failed job on the Mac, and `done` with `transcript_id`. A phase after `done`, and the Mac's own recordings, are ignored; an unchanged state writes nothing. Both kinds are normal, so a change rides the next full tick (no nudge: the publisher's fast lane keeps only the urgent kinds, and the companions' normal-kind nudges were removed). A rebuilt hub reloads the remembered jobs, so it keeps following them.
- **Window.** A job is published until 7 days after it ended (`done`/`failed`; exactly 7 days is kept); an unfinished one stays. At most 100, latest `updated_at` first. Payload `{id, status, percent?, transcript_id?, error?, updated_at}`; `error` is cut at 300 with `…` (the Kit mirror has no `_clipped` flag). Sidecar rows are forgotten 31 days after their last update, past the `meeting_transcript` window, because they carry its `phone_recording_id`.
- **Known gaps (ledgered).** A job that was queued or running when the app quit is not re-enqueued (the transcriber offers it in its recovered list) and its record stays at its last state until the owner transcribes it on the Mac; phases that happen while the hub is off are not seen. `received` is never published by this hub (the job is `queued` once the ingest returns).
- **Wire shape.** `RecordingJobSliceTests` reads the Kit `recording_job` literals from `CalendarMirrorFixtureTests` and checks keys and literal types.

### Meeting transcripts (C5)

- **`meeting_transcript`** (`MeetingTranscriptSlice`, record `meeting_transcript-<meeting_transcripts.id>`), registered in `AppState.buildMobileHub`. An explicit column list: `audio_path`, `speakers_json` (voice embeddings) and `notes_md` are never selected, and of `chapters_json` only `overall_summary` is read (published as `overview`, 2000).
- **Window.** Created in the last 30 days, or its event is in the calendar window (`CalendarEventSlice.windowEventIDs`, every copy of a de-duplicated event); at most 200, newest first. `title` 300.
- **Recap.** `meeting_recaps` by `transcript_id` first, then by the transcript's `event_id` (the Recap tab's precedence, `MeetingRecapQueries.fetchForRecording`), so a recap whose event aged out or was deleted is still found; otherwise the transcript's own `summary_json` (ad-hoc recordings). An unreadable recap is logged and the next source is tried. `summary`, `key_decisions[]`, `action_items[]`, `open_questions[]`, each list ≤ 50 entries of ≤ 500 (`_more`). `speakers[]`: the labels of the non-deleted segments in order of first appearance, `""` (not diarized) left out, ≤ 20.
- **`phone_recording_id`** comes from the closure `MeetingTranscriptSlice(phoneRecordingID:)`, wired in `AppState.buildMobileHub` to `PhoneRecordingJobs.uploadID(forTranscript:)` (the sidecar's `phone_recordings.transcript_id`, see C4). A Mac recording has no entry (key absent); a job that finishes reaches the phone's transcript on the next 30 s full tick.
- **Segments asset.** The body rides as the record's CKAsset `segments.json`, `[{start_sec, end_sec, speaker, text}]` of the non-deleted segments (RelayCoder JSON). A legacy row (no or unreadable `segments_json`) is one segment `0 … duration_sec` holding `transcript_text`, speaker `""`; an empty text is `[]`. Over 20 MB (20 × 1024 × 1024 bytes) the array is cut after the last whole segment that fits and the payload carries `segments_clipped: true`.
- **Asset-backed records in the publisher.** A source conforming to `AssetSliceSource` returns, from inside the read, a build that yields `AssetSliceRecord`s (record + `SliceAsset`: file name, SHA-256 digest, and the data unless the staged file already holds that digest). The publisher stages each changed record's file in `SliceAssetStore` (`MobileHub/SliceAssets/<recordName>/segments.json`, `MobileHubStorage.sliceAssets`), sealed under a fresh `K_asset` with its `AssetDescriptor` in `segments.json.desc` beside it (E2E11; the descriptor rides in the record's envelope), written atomically in place, and saves the record with `assetFileURL` pointing at it — the transport's pending save reads that file until it is sent. The hash is `SliceDiff.recordHash`: SHA-256 of the payload plus the asset's plaintext SHA-256 (so an unchanged asset is not re-sealed), so a speaker rename or a deleted segment republishes even when the payload is the same. A record whose staged file is missing or holds another digest is sent again whatever its hash; the store remembers each staged file's digest (a file left by an earlier run is hashed once). The store's lock is an `NSLock` (its critical sections span disk I/O); a sweep logs a listing error other than a missing directory. Files are removed when the record leaves the zone (and swept each cycle), on an account reset (`removeStagedAssets`), and when the Settings toggle turns the hub off (`MobileHubService.disable` → `closeStagedAssets`: the store refuses new files until the publisher's next `start()`); a record that cannot be staged is skipped unhashed and retried. Without a store (stub storage) asset-backed records are skipped, never published without their asset.
- **Cost.** Two phases. Inside the publisher's read the slice reads the window, each row's candidate recaps — `id` and `updated_at` only, no `recap_json` — and its light key (board #724): the small columns, `updated_at`, the big columns' byte lengths (`octet_length` of `segments_json`, `transcript_text`, `summary_json`, `chapters_json`, read from the record header without loading the text), every candidate recap's `id`/`updated_at` (which one is readable is known only once decoded) and the phone recording id. A row whose light key matches the cached one, whose `updated_at` is at least 2 s old (`settleTime`: the column has whole seconds, so a same-second, same-length rewrite is caught by the full read while the row settles; a row read while settling caches no light key), and whose staged file still holds its asset reuses the cached payload and digest without reading its big columns. Every other row is read in full (`transcript_text` only when `segments_json` is NULL or not valid JSON) and fingerprinted: SHA-256 over the raw columns, the candidate recaps' `id`/`updated_at` and the phone recording id; a cached fingerprint still reuses the record. Only the rows left to build have their candidates' `recap_json` read, still inside the read; the build decodes them in precedence order (own recap first, then the event's) and the first readable one wins. Every writer of these columns (Go's `SetMeetingTranscript*`, the Desktop's `MeetingTranscriptQueries`) bumps `updated_at`. Changed rows are decoded, encoded and hashed in the build the publisher runs after the read ended. An unchanged tick reads no big column, no `recap_json` and builds no asset (`fullReads`, `recapReads`, `assetBuilds`, pinned by tests); a fresh launch builds each once.
- **Wire shape.** The Kit pins `meeting_transcript` and `segments.json` inline in `CalendarMirrorFixtureTests`; `MeetingTranscriptSliceTests` reads both literals (`SliceJSON.kitInlineFixture`) and checks keys and literal types.
- Tests: `MeetingTranscriptSliceTests` (fixture shapes, recap join and precedence, ad-hoc `summary_json`, legacy and unreadable segments, deleted segments, the 20 MB clip and its boundary, hidden columns, the 200 cap and the 30-day/event window, speaker rename through the publisher); `SlicePublisherTests` (staging, asset-only change, file removal, missing file, closed store, no store); `MobileHubServiceTests` (account reset and disable remove the files).

## Kit (WatchtowerSync + WatchtowerKit)

### Kit package and wire rules (A3)

- **Port.** The sync core of the old `mobile-app` branch, re-homed as `WatchtowerSync`, with the chat seam removed. `SliceKind` keeps the branch's kinds and adds `heartbeat` and `device_grant` (later tasks add the workbench, calendar and recording kinds); `RelayRecordKind` is `action`, `heartbeat` (wire compatibility only), `recording_upload` and `device`; an unknown kind is logged once and ignored.
- **Tolerant decoding.** An unknown `ActionStatus` decodes as `pending`, an unknown `ActionReason` as nil, an unknown `RecordingUploadStatus` as `pending`, and enum-like slice fields are `OpenWireValue`s, so a record from a newer Mac never fails as a whole. `RelayCoder` is the one wire coder: snake_case keys, Unix-second dates, sorted keys, a nil optional is an absent key.
- **Payloads.** `ActionRequestPayload` gains `device_id`, `reason` and `result`; `HeartbeatPayload` (DataZone, §4.1) and `DevicePayload` (RelayZone, plain name `device-<device_id>`, opaque on the wire) carry the hub's and the phone's identity; `DeviceHello` (E2E4) is the `DevicePayload` plus the phone's public keys. `CloudRecordFactory` writes the heartbeat to DataZone, so a RelayZone copy (writable by a share participant) is never trusted.
- Tests: `RelayPayloadTests`, `ActionStatusTests`, `SliceKindTests`, `CloudRecordFactoryTests`, `KitFixtureTests`.

### Transport: scopes, errors and the local buffer (A4, C8)

- **`CloudKitTransport`** is the `CKSyncEngine` adapter behind `CloudSyncTransport`, and carries already-sealed records: `SecureTransport` sits above it (see End-to-end encryption). Engine events land in `TransportStore` (GRDB: an incoming event buffer whose local seqs are the consumers' change tokens, a pending-send queue, archived system fields, the engine state). `InMemoryCloudTransport` is the test and demo fake.
- **Scopes** (`CloudDatabaseScope`, spec §2.3). `private` — the hub, and a phone on the Mac's Apple ID; `shared(ownerName:)` — a phone on another Apple ID, which reaches the Mac's zones through accepted shares; another owner's zone is never ours. Only the hub role (`TransportRole.hub`) creates and deletes zones; a phone never does, in either scope (multi-hub spec §4.1). A store records its scope, and opening it under a different scope wipes it first.
- **Errors** (spec §9). `.limitExceeded` halves the batch (once per failure, even when it arrives both thrown and per record); a record rejected alone, or whose asset file is gone, is dropped from the queue and reported to the owner. `.requestRateLimited` / `.zoneBusy` wait out `Retry-After` (the transport keeps the start of a throttling stretch, for a "slowing down" notice after 60 s). `.quotaExceeded` pauses sends until `resume()` (`TransportEvent.quotaExceeded`). An expired change token re-fetches once. In the phone role a vanished hub zone (`.zoneNotFound`, `.userDeletedZone`, a zone-deleted event), in either scope, emits `TransportEvent.unlinked`, terminal for that transport; the phone never re-creates a hub's zone. In the hub role a deleted zone is re-registered so the pending rows re-create it. An account sign-out or switch wipes the store and relaunches the engine (`setAccountResetHandler`); in the hub role a server-deleted DataZone also fires the data-zone reset handler once.
- **Pulls** are single-flight: a `pull()` arriving meanwhile joins the running one, and fetch errors delivered while it awaits the engine are parked and handled once. `stop()` cancels and drops the engine; saves made meanwhile wait in the store.
- **Zone poll.** `CKSyncEngine.fetchChanges()` fetches only the zones a delivered push put in its unfetched-changes set (`State.zoneIDsWithUnfetchedServerChanges`, read-only); an explicit `FetchChangesOptions(scope: .zoneIDs(…))` only narrows that set, so without a push a manual fetch sends no request (seen live: a hub never read a linking phone's `device` record until its engine restarted). So after the engine fetch every `pull()` also polls each zone of its scope (`private`: this account's; `shared`: the owner's) with `CKDatabase.recordZoneChanges(inZoneWith:since:)` through the driver (`SyncEngineDriving.fetchZoneChanges`), following `moreComing` pages, with its own per-zone token in `TransportStore`'s `poll_tokens` (cleared by a wipe and a zone eviction). Pages take the engine's buffering path minus what the store already holds: a record whose change tag matches its archived system fields, and a deletion of a record without system fields. Errors: an expired poll token restarts that zone from the beginning on the next pull; a missing zone is quiet in the hub role (not created yet) and unlinks in the phone role, in either scope; throttling waits like any throttle; anything else is thrown. No poll after a failed engine fetch, while stopped, unlinked or throttled, or once the engine was replaced (an account reset). A record that fails to download inside a page is logged and skipped. The phone's push fetch (`fetchForPush`, see Fetching below) skips the poll; the phone's 60 s safety poll, pull to refresh and return to the foreground keep it.
- **Record types (E2E7, E2E15).** A record is saved as `WatchtowerEnvelope` (`CloudKitTransport.recordType`): `kind` and `modifiedAt` plain, the envelope in `encryptedValues["payload"]`, the sealed asset as `asset`. A payload-free signal is `WatchtowerSignal` (`signalRecordType`). The pre-E2E `WatchtowerRecord` type is ignored on fetch, logged once per type (`cloudRecord(from:zone:)`). A delete of a record the server never saw (`unknownItem`, e.g. a `sig-` signal) counts as success.
- **Creator.** `CloudRecord.creatorUserRecordName` is a fetched record's `creatorUserRecordID.recordName` (the owner's own records read `CKCurrentUserDefaultName`), kept in the buffer's `events.creator` column. It is never sent on a save; nil for a record this device built, in `InMemoryCloudTransport` unless a test sets it, and for an event buffered before the column existed. The hub's device gate reads it.
- **Assets.** `CloudRecord.assetFileURL` rides as a `CKAsset`, always a sealed file (`ChunkedAssetCipher`; its key is inside the record's envelope): a phone `recording_upload` in RelayZone, or an asset-backed slice record (a meeting transcript's `segments.json`) in DataZone. A rewrite with nil removes the asset. Fetched asset files are copied into the store's stash, because CloudKit's own files are temporary. The stash file is named by the hex SHA-256 of zone and record name (`TransportStore.stashFileName`), so a re-fetch overwrites and a server-supplied name such as `..` never becomes a path (target 149); an asset over `TransportStore.maximumStashBytes` (the phone's upload cap, sealed: `RecordingUploader.maximumSealedAssetBytes`), or whose size cannot be read, is not copied. Its event keeps CloudKit's temporary URL, so the hub answers an oversized upload with the too-large message only while that file still exists; once CloudKit purged it the hub reports the audio as missing ("did not reach your Mac") — a known v1 limit, since only a phone that bypasses its own uploader cap can send one.
- **Buffer retention.** `CompactingTransport.compactEvents(in:keepSince:)` drops events at or below a consumer's token: the phone's `ReplicaHydrator` compacts `.data` and its `RelayFeed` compacts `.relay` after each persisted token (best-effort, logged). Data-zone compaction also deletes the stash files of the compacted events, except a path a newer event still references, so a consumer copies an asset before it compacts past it. The hub never compacts `.relay`: its daily hygiene needs the full-zone scan, so it sweeps by age (`SweepingTransport.sweepEvents`, older than the cutoff **and** at or below the consumer token). Stash files no buffered event and no pending row references are swept at open, after a zone eviction and after a wipe.
- Tests: `CloudKitTransportErrorTests`, `CloudKitTransportScopeTests`, `CloudKitTransportPollTests`, `CloudKitTransportPushFetchTests`, `CloudKitTransportResetTests`, `CloudKitTransportStopTests`, `CloudKitTransportMappingTests`, `TransportStoreTests`, `TransportAssetTests`, `InMemoryCloudTransportTests`.

### Replica, outbox and relay feed (A3, A11)

- **`ReplicaStore`** is the phone's mirror of DataZone and holds **ciphertext** (replica format 2; a format-1 plaintext store is dropped and re-hydrated): `slice_records` (each row the hub's envelope with its wire name, `(epoch, seq)`, `needs_index`, and after indexing its plain `kind` and `plain_name`; opened and decoded on read, so a corrupt one is skipped and counted, never fails a batch), `data_seen` (the DataZone rollback guard), `relay_seen` (the relay rollback guard) and the phone's seq ceiling, `replica_meta` (the data and relay tokens, the alert watermark), the `pending_actions` overlay, the `phone_recordings` ledger with `phone_recording_marks`, and `slice_assets` (a record's sealed asset bytes, decrypted on read, or the reason it was unreadable; files over the sealed length of 20 MiB are refused unread). `apply` takes one data-zone batch in one transaction and refuses a token older than the stored one; it verifies every envelope's hub signature with the public key (so it runs while the phone is locked), refuses another hub's records and older versions, and keeps the rest unindexed until `indexPending()` opens them with the epoch keys (see The locked phone). Asset files are read before the write transaction. `resetSyncTokens()` is SPI (`@_spi(DemoTransport)`) for the demo transport only. `slice_kind_versions` (board #724) holds one change stamp per slice kind. Under E2E a stored version is readable only once indexed, so `indexPending` bumps the kind of every row it opens (once per kind per pass, in its write transaction; the hydrator indexes right after each apply, a locked phone at the unlock), and `apply` bumps only for a deletion (by the stored row's kind; a row still unindexed has none) and for a newer-format version of a row whose kind is known; a dropped stale batch bumps nothing. `ReplicaStore.keyRefusalCount` counts rows a list read skipped because their keys could not be read (locked, or an epoch's keys not here yet); `kindVersions(_:from:)` reads them (0 for a kind never written) and `kindVersionsRegion` / `pendingActionsRegion` / `phoneRecordingsRegions` are what an observation tracks. Like every replica table it is created with `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` (the replica is not the Go goose database): an older replica starts with no stamps, so each kind reads 0 until its next write. The UI reads it through GRDB `ValueObservation` (`*(from: db)` variants for the fetch closures).
- **`ReplicaHydrator`** pulls DataZone into the replica: one cycle at a time (a caller arriving mid-cycle awaits it), `onRecordsApplied` for the records of known kinds a batch persisted.
- **`ActionOutbox`** is the phone's action producer: the action is sealed once at enqueue (`SecureTransport.seal`, p2h; a locked keychain throws before anything is sent), the transport saves that envelope (`saveSealed`), then a `pending` overlay row stores the same envelope, opened on read with the phone's own key (no plaintext action at rest; no phantom chip on a failed save). `sweepSilentPending` also prunes the relay rollback guard past 8 days. It refuses while no device id is set (`ActionOutboxError.notLinked`; a blank id counts as none). `applyEcho`: `received`/`held` mark the row (`echo_status`), `failed`/`expired`/`cancelled` flip it to failed with the echo's `reason` and `result`, `applied` removes it and tells the applied observer (once per removed row). `sweepSilentPending` fails rows the Mac never echoed within 24 h.
- **`RelayFeed`** is the phone's single relay consumer: it owns the relay token and routes `action` echoes to the outbox and `recording_upload` echoes to the uploader; a RelayZone `heartbeat` and `device` record are ignored. It reads through `SecureTransport`, which opens the hub's echoes under this phone's h2p key; a locked keychain throws `keysUnavailable`, so the token stays and the echoes replay after unlock. The token is persisted at batch end, so a throw replays the batch (routing is idempotent), and an undecodable payload is logged and left behind. The Mac reads as online while the DataZone heartbeat is under 35 min (2100 s) old (`heartbeatStaleAfter`; the Mac writes it every 15 min, board #724 owner decision Q2).
- Tests: `ReplicaTests`, `ReplicaResetTests`, `ReplicaSliceAssetTests`, `ReplicaHydratorTests`, `ActionOutboxTests`, `RelayFeedTests`, `RelayFeedRecordingUploadTests`.

### Link code, device grant and keybox (A5, E2E4)

- **`LinkPayload`** is the QR code (spec §2.3): `watchtower://link?d=<base64url(JSON)>`, unpadded canonical base64url, sorted keys; fields `v`, `container`, `hub_id`, `mac_name` (≤ 60), `owner_user`, a single-use 32-byte `nonce`, `iat`, `exp = iat + 600`, the two share URLs (absent when none), and the hub's public keys `hub_sig` and `hub_kx` (base64url of 32 bytes; a key of another length or all zero, a small-order point, is refused). `v` is 3 since the E2E build (2 was the multi-hub build, multi-hub spec §3.4) and is checked first, so a newer code reads as "Update Watchtower on this iPhone" (`.newerVersion`) and an older one (v2 without keys, or v1) as "Update Watchtower on this Mac" (`.olderVersion`, §6.7); a code for another container is refused. The codec only encodes and decodes: the expiry and nonce checks belong to the hub (A8, see Linking, hub half) and the phone's link flow (A12, [phone half](#onboarding-and-linking-phone-half-a12)).
- **`DeviceGrant`** is the hub's `device_grant-<device_id>` slice: the phone's name, `link_refused` (`used_code`, `expired_code`, `unknown_code`, `not_confirmed`, or an unknown value, still a refusal), `typing_allowed` (default false), `start_sessions_allowed` (default true), `awaiting_confirmation` (the hub read the hello and waits for the owner) and `revoked` (removed on the Mac, D9); the last two are absent otherwise.
- **`Keybox`** (E2E4) is what travels: `{grant, keys?}`, sealed to the phone (`keybox-<device_id>`). `KeyboxKeys`: `epoch`, `data_key`, `alert_key`, `meta_key` and, after a rotation, `previous_epoch`/`previous_data_key`/`previous_alert_key` (base64url of 32 bytes). The replica's keybox reader (`ReplicaStore.keybox(deviceID:from:)`) opens the whole keybox for the link flow; `device_grant` readers get the `grant` JSON as the payload, unchanged.
- **`DeviceHello`** (E2E4): `{device, signing_key, agreement_key}`, record `hello-<device_id>`, sealed to `hub_kx`.
- Tests: `LinkPayloadTests` (v2 fixtures rewritten to v3).

### Workbench slice mirrors and action params (B1)

- **Kinds.** `SliceKind` gains `workbench`, `workbench_target`, `workbench_comment`, `terminal_session`, `owner_ask`, `session_report` and `session_timeline` (design §4.2–§4.9), and `ask_alert` (§4.7; the hub's B4 publishes it, its Kit mirror comes with A13). The phone decodes each record's payload with its `SliceMirror` in `WatchtowerKit/Models` (`Workbench`, `WorkbenchTarget`, `WorkbenchComment`, `TerminalSessionState`, `OwnerAsk`, `SessionReport`, `SessionTimeline`) through `RelayCoder` — snake_case keys, Unix-second dates, a nil optional is an absent key, unknown keys are ignored.
- **Enum-like fields** (state kind and tone, target status and priority, ask kind/status/withdrawn reason, verdict, check state, milestone kind, start mode) are `OpenWireValue`s: a value from a newer Mac decodes, keeps its raw string and reads `isKnown == false`.
- **Clipping on the wire.** `<field>_clipped` and `<list>_more` are absent unless the hub cut something, so the mirrors keep them optional (`Bool?`, `Int?`).
- **`session_report`** is the CLI's report as Go writes it (`internal/sessionreport.Report`): datetimes stay Go's UTC strings and every key may be absent, as in Core's `SessionReport`; the hub adds only `<list>_more`, `items_more` and `phases_clipped`. The other kinds carry dates as `Date`.
- **`session_timeline`** payload is `{session_id?, milestones, milestones_more?}`: `session_id` repeats the record id and may be absent. An ask's `withdrawn_reason` is absent (or `""`, read as nil) unless the ask is withdrawn.
- **`OwnerAskAnswer`** (Kit) encodes byte for byte like Core's and Go's (`internal/asks/testdata/answers`); an ask's `payload` resolves missing question and check item ids to their 1-based position, as Core and Go do.
- **Action params.** `ActionKind` gains the twelve §5.2 kinds. Each has a typed `ActionParams` struct whose `wireParams()` is the request's `params` object. Because `ask_answer` carries an object and `session_start` carries bools, `JSONValue` (WatchtowerSync) gains `.bool`, `.array` and `.object`; a row never produces them, and `databaseValue` stores them as 0/1 and JSON text.
- **Fixtures.** `WatchtowerKit/Tests/Fixtures/workbench/*.json` are the frozen payloads (`WorkbenchMirrorFixtureTests`); the hub's projection tests pin their encoder against the same files.

### Calendar and recording mirrors (C1)

- **`calendar_event`**, **`meeting_transcript`** and **`recording_job`** mirrors (`WatchtowerKit/Models`), RelayCoder JSON. Times stay the stored ISO8601 strings for the calendar and transcript kinds; `CalendarEvent.localStart(in:)` places an all-day event (stored as UTC midnight) on its local day. The transcript body is not in the payload: it is the record's `segments.json` asset (`TranscriptSegment`). An unknown `RecordingJob` status decodes as `queued`.

### App shell (A11)

- **Boot.** `WatchtowerMobileApp` builds one `AppRoot` for the app's lifetime, which owns the current `AppEnvironment` and the link flow (A12) (or shows `BootFailureView` when the replica cannot open; a signed build without its app group container refuses to boot rather than keep the replica elsewhere). The replica lives in the app group `group.com.aiwatchtowers.watchtower.mobile`, so the notification extensions can read it.
- **Transport switch.** A build that carries the iCloud entitlement (signed device or TestFlight) runs `CloudKitTransport` and starts empty; anything else (unsigned simulator and CI builds) runs `InMemoryCloudTransport` with `DemoSeed`, DEBUG only. The demo pushes records through the transport as the hub would publish them, so hydration and every decode path run end to end; record names are fixed, so a re-seed upserts.
- **Fetching** (board #724, owner decision Q1: the phone fetches when something changed). Every fetch reaches CloudKit once (`RemoteFetchDepth`), then hydrates DataZone and reads RelayZone from what that buffered (the hydrator and the feed never pull on their own; an `applied` echo's re-hydrate is local). Two depths: **full** = `CloudKitTransport.pull()` (engine fetch plus the forced zone poll) for the safety poll, pull to refresh, the return to the foreground, the launch and the link flow; **push** = `CloudKitTransport.fetchForPush(since:grace:pollOnFallback:)`. CKSyncEngine keeps `automaticallySync` on (both roles: it also sends, and the Mac is unchanged) and fetches on a push by itself, so a push waits for that own run (tracked from `willFetchChanges`/`didFetchChanges`; a manual fetch's events are not a run) — a run begun at or after the push covers it, a running one is awaited for at most `max(grace, 30 s)` (`engineRunEndWait`; a run that never reports its end stalls nothing) — and fetches through the engine itself only when no run begins within 2 s (`pushEngineGrace`) of the push or the end wait runs out. That fallback adds the forced zone poll for a foreground push (the engine fetches only the zones a delivered push named, so without the poll it may send no request) and stays light (no poll) in the background. A `pull()` never waits on the engine: one arriving while a push fetch still waits takes the push over (the push's outcome is the pull's); it waits only for a push's own running fallback fetch (one manual fetch at a time). A push waiting on the engine's run returns at once, without a request, when the link ends, the account resets or the transport stops; a push under a server-requested wait or after the unlink sends nothing and throws nothing. Each own run's end is reported (`setEngineFetchedHandler`) and, while the app is active, what it buffered is applied at once (local reads only; skipped while a fetch still waits on CloudKit, since that fetch hydrates right after — a skipped apply is owed, and if that fetch fails before hydrating, the last fetch off CloudKit runs it). **Safety poll** (per `HubSession`, intervals in `SessionCadence`; roles in "Several Macs (multi-hub F2)"): one `.loop` fetch every 60 s for the session on screen (`safetyPollInterval`) on every tab; it pauses whenever the scene is not active (inactive — Control Center, the app switcher — as well as the background; the recorder's audio background mode does not keep it running), restarts from each foreground push fetch, and the launch fetch is not followed by an immediate cycle. **Return to the foreground:** an immediate full fetch unless the last successful fetch is under 5 s old (`foregroundRefetchAfter`); then only a local apply of what the engine buffered meanwhile (no network). **Active state at launch:** `AppEnvironment.isActive` is seeded from `applicationState == .active` when the environment is built (`startsActive`; tests default to active), so a launch into the background (a silent push, no scene) never starts the safety poll, applies no engine run and restarts no poll after a push; the scene's first `.active` phase sets it. **Silent push** (`content-available`, `AppDelegate` → `PushCompletion.serve` → `AppEnvironment.handlePush(at:inForeground:)`, foreground = `applicationState == .active`; the arrival is stamped in the delegate before any hop and is the light fetch's `since`): a fetch begun after the push serves it. A push that arrives before the boot has started the transport waits for the launch fetch, which serves it; the launch fetch then takes the push's light depth (no forced zone poll) when the app is not active, and the first activation afterwards fetches in full whatever the last fetch's age. Foreground and background alike it is fetched at once (leading edge); a push arriving while a fetch runs gets one follow-up fetch after it, shared by every push meanwhile (so a burst costs at most two fetches). Only a foreground push restarts the safety poll; a background one starts no poll. Every push's completion handler gets the outcome of the fetch that served it: `.newData` when the replica or the feed took something in, `.noData`, `.failed` when the data fetch failed — or, if that fetch has not ended 25 s after the push (`PushCompletion.deadline`, inside iOS's ~30 s), the deadline's `.noData` (the push's own fetch has not ended, so nothing says it failed; an unrelated earlier failure is not reported); exactly one of the two completes it, and a fetch past the deadline keeps going. At boot, after the first fetch: the 24 h silent-pending sweep, launch recovery of cut-short captures, then — only when the app is active — the upload pass with its relaunch retry; a launch into the background uploads nothing, and the first activation runs the pass (see Phone recorder and uploader). A cold launch runs one pass: the retry gate's opening runs one only after the boot, whose own pass covers a gate open when it reaches it. CloudKit fetch failures and replica hydrate failures log apart, with the trigger and depth. Tests: `FetchCadenceWiringTests`, `CloudKitTransportPushFetchTests` (Kit).
- **Tabs.** Now, Workbench and Calendar (design pass §5: More is gone). Settings is pushed from the gear in Now's toolbar (`NowRoute.settings`); a free voice note is the Calendar's mic button. The Workbench badge is the open-ask count (orange, the only tab badge). `RootTabView.configureTabBarAppearance()` sets the badge colour on every `UITabBarAppearance` item layout as well as `UITabBarItem.appearance()`, because the latter alone drew red on the iOS 26 tab bar in the captures. A minimized capture shows a red bar above the tabs.
- **Settings.** Pushed from Now's gear; all of it is about the Mac on screen (the active session). **This Mac** section: one status row (the Mac's name, then Online, Offline · seen … ago, or Not connected, with a dot; a name too long for the row puts the status on a line under it; online while the heartbeat is under 2100 s old, one stamped ahead of the phone's clock reads online), under it any failed replica observation since launch (`<name> updates: failed N×, last <time>: <message>`, "(recovered)" once a value arrived; no row while healthy: `ObservationHealth`, `SettingsViewModel.diagnostics`), then **Last sync** ("3 min. ago", the session's `lastSyncAt`), **Queued** (the outbox count), "Sync: Demo" on the demo transport, and the **Macs** row (the number of linked Macs, a link to Settings → Macs; shown on a live build or when any Mac is linked). **Accounts** (read-only, only when the active Mac's heartbeat lists any: a green dot and OK, a red mark and Error or Revoked, or the Mac's own word; footer "Accounts are managed on your Mac."). **Workbench:** "Type into sessions from this phone" (`typing_requested`, off) and "Start sessions from this phone" (`start_sessions`, on) are requests to the active Mac, kept per hub in `hub.<hub_id>.device.*` (a choice made while unlinked is adopted by the hub whose link completes) — while linked each change rewrites that session's `device` record (sealed through that session's `SecureTransport`, E2E17), writes run one at a time and the newest wins, a failed write reverts only its own change and shows the error; the grant shows "Waiting for your Mac to confirm" or "Allowed". **Notifications:** "New asks" (on). Battery diagnostics follow (below), then the parked "Chat without the Mac — later", then **Unlink <Mac>** for the active Mac (live builds only, with a confirmation; the same `LinkingViewModel.unlink(hubID)` as Settings → Macs). The other Macs, with reorder, Unlink and Forget, are in Settings → Macs.
- **Battery diagnostics** (board #724, measuring only). Settings shows a **Battery** section after Notifications: one row per counter valued "today · yesterday", then the newest MetricKit daily report (CPU, foreground and background time, background audio, Wi-Fi and cellular ↓/↑ MB, launches, background exits, peak memory), or "No MetricKit report yet". The counters are the Kit's `EnergyCounter` (one `UserDefaults` integer per local day and `EnergyEvent`, the app group's suite on signed builds, the last 14 days kept): silent pushes and cold launches in background/foreground (`AppDelegate` through `ColdLaunchClassifier`, once per process: background when the launch options carry a remote notification or a push arrives before any activation, foreground at the scene's first `.active`; `applicationState` at launch is not used — a scene-based app reads `.background` there even on a tap — and a prewarmed process (`ActivePrewarm=1`) counts only the owner's activation), fetches by `FetchTrigger` (`AppEnvironment.refresh(_:)` — timed loop, push, pull to refresh, foreground, launch, linking), CloudKit requests counted inside `CloudKitTransport` (manual engine fetches, the engine's own fetch runs from its `willFetchChanges` event — "iCloud's own fetches"; a manual fetch's own event, an expired token's re-fetch included, is excluded and fires no `engineFetchedHandler` —, zone checks, DataZone and RelayZone poll pages, send batches) and recording upload attempts (`RecordingUploader`). MetricKit reports go through `MetricSubscriber` into `MetricHistory` (`battery-metrics.json` beside the replica, last 14 daily and diagnostic reports as the `MetricDaySummary`/`MetricDiagnosticSummary` value types). MetricKit gives CPU time per day without a foreground/background split, so the split shown is run time. `BatteryDiagnostics.shared` holds both for the process (the link flow replaces environments, the counts span them); Settings re-reads them every 30 s while on screen. Tests: `EnergyCounterTests` (Kit), `BatteryDiagnosticsTests`.

The NotifyService extension already opens an ask alert (E2E18): on a private-scope `CKQueryNotification` it reads the alert material from the alerts keychain group, fetches the alert the signal names (20 s timeouts), opens it with `AlertOpener` (title = workbench name, body = ask title), and on any failure delivers the generic "A session is waiting". The subscription is not registered yet.
- Tests: `ReplicaWiringTests`, `RootTabTests`, `SettingsWiringTests`.

### Onboarding and linking, phone half (A12, E2E16)

- **Root.** `AppRoot` (main actor, the app's lifetime) owns the `AppEnvironment` (one `HubSession` per linked Mac, kept for good), the `HubRegistry` and `LinkingViewModel`, and is the flow's `LinkHost`. There is no environment restart any more (F2 removed `Restarter` and `retireEnvironment`): a scan builds the scanned hub's session in its own directories, a link adds its row and puts it on screen, an unlink or a removal takes one hub away, a Mac's removal freezes one, an account change removes all ("Several Macs (multi-hub F2)" below). A wipe never takes the owner's audio silently (board #63): before a session goes, a stop already saving is waited for and a running capture is stopped and saved (`PhoneRecorderController.stopForWipe`); after its shutdown (so its own launch recovery has ended) captures still `recording` are finalized from their files, and every recording whose audio is on the phone and that the Mac has not acknowledged (`waiting`, `uploading`, or a failure that offers Retry) is **held for the hub it was recorded for** (`HubSession.handOverRecordings`, `RecordingHold`): its audio moves to the recordings folder's hidden `.held` folder with a JSON manifest (hub id, the Mac's name from its last heartbeat, the row's fields and marks), out of the replica and out of the launch sweep's reach (it skips hidden files), so it survives a failed removal and a kill. A session adopts, at boot and before its sweep, only the held recordings of its own hub (`adoptHeld`: `waiting` rows again, sent once a device id is set), so **a recording never reaches another owner's Mac**; the rest are listed in Recordings as "Waiting for <Mac> — link it again to send" (`heldRecordings`, `RecordingEntryState.heldForMac`). **On an iCloud account change nothing is carried** (`keepRecordings: false`): the recordings, held ones included, were made for the old account's Mac and are reported "Not sent". A row over the size cap, or one whose audio is gone, is reported "Not sent". A ledger or manifest that cannot be read moves the remaining audio to `.held/unreadable` (kept, never sent) and the removal says so (`LocalWipe.problem`, `LinkNotice.recordingsProblem`); a held recording that cannot be adopted stays held for the next boot and is reported the same way. A removal that stopped a capture shows "The link to <Mac> ended during a recording. It's saved on this iPhone and goes to <Mac> after you link it again" (`LinkNotice.recordingKept`, naming the Mac when one hub's link ended — an account change, which ends several, keeps "your Mac" — with any "Not sent" count), in place of the removal or "Not sent" notice. Each session seals every write and opens its replica with this phone's keys for its own hub ("E2E on several Macs" below). `AppDelegate.root` (weak) reaches the environment for silent pushes. The demo root keeps its environment, is linked as `DemoSeed.device`, never onboards and ignores link URLs. `AppRootView` shows `OnboardingView` while there is no hub on screen (a live build with no row, or the last one just removed), else the tabs with the flow in a full-screen cover while a phase runs and the link notice in a bar above them.
- **`LinkStore`** (`UserDefaults`, plain JSON): the link (`LinkRecord`: `hub_id`, Mac name, `DeviceScope`, owner name, this phone's `user_record_name`, the nonce, the two share URLs in `shared` scope) and a `PendingLink` (the link, the wait's deadline, the grant the replica held before the write). Since F2 the saved links live in the registry (one row per Mac); `LinkStore` keeps the pending link, and its legacy `link.record` is only what `HubBoot.migrateF1` reads. The link's hub id is validated before any path is made from it (`HubPaths.isValidHubID`). `PendingLink` also keeps the scanned code's `iat` (the Mac's clock), which binds the answer to this scan. `DeviceIdentity`: the device id is the key-derived id (D7) read from `PhoneKeyStore.deviceID()` (the non-secret `deviceid` Keychain item, readable while locked; nil until the link flow first creates the device keys); name ≤ 60 from `UIDevice`, model, marketing version. A launch outside the link flow never creates an identity (F14). Before the first unlock since a restart (a push launch) the read throws `KeyStoreError.locked` and the boot becomes `Boot.waitingForUnlock`: "Unlock your iPhone to continue", booting again on `protectedDataDidBecomeAvailable` and every 2 s while protected data is available, until a boot gets past the lock. Main's #62 random device id (Keychain item plus `device.id` default) is deleted at every launch (`LegacyDeviceID.remove`). At launch, before anything else, every saved link (registry row, an F1 link moved in by `HubBoot.migrateF1` included) or pending link without a hub signing key (made before E2E) is dropped — the row's hub removed with its recordings held, nothing sealed for it — with "Watchtower now encrypts your data. Scan the code on your Mac again." (`dropUnencryptedLinks`, `LinkNotice.encryptionReScan`); other hubs are untouched.
- **The scan** (`LinkingViewModel.open`, spec §2.3; `ScanView`'s AVFoundation QR reader and the `watchtower://link` URL from the Camera app run the same call). Step 1: `LinkPayload.parse` (`v` above 3 → "Update Watchtower on this iPhone"; `v` below 3 → "Update Watchtower on this Mac"; any other parse failure, and a hub id that is not a UUID or keys or a nonce that do not decode (`ScannedHubKeys`) → "This code can't be used…"); expired only when `exp < now − 120 s` (`expiryGrace`, so a phone clock ahead of the Mac's does not refuse — the Mac's own check decides). Already linked to this hub with a linked grant in the replica → "Linked to <Mac>" with nothing written; a re-scan of a linked hub without one (or of a hub the Mac removed) is a re-pair: that hub's link ends first (`endLink`: its row, session, files and keys; its "Not sent" count shown), other hubs untouched, so a refused or unanswered re-pair leaves no row whose keys open nothing (E2E16 I-1; this replaces F2's "a failed rescan keeps the linked Mac", which the scan's key replacement cannot honour). Step 2: `accountStatus` not `.available` → "Sign in to iCloud on this iPhone to use Watchtower". Step 3 (R2): `owner_user` equal to the phone's `userRecordID().recordName` → `private`, share URLs ignored and never accepted; different → both URLs fetched and accepted (`CloudKitLinkContainer`: `CKFetchShareMetadataOperation`, then one `CKAcceptSharesOperation`; before the accept, `CloudKitLinkContainer.checkShares` requires exactly the zone-wide shares of the code's `hub_id` zones, each owned by `owner_user` — target 168), a missing URL, other shares or any failure → "This Mac can't share with another Apple ID right now…", then `shared(ownerName: owner_user)`. A code of another hub links beside the existing ones (no prompt: F2 dropped the switch prompt); with `HubRegistry.limit` (4) Macs linked a new hub is refused before anything is accepted or written (`LinkFailure.tooManyMacs`), and so is a `hub_id` that is not a plain name (`HubPaths.isValidHubID`). Step 4: the device keys are created if absent and the scan's `HubLinkKeys` stored; `prepare(scope:hubID:fresh: true)` with the code's `hub_id` (builds the hub's session on its own empty files; a session of that hub still running is removed first with its recordings held, so an old link's rollback marks, outbox and sealed recordings go, and the new session's seqs start above the ceiling such a removal left in this process), the baseline keybox fetched, the `PendingLink` saved, then the sealed `hello-<device_id>` (`DeviceHello`: the `device` payload with `link_nonce`, `scope`, `user_record_name` and the Settings choices, plus the public keys) saved in that database. Step 5: the four-digit code (`linkCode`, `KeyDerivation.sas`) is shown: "Check that your Mac shows **7342**, then click Link on the Mac." Every 2 s (`grantPollInterval`) until the code's `exp` + 120 s (`expiryGrace`; at most a full code lifetime plus the grace from now), one fetch and a read of this phone's keybox (`LinkHost.fetchKeybox`); a keybox of the code's hub whose grant differs from the baseline answers — `awaiting_confirmation` → step 2 reads "Your Mac sees this iPhone"; any `link_refused` (`not_confirmed` included) → "This code can't be used — Show a new code on the Mac"; `linked` with keys and `linked_at` ≥ the code's `iat` → the keys join the stored ones, the link is saved in the registry (`LinkHost.linked`: the hub's row, the unlinked choices adopted, the held recordings adopted, `setLinkedDevice`, the hub put on screen; a fifth hub that raced past the scan's check is refused and its keys go), "Linked to <Mac>" (`linked` without keys keeps waiting); none → "Your Mac didn't answer — Keep Settings → Mobile open on the Mac and scan again". A scan that ends without a link removes the keys it stored and the candidate session and files (a hub with no row). A hello whose code the Mac does not know gets no answer at all (F4), so that scan ends on the wait timeout too. Step 6: **Continue** asks for notification permission and opens Now. A kill during the wait leaves the `PendingLink`: the relaunch (`resumePendingLink`) recomputes the code from the stored keys and waits for what is left of the wait in the pending scope, and never shows the phone as linked before a keybox; with the keys locked the pending link is kept for the next launch. A `PendingLink` saved before its baseline was read (`baselineCaptured` false, also for one an older build saved) wrote nothing to the Mac: the relaunch offers to scan again at once instead of taking an earlier answer in the replica (a refusal of another code) as this scan's. A session that cannot be built, or a hello the transport's store cannot queue, is "Couldn't set up sync on this iPhone" (`LinkFailure.local`), not "Couldn't reach iCloud". A code arriving while a link runs is ignored. The failure screen shows each failure's title with its hint under it (design pass O3); the waiting screen shows the three link steps, the code to compare and, after 20 s, "Keep Settings → Mobile open on the Mac." (O2). A failure to read the device keys is "This iPhone's Watchtower keys can't be read. Unlock it and scan again."
- **Several Macs.** The single-link bridge (F1: one transport on the linked hub's zones, restarted on a scope or hub change) is replaced by the registry and one `HubSession` per Mac; see "Several Macs (multi-hub F2)" below.
- **Unlink** (Settings → Macs, live builds only; per Mac, see "Several Macs (multi-hub F2)"): `unlinkHub` takes the hub's registry row before its first await (a removal or account signal arriving meanwhile — leaving the shares is what makes the Mac's zones vanish — finds no row and is ignored), then the `device` record with `unlinked: true` goes out sealed through the hub's own session transport (the one holding the record's server state) and is waited for until it has left the send queue (`FarewellDelivery`, `TransportStore.isPending`, `sendNow()` again while it waits) with a hard 10 s cap that a hung send cannot exceed; best effort, delivery guarantee #143. The session's outbox items still pending, plus recordings whose audio is gone, are counted ("Not sent: N items were still waiting for your Mac"; recordings with their audio are held for their hub, above); then the session is shut down and its files deleted. In `shared` scope the zone-wide share records (`CKRecordNameZoneWideShare`) of the hub's two zones (`DataZone-<hub_id>`, `RelayZone-<hub_id>` owned by the link's owner, `CloudKitLinkContainer.leaveShares(ownerName:hubID:)` through `HubZones`) are deleted from the shared database after the removal. Removing the hub on screen puts the next Mac on screen; the last one leaves Welcome. The hub's keys are deleted from the Keychain after the removal (`removeHubLink`, after the `unlinked` record was sealed with them); Forget and an account change delete a hub's keys too, while the Mac's removal (a freeze) keeps them so the frozen replica stays readable.
- **The link's events** (spec §9). `TransportEvent.unlinked` (`HubSession.onLinkSignal`, wired per session by `AppRoot` before the engine starts) → `removedByMac(hubID)`: the hub is frozen (below), "<Mac> removed this phone". The Mac's Remove also deletes the phone's `device_grant` (`DeviceGrantSlice` never caps a linked phone's grant: only refusals fill the room left under 20), and that is the only removal signal a same-Apple-ID phone gets (its zones are the Mac's own and never vanish, #64). A wrongful wipe loses data, so after each successful fetch (`onFetched`) `AppRoot` reads the heartbeat and the keybox of the device id the link was made with (`LinkRecord.deviceID`, never the Keychain's current id; `ReplicaStore.keybox(deviceID:from:)`, opened with this phone's key) in one snapshot, has the keybox's epoch keys the Keychain lacks merged (`reconcileKeyboxKeys(hubID:)`, final review Phone I-1), and `LinkingViewModel.evaluate(grant:heartbeat:)` reads a removal only when all hold: the link kept its device id and its grant's `linked_at` (`LinkRecord.linkedAt`; an older link never reads one); no scan, unlink or pending link runs; the linked hub's heartbeat is there and newer than `linked_at`; and there is no linked grant for that id (absent, `linked: false` or `revoked`) on two looks at least 30 s apart (`removalConfirmAfter`) with none in between — the first look is kept on the hub's registry row (`HubRow.grantMissingSince`) across a relaunch, and a linked grant of the linked device and hub clears it in any fetch, before and whatever the heartbeat, busy and pending conditions (those gate only the missing-grant path), so a stale first look never counts as the second. A keybox stored but unreadable (a newer Mac's format, an envelope that does not open, keys locked) is never judged — unreadable is not absent — and its warning is logged once per hub until a readable one comes. A keybox indexed after the link (`keyboxChanged(hubID:)`, also from the unlock indexing) adds newer epoch keys; one that reads `revoked` is that Mac removing this phone (D9): the hub freezes, in either scope; a phone that missed it is told by the keybox's absence (#64). Then it is `removedByMac(hubID)`, whose notice carries what was left unsent. A shared phone whose participant removal failed is told the same way. The transport's account reset (any hub's) → the phone's `userRecordName` compared with the links': another account removes every hub and shows Welcome; a sign-out (or a failed check) keeps the replicas; the same check runs at launch. After each successful fetch (`onFetched`, wired per session) the Mac's heartbeat is noted on its registry row (flavor, time), and for the hub on screen no heartbeat for 24 h reads "<Mac> hasn't synced for a day — if it changed iCloud account, link again" (`LinkNotice.stale`, naming that Mac). A switch re-evaluates it for the hub it puts on screen (`AppEnvironment.onActivate` → `LinkingViewModel.activated`, from that session's last heartbeat; none read yet clears it), so one Mac's stale notice never shows over another. A different `hub_id` in a hub's own zone is not a case any more: each hub has its own replica (F1's "Watchtower moved to <mac_name>" notice is gone).
- **Unverified.** The `shared` scope (another Apple ID: share accept, leaving the shares, the account switch under it) runs only against fakes; nothing of it has run on a device yet. The same-Apple-ID path follows S0 (d).
- **Known limits.** The phone takes a keybox that differs from the one the replica held before the hello as the answer, a waiting one only when its `link_code_tag` names this scan's code (an older hub that sends none shows no code: fails closed), and a `linked` one only when `linked_at` ≥ the code's `iat` (a binding by time on the Mac's clock); an identical answer to a second scan (the same grant, unchanged `decided_at`) reads as no answer; a refusal carries its own time as `decided_at`, so a repeated refusal is a new answer. A removal during a capture of that hub ends the capture (stopped, saved and held, above). Audio in `.held/unreadable` is never sent and has no screen. A held recording of a hub this phone never links again waits for good. A rescan's session reset (`prepare`) logs a recording problem but shows no notice. An unlinked live phone runs no session at all (Welcome). A re-scan of a linked Mac ends that Mac's link (a re-pair; the Mac is not told) whenever the replica's keybox does not read as linked: absent, unlinked or revoked, and also unreadable (a newer Mac's format, an envelope that does not open, a read error) — on a re-scan an unreadable keybox counts as not granted, unlike the removal check, which never judges one. A fetch failure alone does not end it: the scan reads the keybox the replica already holds. The old link ends before the share accept, so a `shared` re-pair whose share accept fails leaves no link to that Mac. The seq floor a removal leaves is kept in memory only: a relaunch between an unlink and the next scan of that Mac restarts the phone's seqs, which the hub absorbs at the re-link (`HubSyncState.linkDevice`).

### Several Macs (multi-hub F2)

One phone links up to `HubRegistry.limit` = 4 Macs (multi-hub spec §5–§7, plan `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-13-mobile-multi-hub-f2-phone.md`). Each Mac is its own `HubSession`; nothing is shared between hubs but the app-wide parts and the recordings hold.

- **Registry and paths.** `HubRegistry` (`Sources/App/Hubs/`, GRDB, `hubs.sqlite` in the app's own Application Support, *outside* the app group — a lock on an app-group file at suspension gets the app killed, 0xdead10cc, #232): one `HubRow` per Mac (`LinkRecord` as JSON, flavor, `sort_order`, state `linked`/`unlinked_by_mac`, `grant_missing_since`, last heartbeat, `linked_on`) plus the active hub id; `upsert` refuses a fifth hub (`HubRegistryError.full`), `remove`/`move` renumber. `HubPaths` per hub id: replica `<group>/hubs/<hub_id>/replica.sqlite` (in the app group so the notification extensions may read it; they do not yet), transport store `<appSupport>/hubs/<hub_id>/cloudkit-transport.sqlite`, audio `phone-recordings/hubs/<hub_id>/`; the hold `phone-recordings/.held` is shared across hubs behind `HoldGate`. A hub id is a path component, so `HubPaths.isValidHubID` (non-empty, no `/`, `\`, NUL, no leading `.`) gates every path and a removal's recursive delete. `HubPaths.prepare` creates the three hub directories class C (D8: ciphertext the background reaches after the first unlock). `HubSessionFactory.live(paths:keys:)` builds a session (phone-role `CloudKitTransport` on the hub's own `TransportStore`, opened by `AppEnvironment.liveTransportStore`, which also protects its stash; hub replica, hub recordings folder, the shared hold; this phone's keys for the hub).
- **`HubSession` and `AppEnvironment`.** `HubSession` (`Sources/App/Hubs/`) is what `AppEnvironment` was in F1: the replica, hydrator, outbox and relay feed over one transport, the observed slices, the recorder and uploader, `DeviceSettings`, the fetch machinery (fetch depths, push follow-up, `PushCompletion`) and the safety poll. `AppEnvironment` now holds the sessions by hub id in the registry's order and the app-wide parts (navigation, recaps seen, the badge); the views read the per-hub API forwarded to the active session. `AppEnvironment.live` builds one session per registry row, the registry's active hub on screen (else the first), and none when there are no rows; a row the Mac removed is built stopped and frozen. Tests build `AppEnvironment.single(_:defaults:)`.
- **E2E on several Macs** (mobile E2E plan, "Ordering with the multi-hub work"). One device identity (`PhoneKeyStore.identity()`, the key-derived device id) for every hub; keys per hub under its hub id (`PhoneKeyStore.hubLink(hubID)`, the `CachingPhoneKeyStore` over the Keychain). Each `HubSession` builds its own sealed stack for its `hub_id` (`PhoneKeys(store:hubID:)`): an `EnvelopeReplicaOpener` replica, a `PhoneKeyProvider` `SecureTransport` for the outbox, the uploader, `DeviceSettings`, the relay feed and the link flow, its own seq ceiling in its own replica (F29; `raiseSeqFloor` carries a removed session's ceiling to a rebuilt one of the same hub), and its own unlock observer and keybox signal (`onKeyboxChanged`, `onKeysAvailable`), which `AppRoot` wires to `LinkingViewModel.keyboxChanged(hubID:)` and `reconcileKeyboxKeys(hubID:)`. A session whose hub id is not a UUID or with no device id yet runs the key-less wiring (nothing sealed, every batch refused). A session takes only its own hub's keys: `PhoneKeys` of another hub throw `HubSessionError.keysOfAnotherHub` at build (the demo's Macs share the demo pairing on purpose). **A hub without stored link keys never sends** (port review I-1): its session (`hubLink`, from the hub's public signing key copy) wipes its transport store's queue at init and never starts its CloudKit engine (`startsEngine`), and `AppEnvironment.live` builds such a row stopped, before its boot — a phone upgrading from a build before E2E may hold a bound per-hub store whose unacknowledged saves are plaintext (an action, a `device` record, a recording upload with its plain audio). The launch runs `dropUnencryptedLinks` first (before the orphan pass), which removes the hub with the re-scan notice. A Keychain that cannot say (a read error) starts no engine and wipes nothing for that launch. Unlink, Forget and an account change delete only that hub's keys; a freeze keeps them. The demo's two Macs both run on the demo pairing (`DemoSeed.phoneKeys`), each seeded through the demo hub with its own seq ceiling key (`demo.hubSeqCeiling[.<hub_id>]`). The ask-alert subscription per hub/scope (D14) belongs to A13 with F2's per-hub notifications.
- **One hub's failure stays in that hub** (spec §6.3). A row whose session cannot be built at launch (its replica or transport store will not open, `prepare` fails) is logged and gets no session: it goes into `AppEnvironment.unopenedHubIDs`, the other hubs boot, and the first row that has a session goes on screen (`activate` refuses a hub with no session). The switcher lists the row with "Couldn't open this Mac's data on this iPhone" (`HubListModel.unopenedProblem`), not selectable; Settings → Macs offers it **Forget** (`MacsModel.action`), which removes its row and Settings keys only, with no farewell (it has no transport): nothing is deleted, so its directories are an orphan for the next launch's pass, which builds a session, hands its recordings to the hold and keeps the files if that fails. Only when every row fails does `live` throw (`AppEnvironmentError.noHubOpened`) and the app show `BootFailureView`, not Welcome: the links still exist. A row that does not decode is skipped and logged by `HubRegistry.rows()` (it still counts toward the limit); `HubBoot` checks the files on disk against `hubIDs()`, every row readable or not, so its files are never taken for an orphan's.
- **The forwarders are total.** When the last hub goes (unlink, Forget, an account change), `remove` keeps the removed active session (already shut down) as `retained` until another hub activates, and `activeSession` reads the active session, else that one, else the first session (a link being made): a view still on screen while the root turns to Welcome — the REC bar, the recorder cover, the tab badge — reads it instead of trapping. Only an environment that never held a session traps, and the root never shows the tabs for it.
- **Roles and cadence** (`SessionRole`, `SessionCadence`). The session on screen is `active`, every other `inactive` (`applyRoles` on each switch). Safety poll: active 60 s; inactive 300 s, back to 60 s while it has unsent actions or uploads (so its echoes return). Foreground return: the active session fetches at once unless its last success is under 5 s old; an inactive one only when it is at least 300 s stale. Nothing polls in the background, and every poll pauses while the app is not active (`setActive` for every session).
- **Push routing.** A silent push is routed by its `CKDatabaseNotification` database scope (`PushScope`) to the sessions linked through that scope (`HubSession.databaseScope`); with an unknown scope every session fetches, the demo always. Each fetch is the session's own `handlePush`; the outcome is `.newData` when any brought something, `.failed` when every one failed, else `.noData`, and goes through `PushCompletion` (25 s deadline) as before. `AppEnvironment.waitForBoot` waits for every session's boot.
- **Badge.** The app icon's badge is the sum of the open asks across the hubs that can answer them (`openAskTotal`; a hub the Mac removed, `writesAllowed == false`, is left out until Forget, as is it from the chip's other-Macs count), seeded silently at init so a launch never flashes 0. Per-hub notification content, `ask-alerts-<hub_id>` subscriptions and tap-to-activate belong to A13 (board #17).
- **`DeviceSettings` per hub.** The Workbench choices are keyed `hub.<hub_id>.device.*`. A choice made while no hub is linked is kept under the unlinked keys and adopted by the hub whose link completes (`adoptUnlinkedChoices`, in `LinkHost.linked`); a removal deletes the hub's keys (`removeChoices(of:)`). The legacy global keys (`typingRequested`, `startSessions`) are moved by `HubBoot.migrateF1`.
- **Link flow per hub.** A scan builds the candidate hub's session (`LinkHost.prepare`), reads its keybox (`fetchKeybox`, `refresh(.link)`), and on `linked` adds the registry row and puts the hub on screen (above, "The scan"). A fifth Mac is refused (`.tooManyMacs`) and an invalid hub id never reaches disk. `AppRoot.removeHub` is the one removal path (a second call for the same hub waits for the first and counts nothing): it takes the registry row **before its first await** (a kill afterwards leaves no row that would resurrect the hub; leftover files are an orphan the next launch clears), activates the next Mac when the hub was on screen (none → Welcome), sends the `unlinked` farewell through the hub's own session transport (10 s hard cap, best effort), stops a running capture, shuts the session down, hands its recordings to the hold, drops its Settings keys and deletes its directories. A hand-over that reports a problem keeps the hub's files for the next launch's orphan pass.
- **The Mac removes the phone = freeze.** `TransportEvent.unlinked` or the missing grant (above) marks the row `unlinked_by_mac` (`markRemovedByMac`) and `HubSession.freeze(as:)` stops the session's sync and writes — the replica stays readable, the outbox, uploader and Settings toggles send nothing (`writesAllowed: false`) — until the owner chooses **Forget**. The same freeze is applied to such a row at boot. A rescan of a frozen hub starts it again from empty files.
- **Account change.** `accountChanged` compares the phone's `userRecordName` with each link's: another account removes every hub (`keepRecordings: false`: nothing is carried, recordings are reported "Not sent"), a sign-out keeps them.
- **F1 migration** (`HubBoot.migrateF1`, before any session opens). It runs once, when the registry is empty and `link.record` holds a link: the link's replica, transport store and audio move into the hub's directories (side files `-wal`/`-shm`/`-journal` first, the database last, so a database at the target means the move finished; a failed move throws before the registry is written and the next launch retries), its Workbench choices into the hub's keys, and the link into the registry as the active hub; then the legacy link keys go. An unreadable `link.record` touches nothing. With rows present, leftover legacy keys are cleared. With an empty registry and no link, the unlinked phone's legacy replica and transport store are deleted (the audio stays). `HubBoot.removeOrphans` then clears hub directories with no row (other than the pending link's hub): a session built on each hands its recordings to the hold, then its files go; the launch sweep of loose files deletes regular files only (the hold and `hubs` folders stay).
- **Recording across hubs.** One microphone: the recorder that owns it or the recorder screen (`PhoneRecorderController.holdsMicrophoneOrScreen`: starting, capturing, paused, saving, or showing its result until closed) stays `env.recorder` after a switch and blocks `canStartCapture` on every hub until closed. A recording finishes on the hub it started on: that session saves it and its uploader sends it to that Mac; the recorder says "Recording for <Mac>" when that is not the hub on screen. The Recordings list shows the active hub's recordings plus the held ones for Macs with no session (`AppEnvironment.heldRecordings`), never another linked hub's; so the recorder's **See recordings** after a capture for another Mac puts that Mac on screen first (`showRecordings`).
- **Switcher.** `HubSwitcherChip` (Now and the Workbench list, leading toolbar slot; shown with one Mac too, as the way to Add Mac) shows the active Mac's name and the open asks on the other Macs, and opens `HubListSheet`: per Mac the name (a short hub id suffix when two share it), flavor, online dot (heartbeat under 2100 s old), "Offline N days", "Removed this phone", the last fetch's problem line and the ask count; **Add Mac** is disabled at 4 (`HubListModel`). A switch (`AppEnvironment.activate`) resets navigation to the root and rebuilds the tab content (`.id` on the `TabView`); the recorder cover, the scan cover and the banners live outside it, so a running capture survives.
- **Settings → Macs** (`MacsView`/`MacsModel`). One row per Mac: reorder (Edit, `onMove`, writes the registry through `moveHub`, blocked while a link flow is busy) and one destructive swipe action per row (`MacsModel.action`, disabled while busy) — a removed Mac offers **Forget**; a linked Mac offline for 7 days or more (`HubListModel.forgetAfterDays`) offers **Forget**, which unlinks with the farewell; otherwise **Unlink**. The demo hides the removal actions (`MacsView.offersRemoval`). Settings itself shows the active Mac's status, Last sync, accounts and Workbench choices, a **Macs** row leading here, and a button for the active Mac with the same action and copy as its row in Macs (`MacsModel.action`: **Forget <Mac>** for one that removed this phone or is long offline, else **Unlink <Mac>**).
- **Demo.** `DemoSeed.hubs` = "Acme Mac" (`demo-hub`) and "Home Mac" (`demo-hub-home`), each its own in-memory transport, replica and recordings folder; `AppRoot.demo` fills an in-memory registry from them, so the chip, switcher and Macs list run unchanged.
- **Removed in F2:** the switch prompt, the moved notice, `AppRoot.Restarter`, `LinkHost.retireEnvironment` and the farewell transport (`AppEnvironment.unlinkedHubID` is gone; `DeviceSettings.unlinkedHubID` only names the unlinked choices' keys).

### Workbench and Now tabs, read-only (B5)

- **One read for the app.** `AppEnvironment.workbenchReplica` observes the replica and decodes every Workbench slice (`ReplicaStore.payloads(of:from:)` + the Kit mirrors) plus the heartbeat (through Settings' logging decode) into one `WorkbenchReplicaSnapshot`. An undecodable record is skipped and counted in `skippedRecords`, logged only when the counts change. Now, Workbench and the tab badge (open-ask count) share it. Every replica observation on the phone (calendar, recordings, settings, session detail, workbench, recap) runs through `ResilientObservation`: GRDB ends an observation for good on its first error, so after one it logs, reports to `ObservationHealth`, waits 1 s (doubling to 30 s, back to 1 s after a value) and starts a fresh one; `cancel()` or release stops it for good.
- **Per-kind reads (board #724).** No observation tracks `slice_records`. Each uses `ValueObservation.tracking(regions:fetch:)` over `slice_kind_versions` plus its own sidecar tables (the workbench and Settings add `pending_actions`; recordings and the recap add `phone_recordings` and `phone_recording_marks`), and its fetch reads its kinds' stamps first. A model-owned `ReplicaDecodeCache` keeps each kind's (or single record's) decoded value with the stamp it was read at: an unchanged stamp returns it undecoded, so any write still wakes the fetch, but a heartbeat write decodes only the heartbeat (in the workbench, recordings and Settings snapshots), a calendar write never the board, a board write never the calendar; the overlay and the recordings ledger are re-read every time (small). The cache lives in the model, so a restart (an error, a resume) re-decodes only what changed — except where the observed source changes: Settings starts an empty cache when it observes another replica or device id (the link flow swaps the environment; the old Mac's heartbeat must never be served), and the recap starts one on each `observe`, so earlier transcripts' bodies are not kept for the view's lifetime. The kinds read: workbench — `workbench`, `workbench_target`, `terminal_session`, `owner_ask`, `workbench_comment`, `heartbeat`, `device_grant`; calendar — `calendar_event`, `meeting_transcript`; recordings — `recording_job`, `heartbeat`; Settings — `heartbeat`, `device_grant`; session detail — `session_report`, `session_timeline`; recap — the `meeting_transcript` stamp for its segments asset. `*Snapshot.read(from:store:)` without a cache decodes everything (tests, one-off reads). Mobile E2E: a list read skips the rows it cannot open while locked and returns the rest without throwing, so the cache keeps nothing from a read during which `ReplicaStore.keyRefusalCount` moved; the read after the unlock (every live observation restarts at `protectedDataDidBecomeAvailable`) decodes those kinds again although their stamps did not move (Review Focus 1). A read that throws `.locked` (a single record) stores nothing either.
- **Background rest (board #724).** `ReplicaObservationGate.shared` suspends every `ResilientObservation` while the app is in the background: `WatchtowerMobileApp` drives it from the scene phase (`.background` only — an inactive app is still on screen), and a launch that reads `.background` in `AppDelegate.didFinishLaunching` (a silent push, a prewarm — in a scene-based app a tap too) starts suspended; the scene's first non-background phase resumes it. A suspended observation holds no GRDB observation, so no replica write wakes or decodes anything; one created meanwhile starts on the resume. The resume starts each afresh: its first value is the latest, delivered once, decoding only the kinds written while away. Nothing background work needs is an observation: the outbox, the uploader, the relay feed and the notification paths read the store directly. The gate is not driven while the app only hosts XCTest; tests build their own gates.
- **No lock across a suspension (0xdead10cc, board #724).** The live replica sits in the shared app-group container, and iOS kills an app suspended while it holds a lock on a file there. Rule: no replica transaction may be open when iOS can suspend the app. (1) A silent push's work runs inside a background task (`BackgroundWork`, `UIApplication.beginBackgroundTask`): begun before the fetch, ended when the fetch serving the push returns — after a completion `PushCompletion` sent at the 25 s deadline too — so the app is not suspended mid-fetch. (2) The live replica opens with GRDB's `observesSuspensionNotifications` (`ReplicaStore(path:observesSuspension:)`, only the app-group replica of a `.cloudKit` environment), and `DatabaseSuspension.shared` posts `Database.suspendNotification` whenever the app is in the background (scene phase) with nothing holding it, and `resumeNotification` otherwise. Holders, each a named `BackgroundWork` (a background task plus a hold, ended once; when iOS grants no time — `beginBackgroundTask` returns `.invalid` — the hold is taken only in the foreground and released once at the end, while in the background none is taken, since no expiration would suspend the replica again; no task is ended) except the capture: a push's work (`silent-push-fetch`, from `begin` to the fetch's return, and on past it until the boot is done — `AppEnvironment.waitForBoot`, without holding back the push's completion); the boot (`app-boot`, taken in the live environment's init — before a push launching the app can end its own work — and ended when the boot's launch fetch, outbox sweep and launch recovery are done, or at once by `stop()`); every recording save (`recording-save`, through `PhoneRecorderController.beginSave`: Stop, the 3-hour cap, a long-pause auto-finish, a dead engine — a paused capture holds nothing, so a save in the background would otherwise meet a suspended replica); and a capture whose audio runs (`PhoneRecorderController.onAudioRunning`: the app stays alive in the background and writes the ledger). `AppEnvironment.holdReplica` wires the recorder's two. When the push's background task expires, the push's own hold is released and the replica rests in the background before the task ends — suspended unless other work (a running capture) still holds it; the expiration never takes another holder's hold. A suspension interrupts and rolls back an open transaction, and a later statement fails with `SQLITE_INTERRUPT` (logged by its caller) instead of taking a lock. When a push's fetch returns with the app still in the background, it rests again. The CloudKit transport's own store (`cloudkit-transport.sqlite`) cannot be suspended — CKSyncEngine writes it from its own callbacks at any moment — so the phone keeps it outside the app group, in its own Application Support (no extension reads it; `AppEnvironment.liveTransportStorePath`): an earlier build's store in the app group moves there once on launch with its `-wal`, `-shm` and `-journal` files (side files first, the database last; a failed move puts them back and opens the old path for that launch). The path is resolved once per process (`AppRoot.live` hands it to every environment and the wipe; `TransportStorePathResolver` caches it), so no move runs under a store the process has opened. Stashed assets (`transport-assets`) stay where they are, referenced by absolute path. The Mac hub's store is unchanged. Tests: `BackgroundWorkTests` (push expiration keeps a capture's hold, boot hold), `RecorderTests.testAnAutoFinishInTheBackgroundHoldsTheReplicaForTheSave`, `AppRootWiringTests` (transport store move), `ReplicaTests.testASuspendedReplicaRefusesWritesUntilResumed` (Kit).
- **Pure screen models** (`WorkbenchCardModel`, `WorkbenchMenuModel`, `SessionRowModel`, `WaitingCardModel`, `BoardModel`, `BoardTargetDetailModel`, `NowModel`) carry every string and colour a screen draws; the views only lay them out.
- **Sessions are drawn as given.** Dot tone, ring (`is_ring`) and caption come from the record (`PhoneTone(_:)` maps the five published tones to the system colours of spec §14; an unknown tone draws secondary); the record's glyph stays in the model and the phone draws the dot only (design pass §9.1). The phone adds only the age of a session that is not live. A session row's meta line (`SessionRowModel.metaItems`): the caption in the session's tone, then `#<report_target_id> · <done>/<total>` (absent without `report_target_id`), the `report_pr_line` and "N closed" in secondary. `MetaLine` shows the longest prefix of whole items that fits, ends a shortened line in "…" and gives VoiceOver every item.
- **Counts.** Board progress is `done_targets / (open_targets + done_targets)`, no bar at 0 of 0. "todo" is `open − in progress − blocked`. Session-state counts are the hub's `session_counts`; "not running" is not shown. Now sums them over all workbenches.
- **Board filters.** Open (`todo`, `in_progress`, `in_review`, `blocked`), In progress, Blocked and Archive. Archive shows only `archived` records and the others never do; a matching target keeps its ancestors from the same pool for context, and under Open a shown parent keeps its done children (green check, title struck through and secondary); chip counts stay match-only. A node has a disclosure only with visible children.
- **Sibling order** is the Mac's: priority high/medium/other, then status in_progress/in_review/blocked/todo/done/other, then id (`BoardModel.boardOrder`, a third twin of Core's `WorkbenchBoardOrder` and Go's `boardSiblingOrder`; change all three together).
- **Orange only for waiting and asks.** Views paint only tones the screen models hand them. Each model lists its `ToneUse`s with a `ToneRole` decided from the data; `OrangeToneTests` runs every DemoSeed screen and fails on orange outside a waiting or ask role, and checks that the data-driven tones (status, priority, Mac chip) are never orange. The SwiftLint rule `orange_outside_phone_tone` (WatchtowerMobile only) forbids naming orange under `Sources/Features/` and `Sources/DesignSystem/` outside `Sources/DesignSystem/PhoneTone.swift` (`PhoneTone.waitingForYou`); the design layer itself gets orange only from `PhoneTone`. The project has no snapshot library.
- **DemoSeed** adds three workbenches (one without sessions and with an empty board), ten sessions covering every state kind, open REVIEW/ASK/CHECK asks plus closed ones, a board tree with archived targets and a comment thread.

### Session detail (B7)

- **Reached from** a session row on the Workbench tab or in a target's detail (`SessionRoute`). `SessionDetailModel` is the pure screen model: the header (title, target chip that opens the target, the target's branch or else the workbench's, "Claude Code · <age>", the Mac's state caption), the session's open asks newest first ("since <oldest age>"; the rows open nothing until the answer flow), "Needs approval on the Mac" for a `needs_approval` session (the phone never answers a permission prompt), the report card and the timeline. No report → header and timeline only.
- **Report card.** "done / total targets" with one segment per target up to 24 (more: one segment per run, sized by its count): done green, in progress and in review accent, blocked red, the rest grey; in-progress segments are not orange (orange stays for waiting and asks). ### Phone recorder and uploader (C6, E2E9, E2E17)

- **Capture.** `PhoneRecorderController` (owned by `AppEnvironment`, so a capture survives navigation and Minimize) over the `AudioCaptureEngine` seam; on a device `AVAudioCaptureEngine` writes AAC 44.1 kHz mono 64 kbps `.m4a` (`aac-64k-mono`) as a fragmented MP4 (10 s fragments), so a capture cut short by a kill stays playable up to its last fragment. Files land in Application Support `phone-recordings/<uuid>.m4a` (`completeUnlessOpen`); the plain file and the sealed `<uuid>.enc` both stay until the Mac acknowledges the recording (ask #89 option A). An interruption (a call, Siri) pauses into the same file and the timer leaves the gap out; one that ends without `.shouldResume` stays paused like the Pause button. **A pause releases the audio** (board #724): the engine stops, the session is deactivated with `.notifyOthersOnDeactivation`, the route watch rests (the resume's format check catches a changed microphone) and the ticker stops; the resume re-activates and restarts them. So a paused capture keeps nothing running, and the `audio` background mode no longer keeps the app alive. **A capture paused for 60 min** (`PhoneRecorderController.pauseLimit`, owner decision Q5; counted from the pause or the interruption's start) is finished and saved by itself through Stop's own save and upload path: the row is marked `auto_finished`, the Saved screen shows "Finished automatically after 60 minutes paused.", a local notification says so, and the Recordings list subtitle adds "auto-finished after a 60 min pause". The wait is an injected sleeper; a suspended app's timer cannot fire, so the return to the foreground also finishes a pause past the limit. An interruption longer than the limit finishes the capture the same way, and its later end (even with `.shouldResume`) changes nothing. An auto-finish whose save fails posts "Recording could not be saved" (`onAutoFinishFailed`), since the owner is usually away. A route or configuration change (AirPods, CarPlay) ends the capture with a notice and saves what was written (POC choice). Auto-stop at 3 h with a notice 5 minutes before (a local notification when allowed). Under 1 s is discarded. An event's Record ties the capture to its event; the Calendar's mic button records a voice note (no event).
- **Screens.** `RecordingView`: REC mark, "<Meeting> | No meeting", timer, red waveform, Pause | Stop | Mark moment, Minimize; after Stop the Saved screen shows "Saved · mm:ss" and the recording's way to the Mac ("Waiting for the Mac to wake" while the heartbeat is stale, re-rendered every 30 s). Red is the recording colour.
- **Sealing at finalize** (`RecordingUploader.sealPending`, run by the recorder's stop before the upload pass, and again on unlock). A finished recording is sealed once: the audio is encrypted to `<id>.enc` under a fresh `K_asset` (`ChunkedAssetCipher`), the `recording_upload` record (asset descriptor inside) is sealed by `SecureTransport`, the envelope, its wire name and the sealed file are stored in one row update (`phone_recordings.wire_name`). The plain `.m4a` stays (D8, ask #89 option A) until the Mac confirms receipt. A locked keychain leaves the row `waiting` with its plain file, sealed after unlock. The 90 MB cap is checked on the plain size. The launch sweep keeps both files of every undelivered row. A re-seal (an envelope of an epoch the phone has moved past) writes a new file, `<id>-<n>.enc`, never the one an older queued save may still point at; the row keeps the old `sealed_path` (and the sweep the old file) until the new envelope is stored. The superseded file goes only after the new seal's save has replaced the transport's queued save of that record (the pass's successful save), or, if no save succeeds first, by the next launch sweep. A row whose save is in flight is left alone by a seal pass (`sendingIDs`), and after the save the send keeps both the file it saved and whatever seal the row stores by then.
- **Across a wipe** (main's #63 hold). The hold moves the plain `.m4a` into `.held`. The sealed copy and the stored envelope belong to the old pairing and are not carried. A held recording adopted after a re-link is registered again and sealed fresh under the new link: `RecordingUploader.register` drops its stale seal files. One that no environment adopts is swept from the recordings folder.
- **Ledger** (`phone_recordings`, `RecordingUploader` is its only writer): `recording` (row from the first second; marks are stored as tapped) → `waiting` (finalized, then sealed) → `uploading` (the stored envelope saved into RelayZone with the sealed file) → `delivered` (the hub's `received`: the plain and the sealed file are deleted, ack-then-delete) or `failed` (the hub's message; both files stay, and Retry re-sends the stored envelope unchanged, the same record name and seq). Byte identity holds within an epoch only. A `waiting`/`uploading` row whose stored envelope was sealed under an epoch older than the phone's current one has its seal dropped at the start of each pass, and is sealed again under the current epoch (`RecordingUploader.unsealStaleEpochs`, final review Kit I1). The re-seal uses the same wire name, a new envelope and a fresh asset key. Otherwise a recording sealed before two rotations, or delivered after the hub's 7-day grace, would be refused for good. A local failure (file gone, over 90 MB, no readable audio) offers no Retry. File names are stored relative to the recordings folder, which iOS may move between launches. Columns added in place for board #724: `upload_attempts`, `last_attempt_at`, `auto_finished` (the replica's `ALTER TABLE … ADD COLUMN` pattern; the CHECK rebuild carries them).
- **Bounded upload retry** (board #724). Every save handed to the transport counts an attempt (counted before the save, so an attempt the process dies in still spends its step). A new `waiting` row goes out on the next pass; a row saved before is saved again only when its backoff is due — 1 min, 5 min, 30 min, 2 h, 6 h after the latest attempt (`RecordingUploader.retryDelays`) — and at most 6 times (`PhoneRecording.uploadAttemptCap`). Past the cap the row is "Upload stuck" (`isUploadStuck`, a `waiting`/`uploading` row): the Saved screen, the Recordings list and the agenda show it as a failure with **Retry**, which resets the backoff (`resetPhoneRecordingForRetry`) and sends at once — but only while the Mac reads online: while its heartbeat is stale or absent the row reads "Waiting for the Mac to wake" whatever its attempts (`PhoneUploadStage` checks the Mac first). The backoff has no timer of its own: a due re-send goes out on the next upload pass, and passes run only on triggers — the return to the foreground (the boot's pass on a foreground launch), a capture's finish, a link (`setLinkedDevice`) and the owner's Retry. A `received` echo ends the retries (a delivered row is never sent). Re-sends happen only while the app is active: `AppEnvironment` passes the uploader a gate (`UploadRetryGate`) that follows the scene phase (`setActive`) and starts from `UIApplication.applicationState`, and each opening runs an upload pass after the boot.
- **Launch.** `recoverOnLaunch` finalizes a `recording` row from its file and deletes files no row points at. The boot's upload pass runs only in the foreground; a launch into the background (a silent push) uploads nothing — new rows included — until the first activation. An `uploading` row already sits in the transport's durable send queue, so it is re-saved (the relaunch retry, byte for byte the stored envelope; the hub's ledger absorbs duplicates) only by the first foreground pass of a process that finds the Mac online (`RecordingUploader`'s `macOnline`, which the app reads as `RelayFeed.isDesktopReachable` over the replica's heartbeat) and only when due; a background pass, or one while the Mac sleeps, makes and counts no re-save and leaves that retry armed — a recording merely waiting for a sleeping Mac never walks toward "Upload stuck". Later passes send `waiting` rows only, and overlapping passes merge. One exception: an `uploading` row this pass sealed again (its envelope was of an epoch the phone has moved past, or a pre-E2E build sent it in the clear) is re-sent by that same pass, Mac online or not, backoff or not, because its new envelope was never saved. After a transport wipe the next foreground pass re-saves `uploading` rows without waiting for the backoff or the Mac (the queue that held them is gone). Uploads wait while no device id is set.
- **Marks** stay on the phone (`phone_recording_marks`) and never travel in the upload payload (spec §5.3); the recap view shows them as jump points.

## Known limits (POC)

- **Several hubs, one version.** An unknown `recording_upload` status reads as `pending` on both sides. An older hub next to a newer one (multi-hub phase F, or a downgrade mid-ingest) could ingest or re-echo an upload whose status the newer hub wrote (decode an unknown status as a distinct inert case on both sides if it matters).
- **Notifications** are not built (A13), and nothing here has run against real iCloud yet (A14, B16, C9 device checks).
- **Linking in `shared` scope is written to spec, verified only on fakes.** Spike S0(c) (does closing the public link keep the accepted phone?) was never run; only S0(d) (same Apple ID) passed on a device. The hub builds the spec's primary path (close the link, remove unbound participants) and not the F2 fallback. `CloudKitShareService` and the creator values CloudKit reports are untested until the device smoke.
- **Typing request notice.** The macOS notice for a phone's typing request opens nothing when clicked; the owner answers in Settings → Mobile. A request that arrives while the app is in front shows only on the phone's row.
- **Typing grants from before B18.** A phone allowed to type before it ever sent `typing_requested` (allowed from Settings without asking, or linked before B18) has the request stored as off, so turning its toggle off does not revoke the grant; Revoke on the Mac does. The phone sends no line while its own toggle is off.
- **A cancel that overtakes its line.** `session_input_cancel` for a line the hub has not read yet is a no-op; the line is then handled as usual.
- **A phone's own unlink** (`unlinked: true` in its `device` record) removes it on the hub as Remove does, once the relay reads the record: until then (and if the record never reaches iCloud) the phone stays in `devices`, in the list and past the device gate.
- **Shared scope: one phone per code.** The link closes when the first phone uses the code, and its sweep removes every participant not bound to a linked phone. A second phone that accepted the public link but had not yet written its hello by then is swept and gets no keybox (a waiting request is refused `used_code` when the first one links, F17); phones scan in turn, each with its own code.
- **A removed phone is told through its keybox** (D9). Remove rotates the epoch and publishes the phone's keybox as `revoked` for 7 days, which a same-Apple-ID phone (no zone vanishes for it) and a shared one both read as "This Mac removed this phone". A phone that does not fetch within those 7 days misses the revoked keybox, but then finds its keybox gone (#64): at the second fetch at least 30 s after the first one that missed it, once a heartbeat newer than the link has arrived (the hub writes one every 15 min, and at once when its accounts or `sharing` change), it reads the removal too; until then it gets no newer keys, so nothing published after the removal opens for it, and the hub drops its relay records unanswered. A link saved by a build before 2026-10-12 has no device id or link time and is told only by the revoked keybox.
- **Metadata stays visible** (spec §4): record counts and churn, coarse sizes, timing, hub and device ids in zone and fixed record names, Apple IDs and APNs tokens (see End-to-end encryption → Metadata).
- **`K_meta` is not rotated** on Remove (D5): a removed phone can still map future opaque names of kinds and ids it already knows. Rotating it needs a delete of every old-named record.
- **Relay ordering** (D4): no per-device sequence gate; replay is stopped by the wire-name ledger, the authenticated age window and the per-record `(epoch, seq)` guard.
- **Availability.** Apple or an Apple-ID attacker can delete, withhold or delay records; a RelayZone share participant can trample other phones' records until swept. The phone shows staleness; it cannot prevent it.
- **The notification extension's key.** `K_alert` (titles of ask alerts) is readable after the first unlock, by design: a low-value key, separate from `K_data`.
- **Restore to new hardware, reinstall or a new Mac** means re-pairing: no key is backed up or synced.
- **Shared-scope ask alerts** ride the sync engine's best-effort silent push (D14) and can be late; private scope uses a `CKQuerySubscription` (not registered until A13).
- **No suspected-leak rotation in the UI**: `RotationReason.manual` exists, nothing calls it yet.
- **One hub process per sidecar.** The damaged-identity recovery treats every second `.corrupt` read as damage, and two signed builds sharing one sidecar would cut each other.
- **CloudKit environments** must match between the Mac and the phone (see above); a mismatch fails silently.
