## PURGE-01 — Schedule and cancel

**Status:** Enforced

**Observable:** With purge on revoked access enabled, an account's data is never deleted without a notice of at least 72 hours, shown from the moment the purge is scheduled (red line in Settings → Connections, Home banner, a notification). A revoked account is scheduled due 72 h after the first scheduling pass that sees it; reconnecting before then cancels it, and so does confirmed access after the revocation (a clean Jira/Linear pass, which never writes `ok`); an account in `error` at the due time waits. A disconnect that makes no authenticated call (`google disconnect <service>`) is no confirmed access. When no account of a source has confirmed access for 30 days (offline time counts; disabled accounts count as accounts of their source), each account of it is scheduled, due 72 h later — unless the organization's policy forbids the source (#608, ask #572): its lease is held and its data kept; lease rows are scheduled only after the cycle's syncs ran, so a Mac back from a long absence first gets to prove access; fresh access cancels. A removed account keeps its data on removal (SLACK-01 unchanged), and once its own last confirmed access is 30 days old it gets its own lease purge due 72 h after scheduling; adding it back, or fresh access, cancels it. Turning the purge off schedules nothing and cancels every scheduled purge; a running purge always finishes and the account ends `purged` — unless it fails three times in a row (`failed`, the account untouched and syncing again; a new attempt needs a new notice), or the account proved access during the run (kept, sync clocks reset). A purge starts only from a row that is still scheduled and still due when it starts. A `purged` account accepts only an `ok` write (a Re-login), which revives it. Off by default; on in the corporate build.

**Mechanism:** `purge.Enabled(cfg)` decides the switch, strongest first: the organization's forced `PurgeRevokedData` managed preference (#608, `policy.PurgeRevokedData` through `policySwitch`) > `privacy.purge_revoked_data` > `purge.BuildDefault` (ldflags, `"on"` in the corp profile) > off. The `Set*AccountAuthState` writers keep `revoked_at` (set on `revoked` when empty or older than `access_ok_at`, cleared on `ok`, kept on `error`) and `access_ok_at` (set on `ok`), and refuse every write over a `purged` row except `ok` (`authStateWhereSQL`, `reenableOnLoginSQL`). Jira and Linear passes never write `ok` (LINEAR-03): the daemon calls `MarkAccountAccessOK`, which stamps `access_ok_at` only — Linear after a nil `Sync` error, Jira whenever the pass reached Atlassian (`ReachedSource`) and the error is not `ErrAuthRevoked`; a clean Confluence sync stamps its Jira account. `purge.Plan` reads only the account rows and the open ledger rows; a revoked row with `access_ok_at > revoked_at` is cancelled (an empty or unreadable `access_ok_at` is still revoked); every due date it sets is at least `Grace` after the pass. `purge.Apply` writes the decision (lease rows only when asked); `phaseAccountPurge` runs it first in every cycle without leases, `phaseAccountPurgeLeases` after `phaseExternalSync` with them. `purge.Run` re-plans the one account and starts it with a conditional `StartAccountPurge` that re-checks the account in the same statement (still revoked without newer access, or still past the lease cutoff), so an access proof landing after the re-plan leaves the row scheduled. An empty `access_ok_at` is no evidence and never schedules a lease.

**Why locked:** Deleting without the full notice, or not cancelling on a reconnect, destroys an owner's history over a password change or a provider glitch; not deleting on a real revocation leaves a company's data on a former employee's Mac, the reason the feature exists. A running purge left half-done would leave an account half-deleted and still syncing.

## PURGE-02 — Chunked delete and ledger

**Status:** Enforced

**Observable:** A purge deletes everything attributable to the account without AI — its raw rows, inbox items with their account-only situations and learned rules, knowledge-index documents, and the digests, tracks and people cards that belong to that account alone — and keeps AI text that mixes sources (briefings, Catch-Up recaps, ideas and decisions, memory, chats, automation outputs, transcripts), digests and tracks spanning several accounts, and calendar events tied to a recording or recap. Its sign-in files are deleted. The account row stays, `status = 'purged'`, disabled, its watermarks reset, so permalinks and labels still resolve and a Re-login starts over as a first sync. A purge never holds the database write lock for long — no step scans the knowledge tables inside its write transaction — and survives a daemon stop: it resumes where it was, with the same result as an uninterrupted run. It deletes only its own account's rows: Slack account `1:` never touches `10:`, and a sibling source's row with the same account id stays. Three failures in a row end it `failed` and the owner sees the error.

**Mechanism:** `db.PurgeSteps(source, id)` returns ordered, idempotent statements (dependents first). `RunPurgeStep` runs one transaction, one statement, at most `ChunkRows` (500) rows of the driving table; `(*DB).PurgeSteps` reads the stream-digest and Confluence-space ids first, outside any write transaction, so every knowledge delete is a primary-key range. `purge.Run` repeats each step until it affects nothing, counts the rows on the ledger (`AddPurgeRows`, which resets `failures`), stops on its time budget (`purgeCycleBudget`, 5 minutes per cycle) or a cancelled context and resumes the `running` row from the top next cycle, records a failed chunk in `error` (`FailAccountPurge`; the `MaxChunkFailures`-th in a row sets `failed`), deletes the token files outside any transaction unless access returned during the run (`PurgeAccessReturned`), then `FinishAccountPurge` requires a `running` row and marks it `done` and the account `purged` (`resetAccountSQL` + `purgedAccountSQL`) in one transaction — or keeps the account when access returned. Every CLI login and re-auth path refuses to write credentials for an account whose purge is `running` (`refuseLoginDuringPurge` over `db.AccountPurgeRunning`), so a login cannot land between that check and the token delete. KB documents are deleted directly (the `messages` `kb_dirty` trigger fires on UPDATE only). Every table with an account column is covered by a step of the source that owns it (the foreign key's account table, else `accountTableSources`; Slack's namespace-keyed tables in `slackNamespacedTables`) or named in `purgeKeptTables` (`imap_messages`, `account_purges`, `jira_user_map`, `linear_user_map`). The sync phases and the reaction poll skip accounts in `purgeHold` (purged, or a purge running; refreshed before the startup calendar sync too), so a syncer wired at startup never refills one.

**Why locked:** One big delete transaction would block every hook and the Desktop writer for its whole length (hooks give up after 500 ms / 1 s); a non-resumable purge would leave an account half-deleted after a restart; a new account-scoped table missing from the steps would silently survive every purge; deleting cross-account or AI-written rows would destroy the owner's own work.
