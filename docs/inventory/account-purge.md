## PURGE-01 — Schedule and cancel

**Status:** Enforced

**Observable:** With purge on revoked access enabled, an account's data is never deleted without a notice of at least 72 hours, shown from the moment the purge is scheduled (red line in the Connections tab, Home banner, a notification). A revoked account is scheduled due 72 h after the first scheduling pass that sees it; reconnecting before then cancels it, and so does confirmed access after the revocation (a clean Jira/Linear pass, which never writes `ok`); an account in `error` at the due time waits. A disconnect that makes no authenticated call (`google disconnect <service>`) is no confirmed access. When no account of a source has confirmed access for 30 days (offline time counts; disabled accounts count as accounts of their source), each account of it is scheduled, due 72 h later — only when the switch was turned on by a setting, a policy or the build (with the default on, a live account is never deleted for inactivity, A21) and unless the organization's policy forbids the source (#608, ask #572): its lease is held and its data kept; lease rows are scheduled only after the cycle's syncs ran, so a Mac back from a long absence first gets to prove access; fresh access cancels. A removed account keeps its data on removal (SLACK-01), and once its own last confirmed access is 30 days old it gets its own lease purge due 72 h after scheduling; a removed **Slack** account has a zero lease: the first pass after the remove schedules it, due 72 h later (#1769, A2). Adding it back, or fresh access, cancels it — also between the re-plan and the start of the purge, and a row scheduled before the account's latest confirmed access (added back and removed again before the next pass) is cancelled and rescheduled with a fresh 72 h notice. Turning the purge off schedules nothing and cancels every scheduled purge; a running purge always finishes and the account ends `purged` — unless it fails three times in a row (`failed`, the account untouched and syncing again; a new attempt needs a new notice), or the account proved access during the run (kept, sync clocks reset). A purge starts only from a row that is still scheduled and still due when it starts. A purge whose verify count finds rows left ends `failed` with the tables and counts. With the purge on, a revoked or removed account then **stays held** (spec §9): not synced and not scheduled again on its own (no new notice every 72 h), the failed row visible, until a Re-login or adding a removed account back confirms access (a same-second tie releases). A live account is never held: it keeps syncing and its lease schedules it again with a new notice. A zero-lease row also yields to access stamped in the second it was scheduled. A `purged` account accepts only an `ok` write (a Re-login), which revives it. **On by default** in every build and for every source, origin `default` (#1769, A1); the per-account rules are:

| Switch, origin | Live account | Removed account | Revoked account |
|---|---|---|---|
| off | nothing (scheduled rows cancelled) | nothing | nothing |
| on, origin `default` | no inactivity lease | Slack: zero lease; others: 30-day removed lease | 72 h |
| on, origin `setting`, `policy` or `build` | 30-day lease | same | 72 h |
| any, source forbidden by policy (POLICY-03) | lease held | lease held | 72 h |

**Mechanism:** `purge.Enabled(cfg)` decides the switch, strongest first: the organization's forced `PurgeRevokedData` managed preference (#608, `policy.PurgeRevokedData` through `policySwitch`) > `privacy.purge_revoked_data` > `purge.BuildDefault` (ldflags, `"on"` in the corp profile, origin `build`) > on with origin `default`; Go and the Desktop read one fixture of it, `internal/purge/testdata/default_cases.json`. `purge.Rules(on, origin, policy.SourceAllowed)` — in the daemon `purge.AccountRules(on, origin, accountSyncAllowed)`, the same rule over a per-account verdict that also stops an Atlassian API-token account under `AllowAtlassianAPIToken = false` (#1887) — gives each account a `Rule` (`RuleNone`, `RuleRevokedAndRemoved`, `RuleFull`); `purge.Plan`, `purge.Apply` and `purge.Run` take it, and both daemon passes build it the same way (`Daemon.purgeRules`). `leaseDeadlines` gives no deadline under `RuleNone`, none to a live account under `RuleRevokedAndRemoved`, and a removed Slack account's own `access_ok_at` (zero lease; the zero time when empty). The `Set*AccountAuthState` writers keep `revoked_at` (set on `revoked` when empty or older than `access_ok_at`, cleared on `ok`, kept on `error`) and `access_ok_at` (set on `ok`), and refuse every write over a `purged` row except `ok` (`authStateWhereSQL`, `reenableOnLoginSQL`). Jira and Linear passes never write `ok` (LINEAR-03): the daemon calls `MarkAccountAccessOK`, which stamps `access_ok_at` only — Linear after a nil `Sync` error, Jira whenever the pass reached Atlassian (`ReachedSource`) and the error is not `ErrAuthRevoked` or `ErrAPITokenDead` (a dead API token is `error` / `api_token_dead`, never `revoked`, #1887 D22); a clean Confluence sync stamps its Jira account. `purge.Plan` reads only the account rows and the open ledger rows; a revoked row with `access_ok_at > revoked_at` is cancelled (an empty or unreadable `access_ok_at` is still revoked); every due date it sets is at least `Grace` after the pass. `purge.Apply` writes the decision (lease rows only when asked); `phaseAccountPurge` runs it first in every cycle without leases, `phaseAccountPurgeLeases` after `phaseExternalSync` with them. `purge.Run` re-plans the one account with the same rules and starts it with a conditional `StartAccountPurge(id, cutoff, requireRemoved)` that re-checks the account in the same statement (still revoked without newer access, or still past the row's own cutoff from `purge.Cutoff` — `now − Lease`, or `now` with the account still `removed` for a zero-lease row), so an access proof or an "add again" landing after the re-plan leaves the row scheduled. An empty `access_ok_at` is no evidence and never schedules a lease.

**Why locked:** Deleting without the full notice, or not cancelling on a reconnect, destroys an owner's history over a password change or a provider glitch; not deleting on a real revocation leaves a company's data on a former employee's Mac, the reason the feature exists. A running purge left half-done would leave an account half-deleted and still syncing.

## PURGE-02 — Chunked delete and ledger

**Status:** Enforced

**Observable:** A purge deletes everything attributable to the account without AI — its raw rows, inbox items with their account-only situations and learned rules, knowledge-index documents, and the digests, tracks and people cards that belong to that account alone — and keeps AI text that mixes sources (briefings, Catch-Up recaps, ideas and decisions, memory, chats, automation outputs, transcripts), digests and tracks spanning several accounts, and calendar events tied to a recording or recap. Its sign-in files are deleted. The account row stays, `status = 'purged'`, disabled, its watermarks reset, so permalinks and labels still resolve and a Re-login starts over as a first sync. A purge never holds the database write lock for long — no step scans the knowledge tables inside its write transaction — and survives a daemon stop: it resumes where it was, with the same result as an uninterrupted run. It deletes only its own account's rows: Slack account `1:` never touches `10:`, and a sibling source's row with the same account id stays. Three failures in a row end it `failed` and the owner sees the error. A purge reported `done` left nothing behind: after the last step it counts what every step of the list it ran still matches, and any row left ends it `failed` with the tables and counts, the account held (#1769, A4); a shutdown or a spent budget during the count is a pause.

**Mechanism:** `db.PurgeSteps(source, id)` returns ordered, idempotent statements (dependents first). `RunPurgeStep` runs one transaction, one statement, at most `ChunkRows` (500) rows of the driving table; `(*DB).PurgeSteps` reads the stream-digest and Confluence-space ids first, outside any write transaction, so every knowledge delete is a primary-key range. `purge.Run` repeats each step until it affects nothing, counts the rows on the ledger (`AddPurgeRows`, which resets `failures`), stops on its time budget (`purgeCycleBudget`, 5 minutes per cycle) or a cancelled context and resumes the `running` row from the top next cycle, records a failed chunk in `error` (`FailAccountPurge`; the `MaxChunkFailures`-th in a row sets `failed`), verifies with `(*DB).PurgeLeft(ctx, steps)` over the step list the run executed (ids read before the deletes; each step's count form `PurgeStep.Count`, the same predicate as `SELECT count(*)`, in one read-only transaction bounded by the run's context and budget — expiry is a pause; rows left → `failed` at once with `error = "left: <table>=<n>, …"`, a revoked or removed account held by `purge.VerifyHeld` in `Apply` and, with the purge on, `refreshPurgeHold`; a failed count → a failed chunk), deletes the token files outside any transaction unless access returned during the run (`PurgeAccessReturned`), then `FinishAccountPurge` requires a `running` row and marks it `done` and the account `purged` (`resetAccountSQL` + `purgedAccountSQL`) in one transaction — or keeps the account when access returned. Every CLI login and re-auth path refuses to write credentials for an account whose purge is `running` (`refuseLoginDuringPurge` over `db.AccountPurgeRunning`), so a login cannot land between that check and the token delete. A stream digest or Confluence space row is deleted only while none of its knowledge rows is left, in the same statement (its count form counts every such parent; a digest's `kb_dirty` marks go before its documents), so a purge resumed after that step never loses the ids its count needs. KB documents are deleted directly (the `messages` `kb_dirty` trigger fires on UPDATE only). Every table with an account column is covered by a step of the source that owns it (the foreign key's account table, else `accountTableSources`; Slack's namespace-keyed tables in `slackNamespacedTables`) or named in `purgeKeptTables` (`imap_messages`, `account_purges`, `jira_user_map`, `linear_user_map`). The sync phases and the reaction poll skip accounts in `purgeHold` (purged, or a purge running; refreshed before the startup calendar sync too), so a syncer wired at startup never refills one.

*Extension (2026-10-25, images and links become knowledge, asks #576/#590/#594):* a purge also deletes the account's image descriptions and link summaries (`media_items`, migration 00156): the Slack, Google and Confluence step lists delete them first (`media items` by `slack_channel_id` range, `gmail_account_id`, and `ext_source_id` of the Jira account's spaces), chunked and counted like every step, before the raw rows whose foreign-key cascade would otherwise delete them uncounted and outside the chunk limit; their `kb_dirty` marks go with the knowledge steps. IMAP media rows stay with `imap_messages` (kept, as before); chat and target items are the owner's uploads and have no account. `media_items` has no account column, so the coverage guard reads it through its parent columns.

**Why locked:** One big delete transaction would block every hook and the Desktop writer for its whole length (hooks give up after 500 ms / 1 s); a non-resumable purge would leave an account half-deleted after a restart; a new account-scoped table missing from the steps would silently survive every purge; deleting cross-account or AI-written rows would destroy the owner's own work.
